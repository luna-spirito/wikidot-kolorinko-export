[[div style="text-align: center; color: #00008b;"]]
[[size 225%]]
+ __**Analysis Division**__
[[/size]]
[[/div]]
The Analysis Division is the group directly responsible for all research, analysis, and studying related to all Documented Anomalies (DA) in the possession of the FASA. This Division is composed almost entirely of scientists and researchers of various specializations. They draft the plans for containment, utilization, and destruction of all DAs that come to the FASA. It is their responsibility to conduct experimentation, study, and research on anomalous objects, as well as to produce anomalous objects when the opportunity to safely do so is available.
[[div style="text-align: center;  color: #356f91;"]]
[[size 225%]]
**Ranking System**
[[/size]]
[[/div]]
++ Prospective Scientist (Tier 0)

Prospective Scientists are people found within the United States of America (or allied countries) who have shown the mental capacity and intelligence to work with the FASA. They are to be treated as non-personnel until the end of their conventional education. At this point, they are to be confronted with an invitation to the FASA. If they refuse, they will be treated with high tier amnestics, and removed from the FASA Personnel Database. If they accept, they will be promoted to Tier 1.

++ Initiate Scientists (Tier 1)

People who accept the call of the FASA should be brought to a local FASA Education Center, where they will spend the next five years of their life receiving special education from dedicated Tier 4 Senior Researchers, who will teach them everything they need to know about Documented Anomalies and FASA Equipment. Upon the completion of their five year course, they are to be assigned a Site and be sent there via cheapest means available per area.

++ Associate Scientist (Tier 2)

Associate Scientists is a term that refers to all recently graduating scientists who have recently been assigned an FASA Site. They are to be taught what they'll need to know for their particular site based on it's special conditions. They must work as an associate scientist for eight years before being promoted to Standard Researcher. All scientists from this point forward (Tier 2+) are permitted to formally request a Site Change to Administration[[footnote]] Reasons for this Site Change include but are not limited to: Closeness to family and home, lack of expertise in a special site requirement, or the desire to be placed on American soil. These requests are to be accepted at the discretion of the Administration Division. [[/footnote]]

++ Standard Researcher (Tier 3)

This rank is given to all scientists that are active and have worked on-site at any site for at least 8 years. These researchers are the standard researchers of the FASA, and will most likely stay at this rank. Scientists who have shown to excel in the anomalous field will be promoted to Senior Researcher, at the discretion of The Research Head (Tier 5).

++ Senior Researcher Rank (Tier 4)

Senior Researchers are veteran researchers that have proven tremendous worth to the FASA. All Senior Researchers must meet at the FASA HQ (Area-050) for a meeting where all the Director Class Personnel will be present. This meeting will discuss the progress of the Analysis Division and determine the yearly milestones and goals of the Division. These researchers are to hold authority over researchers in their field.

++ The Lead Researchers (Tier 5)

The Lead Researchers are researchers that have proved their capabilities to the Agency and have been elevated to the highest possible rank of the Agency's scientific division. There are only 100 Lead Researchers in the entirety of the FASA Organization. In addition to being the most respected and highly prioritized scientists in the Agency, they also compose the ASRC (Anomalous Sciences Review Counsel). Every two months, the ASRC meet to discuss various protocols and procedures surrounding various anomalies. The ASRC determines the AUS (ASCR Usability Score) of an anomaly, and ultimately determines whether they should be used by the Agency, destroyed by the Agency, or contained indefinitely by the Agency.

+ __**Administration Division**__
[[/size]]
[[/div]]
The Administration Division is designated for the management of the other two divisions, as well as other staff of the FASA that don't fall into the other groups (such as Inmates, Janitors, etc.) This group consists of the heads of all the Sub-Divisions of the FASA. They are also assigned with the approval, drafting, and delegation of missions and expeditions to the Enforcement Division. They do the hiring, firing, terminating, and diplomacy for the FASA. Due to the extensive size of the FASA, only important roles will be listed here.

[[div style="text-align: center;  color: #356f91;"]]
[[size 225%]]
**Ranking System**
[[/size]]
[[/div]]

++ Important Administrative Ranks

Due to the extensive list of ranks in the FASA, a much smaller, summarized list has been created that features the more important ranks among Administration. The list consists as follows:

+++ Team Lead

Team Leads direct goals within small groups of staff that aren't specifically lead by any Division. Examples of this would be the Electronic System Maintenance Team, or the Head of the Janitorial Staff.

+++ Department Heads

Department Heads are personnel who are placed in charge of a division related to non-anomalous issues, such as Inmate Intake, Mission Assignment, Economics, etc. They're in charge of setting the goals within that division, approving protocols, and coordinating with the other divisions.

+++ Site Managers / Area Managers

The Site Manager is the boss of an FASA Site. They assign incoming DAs and Inmates to specific divisions and teams within their site, and maintain close contact with the other administration in their site to ensure safety, research, and order within their site.

Area Managers have a much different task, due to the nature of Areas as opposed to Sites. Since Areas do not host DAs or any anomalies, Area Managers have to work closely with Site Managers to ensure information is stored properly, as well as to maintain Agency infrastructure in a region or state. This is especially important in foreign areas, due to the lack of United States governmental protection in those areas.

+++ Regional Managers

The Regional Managers are in charge of an entire Agency region. Regional Managers coordinate large scale operations, approve site creations, select Area/Site Managers, and control the flow of FASA traffic through their area. As a result, Regional Managers are invited to the yearly meeting at the FASA (Area-050). All five of the Director Class personnel will be there to discuss the goals of the Administrative Division, and check for corruption and ensure their performance.

+++ Global Managers

The Global Managers are the top of the totem pole within the Agency. Only eight of them exist, one for each continent. They set Agency-wide goals and have a large amount of administrative power within the FASA. Additionally, Global Managers manage relationships with politicians and work closely with the highest of government officials. Global Managers have access to most of the FASA database and are only one step below the Head Global Manager.

+++ Head Global Director

The Head Global Director is the absolute top of the totem pole in the Agency. Not only do they have authority over the Global Directors, but they are also among the five Directors of the FASA. They are responsible for handling all international affairs and making the large administrative decisions with input from the Global Managers. They have unlimited access to the Agency database.

[[tab Enforcement]]

[[div style="text-align: center; color: #00008b;"]]
[[size 225%]]
+ __**Enforcement Division**__
[[/size]]
[[/div]]

The Enforcement Division is dedicated to handling all the violent persons, anomalies, and groups that stand in the way of the FASA. This group is similar to the U.S military, but is designated specifically and only for the Federal Agency for the Strange and Anomalous. They are trained in the art of Anomalous Combat and are prepared to fight with their lives for the American people.
[[div style="text-align: center;  color: #356f91;"]]
[[size 225%]]
**Ranking System**
[[/size]]
[[/div]]
++ Scouting Agents
Scouts are members of the FASA who work either as sleeper agents in other organizations or are members of the general public community who feed information to the Agency when anomalous events are occurring. A lot of domestic scouts work in police precincts, hospitals, national parks, and SWAT teams in order to provide live information about anomalous emergencies. Veterans of the U.S Army that have shown the skill required are to be invited to the Enforcement Branch of the FASA starting as a Scout. Should they decline, they will be treated with amnestics and allowed to continue their lives. If they show excellence in their line of work they may be offered a promotion into an AET unit.

++ AET Agents
AET Agents are members of the FASA who are highly skilled in armed combat against both people and anomalies and have chosen or been chosen to join an AET as a result. AETs, also known as Anomalous Engagement Teams, are Task Forces formed by the Enforcement Division's higher command to ensure that all anomalies can be safely secured and contained, regardless of how specific the requirements to do so may be. These teams range from very small (about 5 members) to incredibly large (in the tens of thousands). AETs are given their designation based upon a constellation that represents the overall objective of the task force, and are given a code name for members of personnel to refer to them by.

[[/size]]

All members of AET-Orion-3 are to be trained to control crowds and manage large groups of people while under pressure.

[[tab Testing]]
[[div style="text-align: center; color: #00008b;"]]
[[size 225%]]
+ __**Testing Protocols**__
[[/size]]
[[/div]]
[[div style="text-align: center; color: #356f91;"]]
[[size 225%]]
**Overview**
[[/size]]
[[/div]]
Due to the dangerous nature of anomalies, testing is paramount in gaining an understanding of their effects. Testing of anomalies is done using various incremental protocols and phases in order to minimize danger to personnel, as well as to retain morale among members of personnel and to reserve as many resources as possible.

++ Protocol Alpha: Collection Assessment

Collection Assessment is the process of analyzing and theorizing on data that was recorded during the collection or acquisition of an anomaly. This stage is considered to be the most important and must be completed before any formal testing can be conducted by the Agency.

++ Protocol Beta: Animal Testing

To protect human life and maximize overall morale, animal testing is used to gauge any potential biological hazards that are presented by an anomaly. The Agency uses mice, rabbits and pigs to gather research data regarding the way an anomaly interacts with living organisms. This stage may be skipped if an anomaly requires intelligent interaction to activate or does not impact living organisms.

++ Protocol Gamma: Drone Testing

The Agency makes use of various drones and robotic devices to conduct testing on anomalies that could be dangerous to get close to. Generally drones owned by the Agency are minimalistic, and are used to perform a various number of mundane tasks (take pictures, gather samples, transmit interviews, record video or audio, etc). However, in the event of more complicated anomalous properties, drones with various augments and modifications may be used in order to properly gather data regarding an anomaly.

Stage Gamma may be unnecessary if a particular anomaly does not pose a threat to living organisms to interact with. Anomalies that have the ability to interface with or interfere with technology should not undergo Stage Gamma, in order to prevent potential danger to Agency personnel.

++ Protocol Delta: Personnel Testing

Due to the nature of some anomalies and their interaction with intelligent organisms, it is sometimes necessary to use human test subjects when testing certain anomalies. In regards to anomalies that have easily predictable or avoidable anomalous effects, members of personnel may be specially trained to test with certain anomalies. In order to prevent injury or death, personnel who are responsible for the direct handling of anomalies are to be equipped with all necessary equipment and are to be completely debriefed on all potential dangers that an anomaly poses. 

++ Protocol Epsilon: Inmate Testing

Some anomalies have an unavoidable margin of potential death or permanent mutilation that it may cause upon living test subjects. As a result, prisoners of the United States prison system who have committed heinous crimes may be used in the place of trained personnel. All inmates being used as test subjects should be properly informed on a need-to-know basis and are to be given equipment required to maximize their potential survival.

[[tab Security Levels]]
[[div style="text-align: center; color: #00008b;"]]
[[size 225%]]
+ __**Security Clearance Levels**__
[[/size]]
[[/div]]
[[div style="text-align: center;  color: #356f91;"]]
[[size 225%]]
**Overview**
[[/size]]
[[/div]]
Security Levels exist to highlight the amount of classified or otherwise undisclosed information that a member of personnel is permitted to know, as well as how closely they are allowed to interact with certain anomalies. The Agency has five security clearance levels. These levels are blue, green, gold, red, and crimson. These levels are assigned on a "need to know" basis, meaning that levels are designated based upon the most classified information a member of personnel needs to know to perform their job correctly. As a result, security levels are usually given on a by-anomaly basis, with exceptions being given to scientists that do a large variety of work in numerous anomalies and require a generalized clearance level. The format for these security levels is represented by the relevant anomaly index being added to the end of their security clearance designation. Members of personnel who do not have a security level, such as Inmate-Class personnel, are considered to be fluid. These members of personnel will be amnesticized at the end of their service to the Agency.
@@@@
[[div style="text-align: center;  color: #356f91;"]]
[[size 225%]]
**Security Levels**
[[/size]]
[[/div]]
++ **Blue** (Level I)
The Blue security level is relegated to working-level Inmate-class personnel, who have agreed to work for the Agency indefinitely, whether it be for testing or as members of janitorial staff. They are to have as minimal knowledge as required for them to perform essential tasks, as determined by FASA personnel. They are not permitted to leave their work areas, but are entitled to rights and minor luxuries.
++ **Green** (Level II)
The Green security level is designated to members of staff that are to work close to anomalies, but are not to come into direct contact with them. These members of personnel generally work in the logistical or administrative branch of the Agency. Having a Green-level security clearance to a specific anomaly gives a member of personnel clearance to move near or in the containment area of a given anomaly, but they are not authorized to interact with it directly without supervision or directions.
@@@@
++ **Yellow** (Level III)
The Yellow security clearance is generally given to security and research personnel that require direct access to detailed data regarding anomalies within containment. Level 3 personnel are mostly security staff, research staff, field agents, and containment specialists. Having a Yellow-level security clearance for a specific anomaly gives a member of personnel access to knowledge regarding the nature of and current procedures surrounding an anomaly. More dangerous knowledge regarding the anomaly may be classified from the document for security purposes.
++ **Red** (Level IV)
The Red security level are assigned to senior administrative officials that require access to operational data of Regional and Site operations, along with strategic intelligence regarding Authority operations and research projects. Personnel with Level 4 clearance are generally assigned to high-level leadership positions such as Regional Directors, Site Directors, Departmental Heads, or Anomalous Engagement Team Commanders. Having Red-level security clearance to a particular anomaly allows access to almost all knowledge currently documented by the Agency. Information may be classified or expunged from the document at the direct orders of one or more directors.
++ **Maroon** (Level V)
Maroon security clearance designates the highest-ranking administrative personnel and grants effectively unlimited access to all Authority data and restricted areas. Personnel with Level 5 clearance are exclusively Global Directors or other global-level operations and administration staff. Maroon security clearance cannot be given on an anomaly to anomaly basis.
