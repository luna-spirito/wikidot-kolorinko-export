**Item #:** SCP-@

**Object Class:** Euclid

**Special Containment Procedures:** Most SCP-@-X instances are contained //in situ// according to THROW AWAY THE KEY doctrine on inanimate Safe-class objects. Relevant keywords have been inserted into the redlists of Foundation webcrawlers, with recovery and detainment under purview of the nearest Site's field personnel.

As the largest individual segment of SCP-@ and the one capable of communication with Foundation personnel, SCP-@-1 is instead contained in a standard humanoid containment facility, established at its location in the former Superstar Gym in Bandung. In order to minimise the biohazard disposal requirements of SCP-@-15's containment locker, SCP-@ has been placed on an IV feeding system, with typical 12-hour rotations for attending medical personnel. 

Following a ruling by the Ethics Committee on 2019-03-12, Maslow-3 and above requirements for regular engagement with sentient anomalies have been waived indefinitely.

**Description:** SCP-@ is a 46-year old Indonesian male whose anatomy has been anomalously distributed across multiple locations worldwide. The discrete sections of its body (designated SCP-@-1 through -59 at the time of writing) exist separated from each other without any apparent effect on the biological function of SCP-@ as a whole.

When questioned, SCP-@ actively denies its current condition, claiming to have 'just fallen over'; in general, the subject is unresponsive to outside stimuli and behaves in a confused, delirious manner consistent with severe concussion. Conventional brain imaging is largely impossible due to the numerous cross-sections taken through the upper half of its skull, but visual inspection indicates the subject suffers from a condition with surface similarity to chronic traumatic encephalopathy. 

All SCP-@-X instances appear to protrude from a fixed area such as the wall or floor of the surrounding building, and are effectively locked in place against the interfacing surface. Attempting to move or otherwise damage these anchor points causes SCP-@ great distress and severely impacts its health for poorly-understood reasons, resulting in symptoms such as arrythmia, cerebrospinal fluid leakage, and massive involuntary haemolacria. 

Despite much of its internals being exposed to the open air, SCP-@ is immune to conditions that would normally occur as a result. As an example, SCP-@ has never contracted pyomyositis[[footnote]]A bacterial infection of muscle tissue.[[/footnote]] despite much of its //trapezius// and //latissimus dorsi// being exposed to contaminated floodwater. This does not appear to be the result of spatial anomalies at the bounds of SCP-@-X instances; medical staff have successfully inserted fiber-optic endoscopes directly into the exposed cross-section of its femoral artery without finding a discontinuity along the cable's length at any point.

**Discovery:** SCP-@'s anomalous properties initially manifested on 2016-10-28. CCTV recordings recovered from the Superstar Gym's security system show SCP-@ tripping over another patron's bag while entering the locker room. After the subject's head makes contact with the corner of a locker, the video cuts to 29 still images of SCP-@'s internal anatomy, such as the inner wall of SCP-@'s cranial cavity, gastrointestinal tract and the nerves of its right ring finger. All images depicted would later be found to correspond with recovered SCP-@-X instances. 

Following the interruption, the feed resumes depicting the object now designated SCP-@-1 protruding from the locker. Shortly thereafter, SCP-@ was reported to authorities by bystanders and taken into custody by embedded Foundation personnel. 

**Addendum:** While originally believed that further instances of SCP-@-X are simply recovered after its initial manifestation and not actively being generated, the first recorded instance of a new SCP-@-X instance being created occured on 2020-08-21. Beginning at 7:24am, SCP-@ repeatedly complained of 'wet lung' and requested medical treatment from supervising personnel. Initial examination indicated nothing out of the ordinary, but after complaints continued, the supervising medical officer was authorised to conduct a bronchoscopy on the patient. 

Despite the administration of antitussives and sedatives, the subject began violently coughing during the procedure, and the elevated intrathoracic pressure resulted in pneumothorax from the camera puncturing the inside of SCP-@'s lung. When it was discovered that the bronchoscope was showing an image of what appeared to be an apartment's interior, the procedure was aborted and agents placed on standby to contain potential new instances of SCP-@-X. Two such instances were discovered within the following week: SCP-@-58, a mass of alveoli from SCP-@'s left lung evidencing the entry wound of the bronchoscope, and SCP-@-59. 

Unlike other instances of SCP-@-X, SCP-@-59 is not strictly a segment of SCP-@'s physical anatomy, but rather a cylindrical volume of space within its airway. Oxygen, carbon dioxide, and saliva droplets have been demonstrated to travel through SCP-@-59 instead of the corresponding space within SCP-@-1. 

A ban on conducting further surgical procedures on SCP-@ to prevent similar incidents occuring are pending Ethics Committee approval.
