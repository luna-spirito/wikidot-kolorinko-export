**Registered Phenomena Code:** 326

**Object Class:** Alpha-Yellow

**Hazard Types:** Transmutation Hazard, Toxic Hazard

**Containment Protocols:** RPC-326 is to be stored in a medium-security wall safe at the Site-016 when not in use.  During training demonstrations, one member of on-site security is required to be present.  Copper and human blood are not permitted to be used during research or training under any circumstances.  Any individual requesting to handle RPC-326 is to be searched for samples of copper or human blood before permission may be given.  Individuals with lacerations or bleeding on an easily accessible part of the body such as the hands or face are not permitted to handle RPC-326.  

**Description:** RPC-326 is the designation given to an anomalous mechanical pencil.  Notably, RPC-326 is capable of transmutation of materials placed into the pencil as a substitute for graphite.  The materials RPC-326 transmutes are limited, and in all cases but one are harmless.  In light of the relative lack of danger RPC-326 presents, it has become an integral part of the Authority's training program.  Trainees that have enrolled in the bimonthly //Introductory Seminar on Anomalous Objects// hosted at Site-016 are given the opportunity to observe demonstrations utilizing RPC-326.  At the end of the seminar these trainees are invited to conduct informal tests while under supervision by research and security personnel.   

**Discovery:** RPC-326 was recovered following reports from a small newspaper of a "magic pencil" that turned graphite to ink.  The newspaper article mentioned that the pencil came from a local general store.  Interviews of the employees yielded that the pencil had been stored for an unknown period of time in the back room as it was not the same type as the ones currently for sale, and that none of the employees knew where it came from.  Class A-1 amnestics were administered to the local populous, and all newspapers regarding the incident were seized.

**Incident Report:**
 
[[collapsible show="SHOW VIDEO TRANSCRIPT" hide="HIDE VIDEO TRANSCRIPT" hideLocation="both"]] 

On 17/9/2004 one trainee was sent to the medical center after suffering minor respiratory damage.  The following is a transcription of events made using several video cameras in and outside of the room the incident occurred in.  

> 14:37:38 -  RPC-326 is handed to a group of trainees along with a large number of materials.  Trainees begin conducting informal tests.  
> 
> 14:59:16 - Supervisor exits the room, and heads down the hall to the break room.  
> 
> 15:00:37 - Trainees finish testing with provided testing supplies.  
> 
> 15:01:46 - One trainee retrieves a copper wire from a nearby maintenance cart.  Copper is noted to have not been tested (formally or informally) before this point in time.  
> 
> 15:02:15 - Trainees succeed in using RPC-326 to transmute copper to blood.  
> 
> 15:03:07 - Trainees use eyedropper to introduce blood into RPC-326.  
> 
> 15:03:15 - Trainees succeed in using RPC-326 to transmute blood to chlorine gas.  
> 
> 15:03:27 - Supervisor returns to the room holding a cup of coffee but does not approach the group of trainees.  
> 
> 15:03:43 - Trainees appear visibly confused as to the nature of the gas rapidly being emitted by RPC-326.  
> 
> 15:03:50 - One trainee inhales strongly next to RPC-326.  (Noted to be improper protocol for smelling a chemical in a laboratory environment.)
> 
> 15:03:57 - The trainee that inhaled the chlorine announces that it smells like a pool and begins to loudly cough.  
> 
> 15:04:00 - Supervisor drops the cup of coffee and rushes toward the group of trainees.  A small but visible cloud of chlorine gas is now present in the room.  
> 
> 15:04:04 - The supervisor grabs RPC-326, removes the cap, and begins to shake the blood out.  Six of the trainees scatter away from the growing cloud.  Two trainees remain in the cloud, one helping the other stand back up after tripping.  
> 
> 15:04:10 - One trainee leads the other out of the cloud, the second trainee is visibly clutching his chest and is red in the face.  
> 
> 15:04:24 - The supervisor gathers all of the trainees on east side of the room.  The cloud is now larger but appears much lighter in color.  RPC-326 has stopped emitting chlorine gas.  
> 
> 15:04:34 - The supervisor seals RPC-326 in a plastic bag, and the group then exits the room.   
> 
> 15:04:54 - The gas cloud has fully dissipated in the room, no visible yellow or green hue is present.  Airtight doors  and automatically sealing air vents are successful in preventing further contamination 
> 
> 15:10:11 - The gas cloud is now visible again, but is only present near to the ground and has a light coloration.  
> 
> 15:23:47 - The Hazardous Chemical Response Team arrives.  
> 

Following medical examination only one person was found to need treatment.  After a short rehabilitation period ████████████ made a full recovery.  Treatment was offered to all other personnel present but was refused. 
 
An investigation was headed by Researcher O'Brian regarding the incident.  The investigation found that the large volume of the room and small amount of chlorine gas created with the limited sample of transmuted blood meant that the incident would not have been particularly dangerous even if Researcher █████ was not present to remove the remaining blood present in RPC-326.  O'Brian's investigation concluded that while the incident only caused injury due to improper laboratory procedure, the protocols regarding informal testing of RPC-326 set by the Site Management were inadequate and could lead to future incidents.  

As a result of the investigation's findings, two supervisors familiar with the anomalous nature of RPC-326 are now required to be present during informal testing.  Additionally, copper has been designated as a prohibited material for informal testing.  Lastly, trainees are to be specifically told not to test with copper or blood during informal tests, and are to only test with provided materials.
