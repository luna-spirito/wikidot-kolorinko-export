= [[span class="Merriweather"]] __**ON THE HUNTING AND CONCEALMENT OF THE ANOMALY**__[[/span]]


> [[span class="Playfair"]]All able-bodied and well-armed “second-hand law enforcers” in the Protectorate, the Concealment offices, and even the Axton-Hornsby Exploration Society—from the Star Gazers to the Jawlock Club—are encouraged to take on the WANTED bounty attached alongside this report. And while a hefty pay from the county’s pocket would be lovely for some of our more... demanding “residents” beneath the saloon, making sure the Black Water Gang ain’t gonna cause more trouble is our first priority.[[/span]]
> 
> [[span class="Playfair"]]But don’t try to play the hero taking the whole crew by yourself. We’ve gotten to many good men in the bone orchards already.[[/span]]
> [[>]]
> [[span class="Playfair"]]- Officer Lester A. Cohen[[/span]]
> [[/>]]

The people of Cochise County, including Tombstone’s very own sheriff, are blissfully ignorant of the Black Lake Gang’s true nature as anomalous creatures, believing them instead to be a human, although fairly odd and certainly violent, group of outlaws terrorizing the region. Whenever possible, our men and communicants across the county are to make sounds of these rumours to prevent unnecessary complications in our already-messy state of affairs. It’ll also result in far less folk trying to “hunt the beasts” by themselves.

Should you see yourself suitable to take on the gang, hopefully with a team following you close by, there are a few things one ought to take note of:
* Dave and his crew are tough, a term which also applies to their physical-selves. Normal gunfire won’t do them any harm, either bouncing off of hard scales or plated iron;
* Outrunning the gunners in horseback is unfeasible, as is making them plummet from their steeds, for they have naught. You are to ensnare them by surprise, and keep them grounded, else they’ll sail away;
* Bring salt with you, and hurl it forth to the beasts __only__ as a last resort. For reasons unbeknownst to the author of this document, they appear to harbour fear towards it. Although, in some cases, it has also been shown to enrage them. And;
* In the case of being cornered - or otherwise put in a non-desirable position by the gang - it is advised to wait until nightfall. The gang has only ever been seen attacking in broad daylight, running away as soon as the dark starts to settle.

Dolefully, these bits of guidance are the best we are able to provide. Most of the men who’ve gotten close enough to catch them have never come back, and those that ran away know only enough not to die. Lavoe, our head-hunter for these things, recommends bringing harpoon rifles and dynamite instead of revolvers. Fishing nets are another useful tool by his accounts, as are crucifixes and other religious periapts; by his accounts, the gang appears to avoid contact with them. All of the previously listed items will **__not__** be freely provided without Cohen or Lavoe’s permission.

Once caught, and if alive, the gang is to be brought in secrecy to the Bucket of Blood Saloon while Dave’s helmet may be given to Officer Morgan as a token of a successful bounty. Chained and placed near a running salamander, the gang may be questioned by senior officers of the Protectorate. Aliment may be provided, but only within a reasonable, and reduced, budget.

> [[span class="Castoro"]] Wretched creatures they be, I tell ya. I’ve lost more years and grown more grey hairs trying to catch em’ than I would’ve if I’d gotten married. Facing them ain’t no child’s game, and the fools that’ve gone out to get them alone don’t live to tell it. While I’m, funnily enough, at sea about these monsters, you’ll see me bald before I give up this dream of being a dry-land fisherman.[[/span]]
> [[>]]
> [[span class="Castoro"]]- Levi Lavoe[[/span]]
> [[/>]]

The Black Lake Gang, known in record as “ANOMALIE OBSCURA No.729”, is a five-man group of outlaws led by the dangerous gunner “Deep Dave.” This group has become widely-known across Cochise County. Be it theft, murder, or a wide number of petty crimes, they are ruthless criminals with the only objective of causing mayhem in whichever community they happen to fall upon. As for their paranatural qualities, the gang presents the following:

# Despite their outward appearance, and usage of heavy clothing and hats, none of its members (with a plausible exemption for Dave himself) are human. Recounts by our men and civilians depict them as a bipedal abominations, resembling an unholy union between men and marine critters;
# Lacking horses that could hold their weight, the gang rides on motorized and expertly crafted land-sails of varying proportions, and;
# Possessing a similar complication with guns, most of its members have been seen firing at law enforcers with squids in hand. While a hilarious thought at first, most casualties related to their attacks have been men with ink-tainted holes through their skulls.

Excluding the titular leader of the gang, its other members include “Calamari Clark”, the gang’s main gunner; “Violin Vince”, the gang’s brute; “Shrimp Shram”, titular bank-vault cleaner; and finally, “Eelan”, Dave’s slithery right hand and second-best gunner.

> [[span class="Castoro"]] Author Note: Only Dave’s "nickname" has been confirmed, with the rest being the invention of Lavoe, hereby added at his insistent request. [[/span]]

++++ __The Tombstone’s Midnight Bank Robbery__
 [[span class="Merriweather"]] **~ 11th of March, 1881 ~** [[/span]]
[[/=]]
-----
__**Author Note:**__ Performed following the robbery of Mr. Finch’s bank, and at the request of Officer Morgan Seth Earp, Officer Cohen and I were sent to the bank to evaluate the situation for possible clues into the capture and/or hunting of the gang.
-----

**Qu.** Best of evenings, Mr. Finch! My condolences for the bag of nails you’ve been through, but I’ve been informed that the state will cover most of the losses. Now, to what brought me here. Officer Morgan wanted me to... inquire you on the nature of the assailants. So, how about you run me through what went on?

**Ans.**  If that bally namby-pamby wants to get anything from me, he better come himself! I don’t pay my goddamn taxes just so he and his good-for-nothing brother can laze-off all day in the Sheriff’s Office. No, sir. I want to speak with Deputy Virgil, and I want to talk with him now! Especially after those… those monsters robbed me of my livelihood!

**Qu.** I’ll see what I can do once we’re done with this ordeal, Mr. Finch. Look, you help me here, and I’ll help you out. All the time you want with Virgil and his brothers. But you must help me //help you//, OK? Now, again, how about you run me through what went on?

**Ans.** Fine, but I’ll have you know my lawyers will hear of this discourtesy. It happened around 6 o’clock. I was just opening up for the day, streets were empty aside from your usual suspects.

**Qu.** Larry and the guys?

**Ans.** Yes, in fact. In the streets, and roostered out of their minds as usual. Must’ve been drinking the whole night prior. Such a shame there wasn’t any lawman around to take care of them…

**Qu.** I get your point loud and clear, Mr. Finch. We'll put some guys back in the office to take care of it. Now, continue.

**Ans.** You better! Now, where was I… Yes, 6 o’clock. I was simply putting everything in order when I heard some commotion on the outside. Thought it was Larry getting the mitten by one of the bar’s ladies or what have you. But that’s when I heard people screaming.

**Qu.** I hear you, I hear you. I’ll take Larry’s account of the events later too, if I can. Now, what happened next? After the screaming, I mean.

**Ans.** Well, I didn’t think much of it at the time, so I just went about as usual. And then I heard shots being fired. My first action was closing down the bank, in case any go-lucky wanted to take their chances during the commotion. And I actually did manage to close the locks down. But… it didn’t matter much.

**Qu.** How so? They went through the back door?

**Ans.** Ha! If only. No, they went through the front door nice and good. I guess you can already tell that, now can’t you? They busted down the fucking door out of its bolts! Do you have ANY idea of how much these things cost? I can’t just go anywhere to buy them. I had this one custom-made in the North to stand dynamite, and they kicked it down like some crooked saloon batwing door!

**Qu.** Did they use explosives? Cut it down, somehow?

**Ans.** I wish. I had my gun in hand ready for anything when I heard… “something” punch the door. Then again, and again. I was already getting nervous, as you can imagine! But I trusted that it wouldn’t budge. How stupid of me.

**Qu.** And then what happened?

**Ans.** Some giant sent it flying out of its bolt, that’s what happened! Looked like he came out of a messed-up Irish fairy tale. 

**Qu.** Giant? What do you mean by “giant”?

**Ans** Have you ever seen someone hunch over to enter through a two-meter hole in the wall? Huh?! That beast of a man had a cannon for an arm, and was covered in so many coats that it might as well have been winter up in York!

**Qu.** Anything else about his figure? Something to help us track him down?

**Ans.** Tough luck, pal. I couldn’t see anything under that wide hat and scarf of his. But I don’t think that’s necessary, unless Seth is so lazy as to miss a giant walking on the desert. Eugh, forget it. The next thing I can recall was four others... men rushing past him.  

**Qu.** Why the hesitation?

**Ans.** Look, I’m not informed about gangs around here unless it's the Cochise County Cowboys. But those gunners looked like something out of a freak show, I tell you.

**Qu.** Four weird-looking men, huh? Mind giving me a rundown of that, Mr. Finch?

**Ans.** Well, I already told you about the giant with a cannon arm. And not only did they tear down my front wall, but the bastard crooked all my bars and the vault lock. Then, there was a slim, tall man who screamed at me to get on the ground, followed behind by a small scoundrel-looking, clock-bearing fiend who rushed up to where the cash was with two bags on hand. I tried to shoot at him, but the giant slapped the rifle out of my hands and threw me on the floor!

**Qu.** Did they try to hurt you? Ask you anything?

**Ans.** The giant talked like a simpleton, and told me to “Stay. Quiet”. As he held me down, I saw… another tall man walking backwards into the bank, holding someone under gunfire. I… I don’t know how, but I saw him shooting with eight pistols at once, all coming from different parts of his attire. Now how the hell is that even possible, eh? And that wasn’t even the worst part. No. That goes to the utterly rancid rotten-fish aroma that came from each and every one of them.

**Qu.** I’m... sure it was simply the shock from being thrown into the ground. You know, seeing multiple things.

**Ans.** Are you taking me by a lunatic, Cohen? I know damn well what I saw, and those things weren’t people! And to top it all off, there came a goddamned diver!

**Qu.** A diver?

**Ans.** A diver, yes! Like the ones that come from the Empire, and into the coast to study sea-life. He was holding a... squid? Yeah, a squid on his hand. And he began threatening me. Asking where the rest of the money was. He must’ve been from the deep south, I assume. From his accent, that is.

**Qu.** How so?

**Ans.** Well, he called me a “Yellow Belly”, whatever the hell that means, and began ordering “Open yer mouth and tell me where the Queen’s Gem be.” All while slapping me across the face with a fucking squid. I told him I had no idea what a “Queen’s Gem” was, and spat on his helmet for good measure.

**Qu.** And then?

**Ans.** And then?! And then he punched me flat on the jaw with his metal gauntlet. I had already began making my silent prayers, because I was sure I was good as gone. Then... I heard it. Some sixth man, somewhere where I couldn't see him, began talking.

**Qu.** A sixth man?

**Ans.** Yep, sure as day. Not like I could forget about his voice even if I wanted to. It was a grainy, deep, dark voice. It was far, but I could still hear it close enough: “You know it isn’t here, but I appreciate the attempt. Showtime's over.”

**Qu.** “Showtime’s over”?

**Ans.** Does it look like I know what every modism in this God-forsaken place is? But… whatever it meant, it didn’t sound good. As the grip on that brute grew stronger I thought my fate was sealed. Then, much like the sixth man, I heard someone shout “Yer not getting away, scoundrels!” at the seven winds. Of course, it had to be none other than that fucking-

**Qu.** That fucking what, Finch?

**Ans.** ...kid. That damned kid from the Bucket of Blood Saloon. You work with him, I presume? Anyways, the attention of all the scoundrels turned towards Lafoe, or whatever his name is. He was covered head-to-toe in crosses, held a Whaler’s harpoon in both hands, and had bags of salt hanging off from his belt. I don’t know what the purpose of it was, but I can’t say it wasn’t… useful. He began shooting left and right while taking cover behind a piece of door that had been sent flying a while back. He first took care of the long-thing with many arms. Threw a salt bag right into his coat, and the monster let out an abysmal scream from the top of its lungs. The next one to follow was the brute, who had already loosened his grip on my back, and instead went on charging to the kid. 

**Qu.** And what did you do?

**Ans.** Me? Simple, I went and hid under my desk. I looked over to see the brute had somehow gotten his arm stuck into the wall, while the other gunners were scramming around to take cover. I have to give it to him, he gave them a good fight until he, as expected, got shot in the shoulder by the other, tall-thing. I thought we were both going to meet out end there, but instead, they all went running out - bags of money still on their backs, to my much demise. 

**Qu.** And what about Lavoe?

**Ans.** That's his name? Well, I helped him on his feet until the officers, and you, arrived. Not much more I can say, at least none that I wouldn’t want Virgil to hear himself. Although, the sixth man was nowhere to be seen, and Lavoe looked at me like I was crazy when I pondered over it...

**Qu.** Alright, Mr. Finch. Thanks for the help. I think this concludes our small conversation.

**Ans.** ‘Thanks for the help’? It should be you, you lazy barrels of whiskey, that help ME out! My money is all but gone, and I didn’t hear or see a single officer outside the bank while it all went down. I’ll make you people pay each and every single penny that was stolen here today, you hear me?! And I swear to the Almighty, if I find that Everson had anything to do with this, you won't stop **me** from blowing up **his** bank!

**Qu.** I’ll have a deputy come here at noon to help you out, but for now I’ll head back to study the evidence. I also need to talk to Lavoe about this and-

**Ans** Oh no, no. You aren’t going anywhere. And YOU, don’t you dare stop writing. I want Virgil to hear each and every-

++++ __Old Willy’s Ranch Assault__
 [[span class="Merriweather"]] **~ 5th of April, 1881 ~** [[/span]]
[[/=]]
-----
__**Author Note:**__ Following a call to the Sheriff’s Office, one telling about an assault perpetrated by monsters, Cohen and I were informed from our offices in the Bucket of Blood saloon and rushed to check the aftermath. While many lawmen were already on the scene, they had yet to speak to Old Willy and his wife. Take note, however, that Mr. Lavoe decided to accompany us this time around.
-----

**Qu.** Willy, Sarah! Has everything been taken care of here? You aren’t hurt, don’t need anything?

**Ans.**  Ney, son, we’re fine as dandy. Me and me wife weren’t touched a hair, thank the Lordy. But t’was horrible, I tell ye! Never been so spooked in me entire life. Thought the Devil himself was at me doorstep.

**Qu.** The Devil, you say? Mind running through me what occurred? I need to get this information to Officer Morgan and-

> **Lavoe.** The Devil... now ain’t that so. Saw any claws? Any stingers? Any salty water running out at them’ feet? The sounds of boating on dirt?
> 
> **Willy.** I-I, what are ye talking about, son?
> 
> **Cohen.** Lavoe, let me run the questions.
> 
> **Lavoe.** But-
> 
> **Cohen.** YOU should keep your lips shut, //you’re talking far too much about details that need no spreading.//
> 
> **Willy.** What did ya say back there, son?

**Qu.** Oh, it was nothing! Forgive my “partner”, Willy. Now, mind following through with my questions?

**Ans.** Aye. Now, I were sleeping with me wife a couple hours far, ye see. But then, I heard someone, or… something out back. I stood up and grabbed me shotgun, of course - there been many scoundrels round’ these parts as of late!

**Qu.** I see, and what did you see outside?

> **Lavoe.** Yeah ol’ man. What ya see? A boat? Were there any beasts o-
> 
> **Cohen.** Interrupt me once more and Thompson will have your head.
> 
> **Lavoe.** I, uh…

**Qu:** Please, Willy. Were you saying?

**Ans.** Ye, ye. So, I looked out me door, and saw a many men round’ the stables, looking at me cattle like inspecting game horses.

**Qu.** Came to rob them, you assume?

**Ans.** T’was me first thought, ye! I went out and fired once out in the sky. ‘Get lost’ I told em’. Thought it be enough to scare em’ off, but instead, they came right at me!

**Qu.** Did they break in?

**Ans.** Ye, ye! There were a big man with em’, must’ve been bigger than two of me bison stacked together’! I tried shootin’ him when he came close, but it did ‘im none! I tried running out to me bed to grab me wife, but they grabbed me by them arms and pulled me onto the wall.

**Qu.** Did they hurt you, or Sarah?

> **Lavoe.** Did the man have one arm bigger than the otha’?
> 
> **Cohen.** Lavoe, I told you-

**Ans.** Ye, indeed! He had them biggest arm I’ve ever saw! Couldn’t move not one inch beneath it, I tell ye. He whispered in me ears to move none. I tried to speak him back, but them lips were shut. 

> **Lavoe.** And who else was in the house? Anyone dressed differently?

**Ans.** Ye. There were a man dressed in, uh… metal? He had a suit all over himself, couldn’t even see them eyes.

> **Lavoe.** And did he speak to you?

**Ans.** Ye, he were the only that did. He told me he were called ‘Div Dave’ or somethin’. Asked were I ad’ hidden the Oax. Began shaking me up and down, slapping me on de’ face with some long fish!

> **Cohen.** That’s starting to sound like a common occurrence...
> 
> **Lavoe.** Who else was there? I need descriptions, ol' man!

**Ans.** Well, uh… there were the big man I told ye about. And then a small hunched feller, alongside two tall ones. One of dem’ were standing beside Dave, looking me like a piece of meat!

> **Lavoe:** Eelan, that scoundrel…

**Qu.** //Ehem//, Willy?

**Ans.** Ye?

**Qu.** What else did they do?

**Ans.** I can’t tell ye, cause' that's when me wife came from de' bedroom. Oh, me little Sarah, as lovely as ever, began throwing chairs at em!

**Qu.** How… nice. And did that work?

**Ans.** Methinks so, ye! Cause' when she threw them salt pitter at em', well let me tell ye, they ran out like scared pussy-cats, I tell ye! The tall one began cursing at the skyes. Seems like the feller had gone through this before.

> **Lavoe.** You wouldn’t know... And you! I told ya so! I told ya I wasn't babbling, salt works like a charm! I-
> 
> [[span class="Castoro"]]- I have been advised to remove the ensuing argument from the accounts. -[[/span]]
> 
> **Lavoe:** Sorry, uh, //ehem.// So, they left? Just like that? What can their master plan be…

**Ans.** I don’t know 'bout any plans, son! I’m just happy me and me wife weren’ hurt. Oh, Sarah, where would me head be without ye! Thank the Lord she made them all six leave.

> **Lavoe.** What? Six?

**Ans.** Six, ye. Five came in me house, but one stayed outside. Feller was riding a black steed, seemed lost in thought. 

> **Lavoe.** More details, Willy. What did he look like?!
> 
> **Cohen.** Calm down, Lavoe! Willy isn't a suspect.

**Qu.** S-Sorry, Willy. So... what was this “sixth” man doing?

**Ans.** I can’t really tell ye. I only saw him when they were all leavin’. He be in the middle of me farm, smiling, before turning ‘round and riding off. He looked like a banker, I can tell ye that much. Methinks he were with the others, ‘cause they all went “poof” together. 

**Qu.** Well... if that’s the case, I’ll leave you two to rest. Willy, Sarah. Thanks for the cooperation. I’ll make sure word of this reaches Officer Morgan.

**Ans.** No problem, son! Remember to pass by me house one day. Me wife makes the best pies, now ain’t she.

**Qu.** Yes, yes. Of course, Willy. I’ll make sure to come along soon.

> **Lavoe.** I don’t remember ever seeing no “banker” with the gang. What’s that all ‘bout? Mr. Finch also talked of a sixth person. Thought he was crazy!
> 
> **Cohen.** We’ll look into it. The last thing we need is more problems.

= [[span class="Merriweather"]] __**ON RELATED DOCUMENTS AND THINGS OF NOTE**__[[/span]]

On the 27th of September, just at daybreak, was found Lavoe, beaten, bloody and with a bag over his head, just outside of the Bucket of Blood Saloon. On him hung a sign strung with rope to his neck, one that read "Be Warned", in red lettering.

Being nursed back to health from near-death by his fellow Axton-Hornsby associates, Lavoe finally broke his silence, demanding to speak with me - the author of this document - in order to share information “vital” to the case of the Black Lake Gang. What follows is a transcript - also written by me - of his retelling.

[[collapsible show="Page No.3 “Nightfall Ambush” - Levi Lavoe" hide=" - "]]
[[div class="paper-bg-1"]]

> **Me.** Are you in condition to begin, Mr. Lavoe?
> 
> **Lavoe.** Sure are, smart-head. I ain’t afeared to tell me tale.
> 
> - Mr. Lavoe began to cough violently for a few seconds -
> 
> **Me.** Then please, do so.

 [[span class="drop-cap"]] I [[/span]]t be at eventide when I hopped onto Sally, methinks. Had heard from Mr. Virgil Earp that there been reports once more of the Gang round’ town. Had nothing better to do, and I didn’t wanna spend no more time with the anglomaniacs like Dr. Kingston. Thought a lil’ venturing would do me good. Mayhaps get some leads to their whereabouts after night-fall.

> **Me.** I take it that was not the end result.

You wouldn’t imagine, Clarky. See, I knew more o’ less where the Gang wanders about bout’ midnight. Though, this night be different, you see. There be a Barley Moon covering the whole sky, you see. Clear as day, I could see all five of em’ riding towards the desert in them land-boats of theirs. With sight’s cleared, I followed far behind on Sally, so they wouldn’t know I be tracking them tails. And far we went, I tell ye. Must’ve been hours of ridin’ from bush to bush, rock to rock, with no clear destination to be found! Thought I was gonna just end it right then and there; grab me iron and start blasting them now that I had the upper hand.

> **Me.** And did you? Is that why you came back bloody and beaten?

We ain’t there yet. But no, I didn’t. Kept my fresh and went onwards with the stalkin’. And that’s when I saw him. Riding a black horse, in the dead-middle of the desert. The sixth man. The elusive figure that keep slippin’ out me hands. The Pale Rider…

> **Me.** Is that another one of your “nicknames"?

Well, ye. But it’ll make sense, I promise!

So, I jumped down from Sally, took me bag o’ supplies, and hid in a nearby bush. Thinking back, I should’ve come closer - couldn’t hear a thing of what they were sayin’. Although, it’s not like it would’ve mattered, ‘cause I had only one thing in mind: revenge.

> **Me.** Revenge?

Ye, for all the times they’ve wronged our town, for them almost blasting me good arm away, and for much, much else, I tell ye. That rage alone could’ve lit the dynamite I had in me hand, but I opted for a lighter. And I held onto it. And kept holding... Kept holding 'till only the butt of the rope remained. And then I threw it, the LORD guiding it in an arc to their feet, and then-

> **Me.** And then?

Come on, Clarky. Let me be dramatic this once! //Ehem//. And then, it blew right on them feet! Big guy must’ve flew five or six feet in the air, I say! With me cover blown, I went back onto Sally and rode in to finish the deed.

> **Me.** Did you manage to take down any of them?

I wish, but darn-it, they tough! Had them with their backs on the sand, still. And with an iron in hand, I went forth to the vermin that almost killed me once.

“We finally met face to face, Eelan. Ya’ slippery scoundrel, thought ye could keep away from me, now didn't ya’?” I proclaimed victorious, standing over the fishy-fiend, his coat blown almost all off; relieving the watery, green, and silky skin beneath it.

“Who?” The scoundrel said, playing the fool, lookin’ ‘round himself. As if he didn’t know I be talking ‘bout him!

> **Me.** Mr. Lavoe, you are aware it is only you who calls them by those nicknames, correct?

Maybe... But I’ve called them that enough times I'd be damned if it didn’t stick to them! Anyhow, standing over Eelan, I scooped around the scene: Dave had gone to see the stars, barely managing to lay down. While Clark, Vince and Shram were knocked out of their lights for good. Seeing as enacting my well-deserved revenge on Eelan - whom I did kick in them ribs for good measure - would be wasted time, I began to walk towards their leader. My mortal rival. My deepest foe. My-

> **Me.** Mr. Lavoe, do mind I don’t exactly possess an endless stream of pages to write in.

Ah, forgive me. So, I strode up to him and pointed me iron to Dave’s head. He looked up to me, from behind his metal visage, with rage filling his eyes - which I could not see, yes, but felt in me soul.

"Levi-

> **Lavoe.** Wait, no, his voice be a bit deeper.
> 
> - Mr. Lavoe grabs a wet rag by the side of his bed and puts it over his mouth -

“​Levi "Cotton Head'' Lavoe. My mortal rival. My deepest foe. My hated adversary. You’ve caught me blue-handed, I give ye that! But yer victory will mean cow’s dung, I tell ye.” Said the fiend to my face, his voice reeking of putrid fish. Laughing and all, despite knowing full well I had a Blue Lightnin’ pointed at his temple.

“It be the end of the line for ye’, Deep Dave. Yer days of terrorizin' the innocent people of Tombstone are over! Now, you can lay down yer arms and come with me, or ye can come in a coffin.” I readied me trusty iron.

With a dash of energy like I ain’t never seen before, Dave rolled outta' the way, causing me to shoot in surprise. Now standing in front of me, he readied his iron squid, as did I. And there we both stood, the life of one another held in each other's hand. Mutual certain perishment, I think would be a good term.

“Innocent?! Now, don’t make me laugh my life off, Lavoe. Ye don’t even know half the story…” The man in the diver suit began to circle ‘round me, and did I to him.

“I need no story to know yer actions are those of an evil fiend! Open yer mouth and see if I care” I said, clearly not caring for his story - although a bit intrigued, yes, for it been a time since I heard a good one.

“This place, Lavoe, there been a time where a desert 'twas not. There been a time, I tell ye, where me and me brothers lived in peace in the waters that once covered the sands here.” Said Dave to me, his voice filled with memories of a life long-gone. “But then came yer people! They drank our water clean, they killed me brothers, they killed me mother! They made them houses with the rocks on our homes, took the salt that gave us life.”

“If such tragedies occurred, I tell ye, why are the poor men and women of Tombstone to blame?” I gripped my iron harder, feeling his anger boiling away the pain from the explosion.

“And were me people to blame?! Where they sentenced to death just for the crime of life!? Yer only a kid, Lavoe. Ye don’t know the darkness that plagues these lands.”

“Ye don’t need to do this, Dave. We can help ye and yer people, if only you come with me!”

“Help? Lavoe, I already have all the help I could need.” He threw his squid onto a nearby puddle o' water, and raised his gloved fists to me. “But I can help ye alright…” 

Seeing the challenge to me manhood, I threw me harpoon out and grabbed me bronze knuckles.

> **Me.** You carry bronze knuckles with you, Mr. Lavoe?

Ye don’t? Eh, anyhow…

He be the first to throw a punch, right in me guts. “Yer a brave kid, Lavoe. Ye could join us! We can change the world, take me word for it!” I could feel me stomach jump and down; the acid nearing me mouth. Throwing a left jab at his helmet, he stumbled over.

“I’ll never take the hand of a thief! Better dead and pure than living in the filth of crime.” Another jab to his helmet, and one of the soft cloth of his chest. 

“Ye don’t know a thing, don’t ye? I told ye I have help, Lavoe, and good help. Ye could be whatever ye want. Ye could be greater than any of yer dreams!” He rose up and punched me in the jaw. Me mind began spinning ‘round and ‘round, I thought I could see double the stars in the night sky!

“Ney! I’ll get ye by yer guts, and ye will pay for yer crimes.” Still stunned, I punched with all me might right into his helmet. I could hear me knuckles crackin’, but I could hear his helmet cracking too. Water began pouring’ out from it.

“One day, Lavoe. Ye will see me word come true. And when yer gone, I’ll still be ‘round these lands. And I’ll make sure ye can never rest-” Before the fiend could keep mouthing, I raised both me hands in the air, and slammed them down in the top of them head. Must’ve been a good one, ‘cause water began coming from all places. I could see him shaking. But I tell ye, that be like no shaking I seen in me life. Like rats running ‘cross his body!

His shaking left hand reached out for his visor, and opened it wide. Letting all the water come out. Me jaw dropped as I saw countless squids, rotten, yet alive, pouring out of his helmet. I never took notice before, but now I could see, clear as day, how his whole body moved as if waves fared ‘cross it. Me hands shaked as he began to laugh, having revealed the beast he truly was.

“Yer never getting rid of me, Lavoe. We’ll always be here, in one way, or the other…” In the mess that was his face, I could see the squids form in a grin. The most disgusting, rotten grin I’ve ever seen.

And then, I feel something big and heavy rush at me temple from behind. And then I saw no more…

> **Me.** Then, is that how you ended up like this?

Easy your horses, Clarky, I aint’ there yet! Now, with the wind knocked out of me, I could only feel what went down after. I could feel being dragged, tied up, beaten. But then, I heard a voice, one that shook me awake.

> - Mr. Lavoe straighten his back and clears his throat -

“Howdy, Mr. Lavoe! Another beautiful night in the Nevada Desert, is it not? How are you faring?” I heard that deep, grainy voice run down me spine, as something took the bag out of me head.

And then I saw “it”.

The Pale Rider, standing in front of me in the vast desert, a wide smile running ‘cross his ashen-grey skin. He was dressed like a banker, just like I’ve been told, and over his eyes were a pair of circular, dark glasses. But even with them, I could still see the darkness creep out of them and into his face.

“I have to say, Lavoe, I’m quite impressed. Never thought I’d see someone with such disregard for their own life. And all for what, now? Revenge? Bounty? You’re quite the kid, I’ll give you that.” He had an unrecognizable accent, I tell ye. Soft and clear, like them men from California, but not quite.

He moved ever closer to me. And I, none the wiser, tried to get up and run, only to find rope on me feet and hands.

“And what do ye want from me, Devil?!” I yelled with all the courage I had left.

“Me? Nothing. I’m just here on… behalf of a friend. Errands, you know?” His grin grew larger, and he began to laugh, as if he’d told the greatest joke in the whole state.

“Errands? Ye call helping these fiends here “errands”? What type o’ friend would ask for such-”

“SILENCE!” The Pale Rider shouted at the top of his lungs, the grin vanishing into anger. “It’s not your turn to speak, not yet.”

He took his hat out, leaving only the bald greyness underneath to be seen.

“I don’t blame you, Lavoe.” He said, gesturing to me like I were a lost puppy. “You can’t see the whole picture. In fact, you never will. Nor will your children. But you shouldn’t worry your head. The pieces are already set in motion, and I only have you and your people to thank. Your turn.”

“Huh? What are ye talking about, ye mad man. Is this a game to ya'? Is that why ye help those… things?!”

“See how easy it was to wait for your turn?” He said, as his grin returned in full effect.

“As for why I help them? Hmmm… Let me tell you a story, Lavoe. It’s been a while since you’ve heard a good one, correct?” I froze, seeing his gaze pass through me, reading me like a damn open book.

The Pale Rider stood up straight, the red Barley Moon casting his shadow onto me as he extended his arms far and wide.

“You’ve heard the start from Dave, haven't you? O’, how he and his people suffered… how their anger seeped into the very earth. How they were wronged by the men from far across the ocean…”

“And what does that have to do with any of this? With the people of Tombstone?” I tried to untangle my ropes, but they’d been put in rough, and me hands were losing touch.

“It has everything to do with it, Lavoe! You see, this place is special to me. Very special. It is the only place in which the river touches our dear home.”

“River? There ain't no river here.”

“The Black River is, Lavoe. The “other” river. And in it lives the anger of those wronged in times immemorial, of those that lived in fear. Their anger allowed them to ‘be’ past their time. They just needed a little hand to... ‘be’ more than just thoughts.”

“What are ye? Some type of sage? Bringing back the dead back above snakes?”

“You could say so. Anger is a very special feeling, Lavoe. It’s timeless. So, with a little help, they were back on their brand-new feet! Only took a few drops... I didn’t require much bargaining to make them do as I pleased. And while yes, they couldn’t do much more than petty crimes - believe me, I’ve tried -, they served their purpose in full. But that’s alright. The River carries far more things in it than angry cephalopods. I would know. And you’ll know too, soon enough.” Somehow, his grin grew even large-

> **Me.** Mr. Lavoe, could I ask how you managed you remember this entire conversation in such detail?
> 
> **Lavoe.** Oh, well, I just always had a good noggin on me. That, and I've been practicing this whole thing since they've told me I couldn't leave me bed here.
> 
> **Me.** Fair enough, I suppose. Continue, if you may.

So, he smiled wide and all, and then he went on again.

“It took some time, yes, but we got it to work. Jumping from train to train, bank to bank. Sometimes we got what we were looking for - or, at least, what I told them we were looking for. And sometimes I just let it slide. But, it was all worth it. I have acquired my material needs, yes, but that’s not what’s important, now is it, Lavoe? Actions matter the most, and actions we have done. Actions that will one day matter more than any gold or gem in the whole world. You just need to sit and wait for the castle to tumble over…”

“But-.” Me hands hurt like no other day, me chest hurt, and I was falling asleep. I couldn’t speak.

“But what for? Disorder, Mr. Lavoe, that’s the “for”. Only great things can come from chaos. And when you are on top, it can only go down.” He laughed again. Laughed through his dark teeth.

“And I’ve had my fair bit of entertainment in this time, I won’t lie. But my mission is nearing its end too, Lavoe. My only task left is to misdirect, to lead you astray into a path with no end. To play the game as I please...”

“And why on earth would ye do that?” I let out as a gasp. Me vision turning black by the second.

The Barley Moon that once stood in the sky soon turned black. And in its centre was only the Pale Rider.

“Because that's how you win this game, Lavoe. And because I would rather people not bother me in the future when I’m working, don't you agree?”

“Yer insane.”

“Perhaps, perhaps... Now, this settles your job, Lavoe. As it settles theirs. I’m sure the Earp brothers will have a lot to deal with in the near future. And, oh… what a shame it would be now that they are beaten and tired from this whole ordeal. But I’ll stick close by, close by the carrals, watching the cards fall into order. With how messy my dear Gang has been, I'm sure someone else will try to step up their game!”

“Yet ain’t gonna get away with this.” I spat blood in his face. But if he noticed, he didn’t care.

“Believe me, Lavoe. I already have. It'll be O.K" he smirked at the saying of that. "And one last thing: cut that damn afro of yours, it doesn’t go well with the hat. Oh, and tell your superiors that Mister Simon sends in his regards!” The Pale Rider was soon up in front of me; my whole view covered in darkness.

“The hell's an afr-” But before I could finish, he knocked me out cold with the butt of his rifle.

> **Me.** And…?
> 
> **Lavoe.** And… here I am.
> 
> **Me.** I… don’t really know what to do with this information, Mr. Lavoe. Would you like me to forward it to Virgil?
> 
> **Lavoe.** Something tells me he has better things to worry about.
> 
> **Me.** But, what about the gang?
> 
> **Lavoe.** I’ll keep an eye out for em’ once I’m outta’ here. But I wouldn’t be surprised if they don’t show them faces here again. I think we got far more things to worry about for the time being. Gotta find that dammed Lake and put an end to the madness.
> 
> **Me.** If you say so, then. I’ll call Officer Cohen.
> 
> **Lavoe.** Please, Clarky. I got a few things to go over with him.
