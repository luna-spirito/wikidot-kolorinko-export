**Registered Phenomenon Code:** 870

**Object Class:** Alpha-White

**Hazard Types:** Sapient, Extra-Dimensional

[[/div]]

**Containment Protocols:** RPC-870 is to be kept in a light containment chamber. All rumors or proof regarding the cryogenically frozen head of Walt Disney are to be debunked and denied. 

Testing regarding RPC-870 is to be performed regularly with a minimum of one Level-2 Researcher, who will operate RPC-870 and document results, and two supervising Level-3 Researchers.

**Description:** RPC-870 is a 1966 Ericsson T65 Rotary Telephone welded to a glass case containing a large amount of ice as well as what appears to be the frozen head of deceased American Entrepreneur Walter Elias Disney. There are no ports on the phone, preventing its use as a conventional telephone. Dialing a number and taking the handset off as if making a call allows the phone operator to speak with an entity designated RPC-870-A. 

This entity claims to be the deceased Walter Disney, who is unaware of the fact that he is deceased. RPC-870-A is willing to discuss many different topics but will typically lose focus and quickly segue back to the topic of his “EPCOT Project.[[footnote]] EPCOT, or the Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow, was the name of a planned commune that was planned to try out ideas meant to solve problems seen in modern cities at the time. It was the main purpose of the Florida project, that ended up turning into Walt Disney World [[/footnote]]” 

RPC-870-A claims that they are currently inside their theme park, Disneyland, but they have noted that the layout and design of the park in general, as well as specific features are vastly different. They have noted that it is continuously around sunset, and the entire park is empty, with a large amount of sand and dust on the park. 

**Discovery:** RPC-870 was discovered on December 17th, 1966, after Disney had been interred at Forest Lawn Cemetery. It was found sitting in Disney’s chair behind his desk by Ub Iwerks, who believed it to be a disturbing prank after seeing it and using the phone to speak to RPC-870-A. Embedded Authority agents were alerted regarding this, taking RPC-870 without any issues.

**Experiment Log:** After RPC-870 was acquired, attempts to understand the nature of RPC-870-A were made. Current experimental procedures consist of a participating researcher and an observing researcher. The former will note what they hear via RPC-870, while the latter will write down the actions of the former in order to observe potential memetic effects. In order to have constant cooperation, participants were instructed to refer to RPC-870-A however they wanted to be referred to as.

> //Backster picks up the phone and appears to wait for several seconds//

> Backster - Well I was wondering, Mr. Disney, where are you right now?

> RPC-870-A - Well, now that you mention it, it seems I've lost myself in thought in Main Street again. I’m sitting in… Disneyland, or so I presume.
> 
> Backster - So you... presume? What do you mean by that, Mr. Disney?
> 
> RPC-870-A - Well, first things first Dr. Backster, I'll have no more of the Mr. Disney this Mr. Disney that. Just call me Walt if you would. Anyways, I believe I’m in Disneyland, but it certainly looks different from the last time I remember being here. For one there’s plenty of sand all over the grounds, enough to come up to my ankles, were I to place my foot down to the ground. Then there’s this aggrandizing statue of myself holding Mickey Mouse’s hand. I’m sure I would’ve remembered asking to get this installed. And I can’t even see Edison Square! It should be right in front of this bench as well. It’s all... quite peculiar.
> 
> Backster - Do you know how you got to Disneyland, or wherever this is? 
> 
> RPC-870-A - Oh, I wouldn't worry yourself over that. I’m sure I just brought the girls here and let them go with their mother and play while I went back to working on EPCOT. Ah, EPCOT, it’s truly going to become something special. I can feel it. 
>  
> Backster - Are you sure ab-
> 
> //Researcher Backster stops talking and begins taking notes immediately//
> 
> RPC-870-A - Oh I’m quite sure Dr Backster, now like I said I need to get back to work, I’ve let too much time slip by talking about needless things, have a great day sir, and remember, if you can dream it, you can do it. 
> 
> //Researcher Backster stops taking notes and becomes silent for a moment//
> 
> Backster - Walt? Walt?
> 
> //Researcher Backster sighs and places the phone on its receiver.//

> Following more failed attempts to contact RPC-870-A again, it was believed that perhaps one of Disney's confidants would be able to get RPC-870-A to communicate with them, as well as confirm its identity as the real Walt Disney. It took 4 months to get approved and set up, conveniently allowing Researchers to have the experiment take place on December 5th, Walt Disney's birthday.
> 
>   Authority agents in the Orlando Police Department were able to arrange a meeting with Roy O Disney and explain the situation. The agents explained that an anonymous caller had been using Disney's name to make calls around the area, but that he was too hard to distinguish from the real Walt. They told him that they figured Roy would be able to call out whatever he didn't know and possibly figure out who it was, since his knowledge of Walt's life was pretty extensive. He agreed, and was given a P-1 amnestic before using RPC-870 to communicate with RPC-870-A

**Post Experiment Notes:** Following the experiment, Roy was injected with the P-2amnestic, while Researchers investigated RPC-870. He was told that the phone was nothing more than a prank, but he suddenly felt ill after talking to it and fainted. He was asked to get some rest and that the agents would be investigating the phone.

As noted, the head was no longer visible, and the ice within was beginning to melt. Over the next few hours, the ice melted completely, leaving nothing but a small amount of water in the bottom of the box. All attempts to use RPC-870 led to hearing nothing but wind and the distant sound of music.
