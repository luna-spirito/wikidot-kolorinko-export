 **Registered Phenomena Code:** 017

**Object Class:** Beta-White

**Hazard Types:** Transmutation Hazard, Tychokinetic Hazard 

**Containment Protocols:** Instances of RPC-017 are to be stored at Site-016. All collected instances of RPC-017 are stored in 1 m^^3^^ secure containment units when not in use or transport. During transport, instances are to be kept in munitions cans and attached to the frame of the vehicle(s) used. All instances, excluding RPC-017-5, and -10, are available for delivery to other Authority facilities for the purpose of experimentation and/or analysis. 

A Security Clearance Level of 3 or higher is needed to requisition instances of RPC-017. Small samples of variants RPC-017-1, RPC-017-4, and RPC-017-8 are also kept in armory vaults at RPC installations at the request of ranking Security staff, for use during emergencies -- these instances may be used only if permitted by the Site Director. 

Orders via the customer service line are made every 12 months by the current provisions manager (currently Researcher Mann). Official requisition forms are collected at the end of March and should be sent 3-5 business days in advance.  

**Description:** RPC-017 is the designation for a collection of firearm lubricants that give any firearm they are applied to anomalous properties. These effects range from physical changes in the firearm a given sample is applied to, to behavioral changes in the user who applies it. Bottles containing RPC-017 instances are marked with manufacturer's slogans that hint as to what a given sample's effect(s) are, though these slogans are vague and are often understood only in retrospect. 

In addition to the slogan, "Harrison Munitions and General Supply CO",[[footnote]]The manufacturing company.[[/footnote]] a price, and a telephone number are also found on a sample's label. All calls placed to this company are relayed to a customer support hotline. No records or calling history of the said company have been found.

Instances of RPC-017 are known to spontaneously appear in gun shops, pawnshops, gun shows, military surplus stores, military bases, police stations, armories, hunting outfitter stores, and flea markets. Instances of RPC-017 may be acquired via its customer service line, however, the serial number of a given sample is required in order to purchase an additional supply.[[footnote]]This means a given sample must be discovered before it can be further purchased.[[/footnote]] Said bottles will anomalously arrive in packages to the caller's location. This will only occur if the caller is situated within an area lacking surveillance and leaves appropriate payment within a manila envelope. If either condition is not met, the transaction is unsuccessful. 

Chemical analysis has found no significant differences between RPC-017 and other consumer firearm oils.  

**Discovery:** RPC-017 was discovered in 1945 by Authority agents embedded in the US military. The anomaly was found in a weapons crate containing Thompson submachine guns (#9403TR). Authority attention was raised when the unit receiving the crate reported inexplicable success in the battle of Iwo Jima, halting a counterattack that much larger units had failed to stop and seizing a series of heavily defended tunnels.  RPC-017 was recovered from the unit commander by the embedded agent after amnestics were administered to those involved.  Other instances have been discovered in increasing rates since the initial discovery.  [http://www.rpc-wiki.net/rpc-017-1 Recovered documents] indicate that RPC-017-1 had a significant impact on the battle.

= **__Incident Report RPC-017-1.1__**
Researcher Hines was testing RPC-017-1 when a containment breach occurred. Hines helped evacuate a team of researchers to a panic room and upon being ordered to join the rest, Hines ignored the order. He then proceeded to assist ASF squads during recontainment.  An interview was conducted regarding Hines’ insubordinate behavior.  

> **Interviewed:** Researcher Hines 
> **Interviewer:** Researcher Mann
> **Foreword:** Interview Regarding Incident RPC-017-1.1.
> 
> -----
> 
> **<Begin Log>** 
> 
> **Mann:** Hello Daniel, how are you holding up?
> 
> **Hines:** (pauses, drawing a breath) Okay given the circumstances I suppose.  
> 
> **Mann:** Do you know why I am conducting this interview?
> 
> **Hines:** Yes.
> 
> **Mann:** Alright, so what was going through your mind when that alarm first went off?
> 
> **Hines:** I just felt that I had to keep my colleagues safe. I was here during a previous breach and didn’t feel that way before. Back then I felt scared.  This time though, (pauses) I don’t know what really came over me, I just felt I had to keep everyone safe.  
> 
> **Mann:** Do you think that feeling in any way involves you having operated a pistol coated with RPC-017-1?
> 
> **Hines:** Absolutely. I guarantee if I wasn’t, I would have just ran for the nearest panic room.  
> 
> **Mann:** How about when you disobeyed the order to get in the panic room with the others?
> 
> **Hines:** I knew there were still people not safe in the site, I just felt it wouldn’t be right to not help them, even if I didn’t know them as well, and it wasn’t my responsibility, I just had to.  (Pauses)  It had to be that oil again, I can’t see any reason to not get in a panic room right now, and we all know I’m not that brave. Plus pissing off the Site Management is the last thing I’d want to do.  
> 
> **Mann:** I suppose that’s true, I can’t really see you putting that dental coverage at risk.  
> 
> **Hines:** (chuckles) My teeth need less work than yours.
> 
> **Mann:** So, if I were to ask you; ethically, do you think helping the ASF squad was the right thing to do?
> 
> **Hines:** I do not think it was the right thing to do; the ASF is professional and trained for such things and I'm not. I could've put them all and the whole site at risk.
> 
> **Mann:** Then why did you continue?
> 
> **Hines:** I said I don't //think// it was the right thing to do. But I wasn't thinking. I was just feeling. And it felt... right.  
> 
> **<End Log>** 
> 
> -----
> 
> **Closing Statement:** Researcher Hines was commended for the role he played in helping to secure the site, and reprimanded for disobeying an order. Information regarding the incident was added to Researcher Hines’ personnel file.

= **__Interview Log RPC-017.2__**

Instances of RPC-017 with dates of manufacture newer than 1980 have a telephone number written on the bottle. The following is a transcript of a call made to this number.  
 
> **Interviewed:** Unknown Female Voice, on Customer Support Line 
> **Interviewer:** Researcher Brakke
> **Foreword:** The following is a transcript of a call to the support line marked on a bottle of RPC-017.
> 
> -----
> 
> **<Begin Log>** 
> 
> **Brakke:** Hello?
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Hi, you’ve reached the Harrison Munitions and General Supply CO customer support line, what can I help you with?
> 
> **Brakke:** Yes, could you tell me about your products?
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Certainly!  We are adding new products to our inventory all the time though, so you will need to be a bit more specific.  
> 
> **Brakke:** I would like to know more about your gun oils. 
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Well, that’s how this company got started, we introduce a new gun oil blend every few years or so.  Currently there are 15 specific blends, some are more common than others, we charge $5.99 per 8 oz. bottle, and shipping is free via <GARBLED> if you buy 6 or more bottles.
> 
> **Brakke:** Okay. What can you tell me about the company?
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** (Sighs. In monotone) "We were founded by Samuel Harrison in 1927 in the great state of Kentucky. The company started out as a hobby for when our founder wasn’t busy with his scientific studies.  Due to Samuel’s hard work we were able to corner the market on specialty firearm oils by the 1950s, and we’ve been expanding ever since.  Harrison Munitions and General Supply CO now manufactures and sells specialty goods to over 4 realities, and 13 ████████████████!  The company is still owned by the Harrison family to this day, and our company has a bright future!"
> 
> **Brakke:** Very interesting. How much product do you move per year, and to who?
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Well, I can’t really divulge some of that information, but I can tell you the vast majority of our clients are individuals or small stores.  We came from a small business so we like to support them. However, we //are// currently engaged in a contract with a //very// large organization. I can’t say who but it is big-time business. As for how much product we sell, I really don’t know, that’s something the shipping and sales departments handle.  May I ask why you want to know all of this? Are you looking for a new job?
> 
> **Brakke:** Well I’m actually quite satisfied with my current job, but if that changes I’ll let you know.  As for why I am asking, I work for an organization that has acquired some of your products and I was a bit curious--
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** (Cuts off Researcher Brakke) -- You were curious where you could get more, right?
> 
> **Brakke:** Yes, that is exactly right.
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Go ahead and read the codes on the bottom of the bottles you would like more of. I should warn you though, that due to a large order made recently, we again have shortages, and can only send 100 bottles per order for the time being. Also tell me the quantity of each that you would like. 
> 
>  **Brakke:** About that; why does your business model mandate that additional samples can be purchased only if one is discovered first?
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Due to the nature of our products, we have to tightly regulate their distribution. We can also gauge the competency of our marketing this way while maintaining a low profile. At least in theory I guess. I’m not in marketing. Seems it has worked well so far.
> 
> **Brakke:** Okay, we... I am interested in getting several of the "Gold Standard". Maybe, 100?
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** I'm sorry, that was... an exclusive product we sold to... how do you know about that?
> 
> **Brakke:** Ah, okay. Well. We are (whispers off record) also interested in the "Offers Great Protection". Let's say 100 bottles please.
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Alright, let me just... there you are. Oh. It seems we have already shipped that order to you.
>  
> **Brakke:** ... I don't see anything.
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** You're with the... "Authority", right? That's what I'm seeing your location as at least. We fulfilled the recurring order of "Offers Great Protection" earlier this week.
> 
> **Brakke:** You did?
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Yes, I'm looking at the invoice right now. That was most of our supply and you'd have to wait for more to be produced. In fact, you guys are the big-time business I was talking about; we've committed most of our production line to fulfill your order. Are you looking to return the items for a refund?
> 
> **Brakke:** ... no, no the order is fine, um. I just wanted to confirm... the number of bottles on the sending end. You know, to make sure they all made it here.
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** The invoice says [DATA REDACTED] bottles. Is that how many you got?
> 
> **Brakke:** ... wow. I mean, yes. Yes. Thank you. What is the name on the order anyway?
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Can't say. There are just initials. "GD". 
> 
> **Brakke:** Is... there a number, or two digits after those initials?
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Yes, GD-[DATA REDACTED]. 
> 
> **Brakke:** Hunh, alright well you have a nice day, thank you for the package.
> 
> **Unknown Female Voice:** Very welcome. Thanks for choosing Harrison Munitions and General Supply CO.
> 
> **<End Log>** 
> 
> -----
> 
> **Closing Statement:** 
> 
> The phone call was unable to be traced.  The name Samuel Harrison has been tied to a known chemical scientist and Sergeant from the First World War who disappeared from his hometown along with his family after a fire devastated the area.  Voice analysis has found no matches for the female voice on the phone.  
> 
> Researcher Brakke was treated with amnestics and reconditioned for another assignment.
