**Registered Phenomena Code:** 532

**Object Class:** Beta-Yellow

**Hazard Types:** Animated Hazard, Aquatic Hazard, Mechanical Hazard, Sapient Hazard

**Containment Protocols:** 


@@@@
[[collapsible show="+Show Obsolescent Containment Protocol 532-A" hide="Opening..."]]
> RPC-532 is contained within a dry-dock at OL-Site-532-A, a section of the ██████ Shipyard in ███████, Argentina. All site personnel will maintain the cover story of belonging to a foreign scrapping company, and will not deny local rumors of "difficulties" that are preventing the scrapping of RPC-532. All site personnel are forbidden from entering RPC-532 outside of testing experiments, and site security will prevent locals from entering RPC-532. Due to its physical condition, RPC-532 is incapable of motion, or resisting its containment in any way; however, persons inside RPC-532 are at risk of anomalous industrial accidents.
[[/collapsible]]


[[collapsible show="+Show Obsolescent Containment Protocol 532-B" hide="Opening..."]]
> RPC-532 is contained within a roofed dry-dock at OL-Site-532-B, at a shipyard of the Authority contractor Milliard Steel in █████, California. One Containment Security Division officer will be present to direct the operations of Milliard Steel security, and prevent trespassing locals from entering the Site. Due to the acquiescence of RPC-532 and its continued physical debilitation, the risk of a containment failure is minimal and site personnel are allowed to enter RPC-532. Research staff that intend to enter RPC-532 must be supervised by the on-site CSD officer and a Milliard Steel employee to prevent industrial accidents.
[[/collapsible]]

RPC-532 is contained within Authority Naval Station █, at Dock 24-C. A full review of RPC-532's hull integrity is to be conducted annually, while standard maintenance is at the discretion of the Commanding Officer of Naval Station █ and RPC-532 itself. During operational use, RPC-532 is not to venture further than the 25km exclusion zone around Naval Station █ unless its route is approved by a Level-4 Protection Division maritime officer. Supervised tours of RPC-532 by on-site personnel are allowed when not in official use. Research personnel wishing to interview RPC-532 may submit a request to the Containment Liason Agency. 

**Description:** RPC-532 is a sentient WW2 Fletcher-class destroyer constructed by Bethlehem Shipping Co. and commissioned into the US Navy in 1943 as USS //Heermann// (DD-532). RPC-532 served with great distinction in the Pacific Theater during the war, notably as the sole surviving surface combatant of Task Unit 77.4.3 (//Taffy 3//) in the famous Battle off Samar[[footnote]]A significant naval battle of WW2, where a vastly superior Japanese force was delayed and eventually turned back by a force of American escort carriers and light surface combatants.[[/footnote]]. After a lengthy and widely-traveled postwar service, RPC-532 was decommissioned and transferred to the Argentinian Navy in 1961 as the ARA //Almirante Brown// (D-20). RPC-532 did not manifest any anomalous activity during its period of active service; when decommissioned from Argentinian service in a highly dilapidated state and sent to be scrapped, RPC-532 began to exert anomalous control over its systems.

RPC-532 is capable of limited control over its equipment and fittings. Examples of this autonomy include:
* Turning its rudder by a maximum of 15 degrees at dry-dock and 7 degrees while underway.
* Releasing superheated steam from valves in its engine room.
* Forcing the structural failure of rusted beams and deck plates.
* Slamming bulkheads open or closed at a recorded speed of up to 5 meters per second.

The means by which RPC-532 influences itself is not fully understood by RPC-532 or the Authority. Chains or other physical impediments are capable of holding the rudder or bulkheads in place against RPC-532's exertions. 

RPC-532 is fully sentient and capable of carrying a vocal conversation in English through the voice tubes connecting the bridge to the engine room. RPC-532's voice is distorted but recognizably female; occasionally, RPC-532 will seamlessly change to a male voice identified as Cmdr. Amos T. Hathaway (USN), its captain during the Battle off Samar. RPC-532 prefers to be referred to as //Heermann,// its first and third name.

RPC-532 is aware of its surroundings within direct line of sight, and demonstrates a clarity of vision comparable to a human using a pair of binoculars. RPC-532 is also apparently aware of anything that happens within its hull or on its deck; this has led to several embarrassing incidents for personnel that expected privacy.

RPC-532 appears to have memory degradation similar to that of a human of the same age (75 years), and has difficulty recalling many years of its existence with the exceptions of its first shakedown cruise, the Battle off Samar, and the wedding of Prince Rainer III of Monaco. Though it is capable of a wide range of discussions, RPC-532 will rapidly lose interest with most non-maritime subjects. Conversely, RPC-532 is highly interested in modern naval technologies and the Asian-Pacific naval arms race. Interviewers are advised to familiarize themselves with those topics to make RPC-532 more amiable to their historical questions.

RPC-532 continues to maintain a stable psychological profile, as far as Authority experts are able to determine. RPC-532 has never claimed to be "trapped" in its body, or otherwise show dissatisfaction with the limits of its existence; deviations to this attitude are to be immediately reported to the Commanding Officer of Naval Station █.

**Acquisition Logs:** RPC-532 was noticed by the AIB[[footnote]]Authority Intelligence Bureau[[/footnote]] when an Authority agent visiting family in ███████, Argentina heard rumors of a "cursed ship" laid up in the city. Investigation revealed that ARA //Almirante Brown,// former USS //Heermann// due for scrapping in the city's shipyard, was the supposed "cursed ship". Many near-fatal industrial accidents had plagued workers attempting to enter the warship to strip it of valuable equipment; beginning with structural failures, workers soon refused to enter the warship when bulkheads started closing on them, crushing hands and ankles.

RPC-532 was easily purchased from the local scrappers by an Authority front company; the establishment of a containment facility was made more difficult by the onset of the Falklands War. After a considerable delay, in June of 1982 a historian from the Research Division arrived at OL-Site 203-A. His first expedition into RPC-532 discovered its ability to speak through the bridge voice-tubes, after dodging several hostile bulkheads; that interview consisted of many shouted expletives from RPC-532. The second interview was recorded and is available below.

[[collapsible show="+Show OL-Site 203-A Interview #2, 7/15/1982" hide="Opening..."]]
> **Interviewed:** RPC-532
> 
> **Interviewer:** Researcher (History) Sherrick 
> 
> **Foreword:** //RPC-532 is laid up in dry-dock at the ██████ Shipyard in ███████, Argentina. Rsr. Sherrick has entered RPC-532 once before,//
> 
> **<Begin Log>**
> 
> 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** I'm back. I said I'd be back.
> 
> **RPC-532:** Go away. 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** Well, thanks for not trying to slam the bridge hatch on me this time.
> 
> **RPC-532:** One "thanks" isn't going to be enough for betraying me, you [expletive].
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** I'm not with the Navy. But I do know who you are. The //Heermann,// right? USS //Heermann?//
> 
> **RPC-532:** God, I haven't heard that name in a long time.
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** It's surreal to be talking to you, frankly. You served well in World War 2.
> 
> **RPC-532:** I served well? Damn you, I charged a line of Jap battlewagons[[footnote]]Slang for battleship. //Heermann// closed with several Imperial Japanese Navy battleships during the Battle off Samar to launch her torpedoes.[[/footnote]], I dueled the Chikuma[[footnote]]IJN //Chikuma// was a //Tone//-class heavy cruiser present at the Battle off Samar.[[/footnote]] to her grave[[footnote]]//Chikuma// and //Heermann// were both badly damaged in the duel. Limping away and under air attack, the //Chikuma// was scuttled later in the day.[[/footnote]]! Ever hear of Samar? 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** I grew on stories of the Navy, Mr- Miss //Heermann//. Samar is the greatest of them all.
> 
> **RPC-532:** You're goddamn right it is. 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** When Americans have their backs up against the wall.
> 
> **RPC-532:** Christ, you're making an old girl nostalgic. 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** I just want to talk. Hear your story.
> 
> //There is a short pause.//
> 
> **RPC-532:** Ah, what the hell. At least my obituary has a newspaper. 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** I'm very glad to hear that. You've had a quite a career - well, a life.
> 
> **RPC-532:** You know, they laid me up and put in reserve after the war. Brought me back in '51. I- Christ, I would've been satisfied if all I got was the war. I watched my crew get home safe to their families, brought them into San Diego. There were moms and kids lining the wharf. They all loved me. Thought I was special.
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** Of course. Anyone would be proud of the record you earned in WW2.
> 
> **RPC-532:** Yeah. God, those were the days. But they brought me back. I toured the world, hell, I was invited to the wedding of the Prince of Monaco. Training ship at the end, I think I liked that the best. All those fresh sailors, learning the ropes on me, and they'd tell the youngsters about Samar. I loved that. 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** So, you can hear people talking on you? What about other ships?
> 
> **RPC-532:** Course I could hear them, they were my crew. And don't ask an old girl questions when she's smiling about the past, okay?
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** It's my job.
> 
> **RPC-532:** Yeah, well, you're in a uniform, it must be your job to shut up and listen every now and again. So listen. All that is fluff. The Navy [expletive] betrayed me.
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** The Military Assistance Program.
> 
> **RPC-532:** I didn't think I'd go back to sea when they decommissioned me again in '57. And you know, I'd had a full life. A little World War. A nice tour of Europe. I wouldn't have complained. None of my sisters did. But they betrayed me. They sent me, a god damned war hero, to the god damned Argentinian Navy! I'm not asking to be a museum ship, but- but I never knew anything except the US Navy. All those years. Samar. They didn't care about any of it. 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** A lot of other ships were transferred to foreign navies under MAP.
> 
> **RPC-532:** A little minesweeper going to South Vietnam isn't the same. And - and either way, they just threw us all away! For God's sake, they sent Enterprise[[footnote]]USS //Enterprise// (CV-6), a //Yorktown//-class aircraft carrier and the most decorated American warship of WW2. Scrapped in 1958 after a failed campaign to preserve her as a museum ship.[[/footnote]] to the breakers! Enterprise! 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** So you were angry, in your Argentinian service. Rightfully angry.
> 
> **RPC-532:** It couldn't last forever. Duty is duty, no matter the flag. Funding was [expletive], especially after they got newer hand-me-downs[[footnote]]Likely referring to ex-British Type 42 destroyers and French-built //Drummond//-class corvettes acquired by the Argentinian Navy in the 70s. Several WW2-era former USN warships remained in Argentinian service during the Falklands War, but all of the slightly older //Fletcher// destroyers had been retired.[[/footnote]], but most of those sailors were alright, I mean- I just got older and older. Rusting away, and so bitter when I thought about it... you know, sitting in port, I had too much time to think. I never really thought too hard before, you know, there's a guy controlling your rudder with a wheel up in the bridge - why bother thinking when it's all controlled? 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** Would you say that this, uh, time to yourself, perhaps mixed with the anger and resentment from your transfer - would you say this caused you to be able to control certain functions of yourself? And speak in your own voice?
> 
> **RPC-532:** I wouldn't know. Maybe. Being sent to be scrapped while there's a war here, I couldn't take it anymore. I... God, I was a bad ship. A bad ship! I hurt those men, they weren't responsible for any of this. They weren't my crew, but still-
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** I think, given the circumstances, your actions are entirely understandable. I suppose people don't really consider their ships' feelings in any of this. 
> 
> **RPC-532:** Well, that's just the way it is. 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** A ship having a voice is unusual, to say the least. Now, how that happened-
> 
> **RPC-532:** Lay off, doc. Hell, I've got a question for you. A request, since you're actually listening to me. The Belgrano[[footnote]]The former USS //Pheonix,// a //Brooklyn//-class light cruiser present at the Attack on Pearl Harbor. After WW2 she was transferred to the Argentinian Navy and fought in the Falklands War. She holds the dubious distinction of being the first warship to be sunk by a nuclear submarine, HMS //Conquerer,// on 5/2/1982.[[/footnote]] died a good death. Older than I am, finally put to rest by the Royal Navy. I want that. I- please, just tow me out to sea, let me sink. Say the Brits got me.
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** Would you still be, ah, aware of everything? Sunk, at the bottom?
> 
> **RPC-532:** I'd finally be resting.
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** Well, //Heermann,// like I said - I'm not Navy. I can't do that for you, and I don't want to. My organization has a different offer.
> 
> **RPC-532:** An offer? You don't need to ask permission to scrap an old girl. I can't stop a crane. These locals just got scared when I dropped the deck on them. God, I can't do that forever.
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** Nothing like that. My organization specializes in the strange and unusual-
> 
> **RPC-532:** You just keep going on about that 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** -and in the course of our duties, we have a powerful fleet. I've sent this situation - your plight - up the ladder, and there are receptive ears. We want to tow you back to an American shipyard, get you restored. Our pockets are deep.
> 
> **RPC-532:** Now why would anyone bother with a hulk like me?
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** Historians who want to talk to you, Maritime officers that want to save a very special piece of humanity's naval history. Consider this a job offer. If you're amiable about it.
> 
> **RPC-532:** The Navy doesn't need me any more. 
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** We're not the Navy. Hell, we've got better ships than the Navy. Our Maritime guys want you for a training ship. Living history, a reminder of the footsteps they're walking in.
> 
> **RPC-532:** I- I didn't expect that. Are you joking?
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** Absolutely-
> 
> **RPC-532:** Please, don't say it's a joke.
> 
> **Dr. Sherrick:** Absolutely not. 
> 
> **RPC-532:** You- you [expletive]. I thought I was forgotten. Oh, God, of course.
> 
> **<End Log>**
> 
> **Closing Statement:** //RPC-532 was towed to the US for a lengthy refit the following year, after the Falklands War ended. During this time and after, RPC-532 has expressed growing appreciation and loyalty towards the Authority. RPC-532 has remained under Beta classification, due to its sentience.//

**Current Status:** 
* **As of 8/5/1990,** RPC-532 has been commissioned into the Authority's naval forces as the RPCAS //Heermann,// a training ship of the Pacific Fleet whose home port is Naval Station █. 
@@@@
* **Update, 9/4/1994:** RPC-532 has cycled through over 1,000 Authority sailors and cadets in the course of its duties as a training ship.
@@@@
* **Update, 26/8/1996:** RPC-532 noted a "strange feeling of emptiness". RPC-532 ceased to communicate with Authority personnel for 4 weeks afterwards.
@@@@
* **Update, 3/18/2002:** RPC-532 continues to exhibit a stable psychological profile. Expresses contentedness at its current circumstances. Highly anticipates its annual post-maintenance shakedown cruise, where it is allowed to steer itself for a half an hour. 
@@@@
* **Update, 9/4/2005:** RPC-532 has cycled through over 10,000 Authority sailors and cadets in the course of its duties as a training ship.
@@@@
* **Update, 11/2/2009:** RPC-532 has recently demonstrated the ability to broadcast its voice through the on-board PA system. This first occurred during a group tour of OAA/RMA[[footnote]]Orphaned by Anomalous Action/Resistant to Memory Alteration[[/footnote]] children, when RPC-532 interrupted the tour guide to personally narrate its memory of the royal marriage of Monaco.
