**Registered Phenomena Code:** 263

**Object Class:** Omega-Black

**Hazard Types:** Ecological Hazard, Aggression Hazard, Climatological Hazard, Geological Hazard, Psychotronic Hazard, Contact Hazard, Explosive Hazard, Extreme Temperature Hazard, Mind Control Hazard, Visual Hazard

**Containment Protocols:** The Authority is to ensure the continual procurement of anomalies, fulfilling a quota that is determined quarterly and subject to sudden modification by the Board of the Global Directors. Seismic activity within and around Mount Vesuvius is to remain a high priority, and in direct proportion to the duration between its eruptions.

When deemed necessary by Authority Earth scientists, two appropriate (preferably inanimate) anomalies are to be selected from Authority containment and transported to Mount Vesuvius per OPERATION WHITECLOAK. Emergency protocols are in place to expedite the selection and delivery processes if necessary. 

Minor eruptions of Mount Vesuvius that are anticipated to produce only plumage are allowed to occur given direct orders from the Global Directors.

As of March 18, 1944,[[footnote]]The date of Mount Vesuvius' most recent large-scale eruption.[[/footnote]] Alpha-White class anomalous objects are no longer sufficient candidates for OPERATION WHITECLOAK.

-----
**Description:** RPC-263 is a spherical object roughly 5 meters in diameter that is submerged beneath Mount (Mt.) Vesuvius within the Earth's mantle.

RPC-263 is able to withstand temperatures in excess of 1250°C and is likely constructed from iron, although it cannot be ruled out that the material may be anomalous in nature. RPC-263 has approximately 1.5 meter-wide partitions in its outer layer which allow for partial visualization into its interior. A cube made of a similar material is embedded in the interior of RPC-263, its vertices intersecting the frames of these partitions. A complex lattice of finer rods encircles the structure -- the purpose of this lattice outside of probable reinforcement is unknown. RPC-263 is currently suspended within the asthenosphere.[[footnote]]Roughly a depth of 80 kilometers.[[/footnote]] 

RPC-263-A is a humanoid structure within RPC-263 that resembles the //Vitruvian Man//,[[footnote]]An illustration made by Leonardo Da Vinci in 1490 that displays a man of perfect proportionality in two superimposed positions.[[/footnote]] and is likewise inscribed within the parameters of the structure's cube and sphere. 

RPC-263-B is the designation given to any pyroclastic plume of smoke, rock fragments, magma, and various other ejecta expelled by Mt. Vesuvius during eruptions. Debris from RPC-263-B ejections consistently contain human genetic material. This genetic material has been cross-referenced with the known living descendants of [DATA REDACTED] (see //Document 263.GT-2019-FR// - {{(LEVEL 4 CLASSIFIED)}}); genetic comparisons to [DATA REDACTED] are unavailable; given the historical figure's ascetic lifestyle, it is likely that he did not reproduce. 

RPC-263-B exhibits a tractive effect on known anomalous entities. When observed by an anomalous sentience or sapience, RPC-263-B is considered a Class III, second-order[[footnote]]Second-order anomalies are those that affect other anomalies.[[/footnote]] cognitohazard, and will result in compulsive behavior including near-ceaseless attempts at decreasing the distance between the anomalous instance and Mt. Vesuvius.
-----
**History & Discovery:** The Authority (then the Auctoritas Impertus[[footnote]]A semi-autonomous arm of the Papal Inquisition started by Pope Callixtus III which would become known for its use and purview of the supernatural.[[/footnote]]) was made aware of RPC-263 via a member of the Order of St. John the Hesychast.[[footnote]]A monastery on the slopes of Mount Vesuvius. The term "Hesychast" refers to a mystical tradition (Hesychasm) that emphasizes reclusive withdrawal for prayer and meditation. "St. John"  is also known historically as "John the Silent".[[/footnote]] This individual, whose name is unknown, initiated contact with Auctoritas Impertus officials on 24 August, 1629 prophesying an imminent and catastrophic eruption from Mt. Vesuvius. 

The Auctoritas Impertus had entered into a working relationship with the Order of St. John the Hesychast in 1514 such that PoI-263[[footnote]]Leonardo Da Vinci.[[/footnote]] might be stopped in his seemingly at-will production of anomalous artifacts, machines, and entities, which was both prolific and unmanageable. Numerous attempts were made by the Auctoritas Impertus to confront and arrest PoI-263; however, all were met with failure due to anomalous counter-measures prepared by PoI-263, who anticipated the tactics of these attempts with relative ease, leading some to theorize his abilities as reality-bending (unconfirmed). Additionally, each unsuccessful attempt seemingly invigorated the creativity of PoI-263 to engineer increasingly refined artifacts for the purposes of sabotage; thus the incidence of rogue anomalous engineering was exacerbated given each failure.

> The heretic saw to the frustration of our machinations against him with ones of his own -- devilish in their construction and cleverness, 'twere as if God himself had him privy  of our plans and arrivals. One day a trap that snatched up men and hoisted them up in a bag as would they grains of harvest; another assay thwarted by an unseen wall, as if the Hand of Beelzebub were staying our advance with some glee. And all the while, the corky witch slipping through our fingers. This very morrow, he escapes into the welken on a winged, wooden dragon! Our Lords are growing weary of these fruitless efforts, embarrassing as they are costly. The heretic has bested us at every whim; we are but playthings to him.
> [[>]]
> - //Adger Hatt, Auctoritas Impertus, Grand Possessioner, 1512//
[[/>]]

It was known through the collection of abandoned texts that PoI-263's intentions and inspiration for the production of anomalous artifacts was informed by his theology, which sought to elucidate the oneness between nature/the divine and the human body. Thus, the Order of St. John the Hesychast was recruited by the Auctoritas Impertus in an attempt to intervene via less reactive, and more proactive means. 

The following is a written account of a declassified conversation regarding this proposition:
[[collapsible show="[+] Display Record" hide="[-] Collapse"]]
> //Then High-Priest Parish Locksley visited Abbot Tooke on the slopes of Mt. Vesuvius on the morning of May 26, 1514. Accompanying them was an Auctoritas Impertus scribe. The transcription has been digitized for posterity and is reproduced below.// [[size 80%]]**(Department of Prints and Drawings, Reference 228.)**[[/size]]
> 
> ------
> 
> **Tooke:** I never mind and would ask for a counterfeit, so that we may keep a record hither.  What endues thou to our slopes?
> 
> **Locksley:** Doth thou know of the man bid Da Vinci?
> 
> **Tooke:** I never.  Should'st I?
> 
> **Locksley:** I might be surprised were thy devoutness to hermetics not so steadfast. His discretion hath meant him much fame. This is a problem that we suffer; in pride, spirit, reputation,  now recruitment. Da Vinci makes machinations that siphon the very essence of His creative breath — whe'r by divinity or devilry we never know, nor mind.  All we know is that we are outmatched by his cunning, time and time yet, and that he revels in such play, as were we sport!
> 
> **Tooke:** Then what could a peaceful order of tome-keepers and wine-pressers do to help thee?
> 
> **Locksley:** Ensured in his writings, he shares many a conviction with thy Order. This theological kinship may afford us some leveraging and it may yet afford your Order some needed innovation. Our augurists[[footnote]]A religious authority who interpreted signs -- particularly in the conduct of birds, the configuration of tea leaves, or the study of entrails -- deciphering these as divine signals.[[/footnote]] say that the Mount is due for a feverish spell. Could not this inventor find ways to protect thy home? No need to suffer the set-backs each time the Mount belches its ash. 
> 
> **Tooke:** The mount hath remained quiet for 14 years, and even that one a bawbling. And o'er a century’s time 'ere that. The soil is watered and vinery adorns the rock faces.  Our auspices see that the Mount is quieted and the Lord hath favor on our piety.  Thou shalt do more in pursuance of our aid.
> 
> **Locksley:** If the Auctoritas Impertus can warrant thy order’s continuation beyond the lives of thy monks and the next generation’s and the next’s... wouldst we strike a pact?
> 
> **Tooke:** Tis a thought. What wouldst thou hast us do?
> 
> **Locksley:** Convince him to compose his machinations on holy ground.  Thy land is sacred — he knows his work is defiled by the hands of the unwashed, there in the towns. 'Tis no place for such sorcery and the people are filled with a fervor for him that draws the eyes of God’s jealous wrath.  Commissions of vain and bloodthirsty Princes, his skill to the highest payer... this is no place for one of his abilities, especially would they be unclean. He shall wish to devote himself to //his// work. He has a passion, we know it from his writings -- to elevate man. Thou offer this aid in thy way of life.  Feed him, clothe him. Survey his heart and do what thou might. This is thy charge, and only thou are capable. Art thou not the keepers of the hallowed? And responsible for the segregation of that what be holy from that what be not?
> 
> **Tooke:** Aye, we are. 
> 
> **Locksley:** Then prithee; give this man respite in the name of the Lord. For his sake, our sake, and yours. 
> 
> //(Fermata.)//[[footnote]]Literally “stop”; the native scribe’s way of indicating a pause.[[/footnote]]
> 
> **Tooke:** Our continuation, says thou? As long as thou exist, so we shall?
> 
> **Locksley:** Thou hast mine most sincere oath. //(PL produces a knife and presses his finger against the apex, blood streaking his outstretched palm.)//
> 
> //(Fermata.)//
> 
> **Tooke:** We shall grant this, surely a man of much thought, the quiet contemplation his work may merit. And if it is that he devotes his life to conjuring devils, we shall grant him Hell-on-Earth.
> 
> //(The two clasp arms and a holy bond is forged.)//

The Auctoritas Impertus was not granted co-management of PoI-263 upon his matriculation into the Order, as this stipulation was not specified in the pact, nor was it spiritually justified per the monks' code of ethics. Every decade, the Authority sent a representative to renegotiate[[footnote]]The Order cycled leadership every ten years.[[/footnote]] with the interest of establishing more thorough containment for PoI-263, and also to catalogue any additional anomalous inventions. The subsequent activities, health, and lifespan of PoI-263 remain unverified.

-----

**Addendum 263.1:** In the year 1519, accounts submitted by agents in proximity to Mount Vesuvius recorded disturbing seismic activity. Analysis demonstrated a 400% increase in such activity from the year prior. Efforts to understand the sudden up-regulation of this activity were unsuccessful.

-----

**Addendum 263.2: Interview Record** - In 1629, the sole remaining member of the Order of St. John the Hesychast presented to the Auctoritas Impertus's 3rd Archdiocese seeking asylum. This individual would not reveal his name "for shame of speaking it".

His conversation with the Archbishop was written down and catalogued. 
[[collapsible show="[+] Display Record" hide="Collapse"]]

> //The following conversation between the Archbishop Frederick Kyrie of the Third Archdiocese and the unnamed member of the Order of St. John the Hesychast (here “Unknown”) was recorded by an Auctoritas Impertus court scribe. It has been digitized for posterity and is reproduced below://  [[size 80%]]**(Department of Prints and Drawings, Reference 395.)**[[/size]]
> 
> -----
> 
> **Unknown:** You must help us! We have made a terrible mistake.
> 
> **Kyrie:** Patience. Let's start at the beginning; what ever became of Leonardo Da Vinci?
> 
> **Unknown:** Brother Da Vinci. He welcomed us; welcomed the opportunity to deny the system of patronage. He felt enslaved by it, like the world of humanly concern was nothing more than a nuisance. We gave him food, shelter, brotherhood, rank... even a separate hermitage for continuous contemplation. 
> 
> **Kyrie:** And did he create?
> 
> **Unknown:** Oh yes. He created a great many things. A vast shield which spared the monastery harm from the Mount's fires; automatic wine presses; tools for refining the wine to a brilliant condition; machines of recreation... he simultaneously resolved our need for hardship in survival and fulfilled our new spare time with entertainment. He was very popular.
> 
> **Kyrie:** How was he able to make all of this?
> 
> **Unknown:** I do not know. It was divinity at work; this I can tell you. I believe it was the Mount's sacredness and its fire -- one and the same, I can tell you.
> 
> **Kyrie:** What happened to him?
> 
> **Unknown:** We... do not know that either. He vanished, sometime in the year of our Lord, fifteen-hundred and nineteen. We wondered if he gave himself to the Mount, or if he ascended into heaven. 
> 
> **Kyrie:** Perhaps he descended into hell.
> 
> **Unknown:** No. No, Da Vinci was wholly divine. The thoughts of God occupied his every moment. He had been perfecting the demonstration of divinity in human form. Last I saw, he was working in sculpture. That was his most intense work, his last. He rarely left the forge.
> 
> **Kyrie:** Forge?
> 
> **Unknown:** He was granted access to the Crypt; a most active pit of the Mount's fire -- said to be the heart of God. He worked there. Lived there.
> 
> **Kyrie:** Let's move on from Brother Da Vinci for now; what has become of your Order?
> 
> **Unknown:** I am the last.
> 
> **Kyrie:** How?
> 
> **Unknown:** A terrible, terrible mistake. The newer generation of monks did not have the wisdom of the elders, God rest their souls. Brother Micah... 
> 
> //(The individual gestures a cross upon his chest and kisses his thumb.)//
> 
> **Unknown:** We took advantage of Brother Da Vinci's works. Lord forgive me. We traveled to the outlying towns and sold them for great profit. Back to the Princes that had noticed Brother Da Vinci's leave. We returned home with riches and made the monastery our... harem.
> 
> **Kyrie:** What did Da Vinci think of this?
> 
> **Unknown:** He made no comment and did not participate, that much I know. He lived his work.
> 
> **Kyrie:** Did your order sin itself to death?
> 
> **Unknown:** No. I... I am torn as to what is right your Grace. Maybe it is because I have been so far away from the Light of God that I do not know how to look for it any longer and ma--
> 
> **Kyrie:** Look at me, child. I am your Light of God. Confess to me what you have to say so that the mercy of the Lord might find favor with you.
> 
> **Unknown:** Brother Da Vinci had strict burial rites in place. He requested them of us in documents. We could not find his body and so we did as he bid with a creation of his. Understand, he was such a beloved member of the Order, we owed our entire lifestyle to him and his work. So we did as he had requested. When he was gone, we pushed his beloved sculpture into the heart of Vesuvius. It rolled in and sank, but it did not melt. It glowed incandescent, and suddenly, the form of the man before my eyes became as two. I saw the very image of St. John the Silent himself! It was him! Speaking with God Himself as he had done so within the wilderness. With arms outstretched welcoming obscurity and isolation as a treasure, he was crucified therein with Brother Da Vinci in the orb he had built. I felt as though the surroundings sank; and all I could intake were the eyes of that man. St. John's anger upon our souls was hotter than that fire, this I can tell you. My brothers and I ran from the Crypt and hid in shame; we dared not enter it again.
> 
> **Kyrie:** How does this lead to your Order's decline?
> 
> **Unknown:** After that, the Mount became violent. Groans of God shook the monastery and the blood of the mountain bubbled like water. Under this attrition, we of the Order felt great shame for what we had become. As penance, we retraced our sales of Brother Da Vinci's creations and brought them back to our holds, but that wasn't enough to quell the anger within Vesuvius. The shaking became more frequent, and stronger. The rending of the walls entered our bones and we could not sleep. The lava shifted the ground beneath our feet and spilled tongues of molten rock into our bedchambers, devouring our maidens and riches. It was a plague of the Lord, this I can tell you.
> 
> **Kyrie:** Did the volcano's awakening kill your Brothers?
> 
> **Unknown:** No. They took themselves in shame and hope. Sacrificed in recompense for our actions, in an effort to spare the surrounding towns the fate we alone justly deserved.
> 
> **Kyrie:** What could explain such guilt as to welcome madness?
> 
> **Unknown:** A curious thing; we felt as though Vesuvius was angry with us for our elevation of Brother Da Vinci over the holy dictums of the Order. The heart of the mountain was holy. No flesh was allowed near it for centuries, yet we let him use it for his inventions. So naturally, we felt a reversal was correct. We began putting Brother Da Vinci's machines to the fires, and we were right! It worked; the eruptions ceased! The bellowing slowed and the molten rock hardened. The volcano was appeased. We were left with nothing but our sleep, and it was so sweet.
> 
> **Kyrie:** How long has this gone on?
> 
> **Unknown:** ... Brother Da Vinci's serums had granted us unnatural ages and Vesuvius was only demanding a couple of times in, say, a year or two. We were able to sustain relative peace for many, many years. 
> 
> **Kyrie:** But then you ran out of inventions?
> 
> **Unknown:** We didn't know what else to do. Out of desperation, we... //(cries)//... we pushed him into the fire. Brother Micah, we... we didn't give him a decision. We were out of sacrifices! It was supposed to happen that way, Brother Thonia sai--... it was the right thing to do! We had to for the people near the slopes! 
> 
> **Kyrie:** Calm thyself, child.
> 
> **Unknown:** I... I was the only one left. I stood above the fires and they ate into my soul well before my skin. I couldn't bring myself to do it. Not for cowardice, trust me! If I didn't try to do something, try to come here... there would be nothing left after me. Please. You have to help. We have forgotten our way of life at a terrible cost. The Mount has splintered its century-long veil of silence and it now knows the taste of the //divine//. It will have more, or taste blood instead.
> 
> **Kyrie:** ... I am not sure how it is we are supposed to help you, child. There is no other Da Vinci, and we are glad of it. Your sins are never your own, this is scripture. If your Order has decided to extend themselves into your neighboring communities for the purposes of material gain and extravagant sinfulness, then you have doomed them to the justice for that sin. The Lord wills that you have this malady. Do not fret, for in your suffering, you will find peace; you will find the fires of the Lord. The bishops will take you to lodge and food now. Then it is back to your home with you. Do what you must there, and we will weigh the severity of your claims while God weighs that of your soul.

**Addendum 263.3:** Approximately two years after the above interview took place (1631), Mount Vesuvius experienced a massive eruption. The force generated from the explosion abbreviated the day by several millionths of a second and moved the North Pole by 2.5 cm. Numerous towns were covered under magma streams, killing around 3,000 individuals. Emissions from this eruption were so extensive that the entirety of southern Europe was covered by the debris, and Vesuvian ash was discovered in Constantinople later that year (1,200 km away). 

In the following months, hundreds of individual fauna and persons, from unaffected towns as far away as 1,000 km, were observed congregating upon the volcano. Those that survived the climb to the central peak then invariably hurled themselves into the maw of the volcano, and presumably to their deaths in the magma below.

This eruption marked the onset of a new stage of activity from Mount Vesuvius; eruptive behavior became near-constant. By 1636, the Auctoritas Impertus had begun enacting OPERATION WHITECLOAK, and subsequent eruptions (1660, 1682, 1694, 1698) were notably more contained with minimal damage and no significant loss of life.
