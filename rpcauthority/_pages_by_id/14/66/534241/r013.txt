**Containment Protocols:** RPC-922 is currently held at Housing Unit .C922 (HU.C992) together with five CSD personnel. The unit is positioned on the outskirts of Site-052, and must be inspected bimonthly for damage. As RPC-922 has historically proven to be efficient in evading capture and observation, HU.C922 lockdown must not be deactivated. Physical access to the entity is therefore impossible, and the CSD personnel used as the initial lure remain restrained to their beds.

Methods of extracting Authority employees are undergoing development, and assigned personnel are encouraged to submit proposals to Research and Containment Head Dr. Lyncon D'lor.

**Description:** RPC-922 is a chimeric entity of humanoid and avian anatomy which caused a phenomenon termed as "Sleep Stalker Syndrome" by sleep researchers and clinicians. The structures superior to the coracoid belong to that of a //Strix Hadorami//, or the desert owl, in opposition with the body of a human male. The entity is dressed in a light blue buttoned shirt tucked into black pants. Chaining the arms together at the wrists are tarnished silver shackles.[[footnote]]It is currently impossible to enforce the humanoid entity dress code.[[/footnote]] The far side of the moon is visible on the cornea of RPC-922’s eyes. It is a dynamic visual, as it corresponds to the moon's current libration.

**Xepimethology[[footnote]]From //Xenos// and //Epimetheus// (literal meaning being "strange afterthought"), the study of Xepimethology is best described as unveiled historical observations with regard to possible anomalous influences and practices. The term was coined by the ancient Greek historian Thucydides to accuse the contemporaneous historian Herodotus' work of including made-up stories for entertainment purposes. Later consideration by the History Department proved much of the criticized work to be true, popularizing the moniker.[[/footnote]] request from Dr. Lyncon D’lor:** RPC-922 is similar to a divine figure originating from northern Egypt, west of the Canopic branch of the Nile river. More specifically, the village of Kom Gi’eif, a modern day archeological site. The site is of a labour camp first founded by Ptolemy I Soter, and continued until the end of the Ptolemaic Kingdom. I have asked our xepimethologists to divert resources to the study of the location. I have well-founded suspicions it will teach us much regarding RPC-922. The following is a resource authored by egyptologist Nailah Bennu, which is the basis of my request.

[[collapsible show="[+] ['Minor Religions of the Ptolemaic Labor Camps' excerpt]" hide="[-] ['Minor Religions of the Ptolemaic Labor Camps' excerpt]"]]
> The size of this particular camp is of special interest, as records prove it to be arguably the largest, producing multiple times the necessary amount to satiate the people of Naucratis. This is easily noticeable in the soil, which shows signs of heavy agricultural use. Historians still debate on the details of the efficiency, as the sheer quantity would cause exhaustion-related permanent injury, incompatible with the consistent high production throughout the long life of the camp. What is agreed upon however, is that the people of the Gi’eif labour camp performed egregious work, more brutal than contemporaneous instances of slavery.
> 
> This is reflected in the confined people’s culture and religion. In combination with the isolation from other native Egyptian societies, the grueling work of the desert heat juxtaposed with the solace of the night resulted in a unique worldview, even in the wider Greek-dominated world of the Hellenistic period.
> 
> A mural on the Gi’eif temple illustrates this best. It demonstrates a common ritual of worship, and what was desired from the deity in return.
> 
> Firstly, the temple itself is of an open design, with corner pillars holding up a ring-shaped platform above. The only wall of the temple lies between the easternmost pillars, where the illustration is carved. On the bottom, are multiple people lying down facing the sky. This group sleeping was a normal ritual of the Gi’eif slaves, as the area was utilized for plantations, not living space. Above the sleeping individuals is the deity, portrayed with the head of an ibis and arms chained. Curiously, the deity wears a shanti but not accessories of royalty, such as a khat or nemes, despite divinity and kingship being closely related in ancient Egyptian religion. The deity is pulling its chained arms apart, almost like one would a string from a bow, while directing up towards a white circular object. On the close face is an arrow piercing inwards.
> 
> Other preserved records of this religion can be used to understand the role of the deity, who is known as Artshu. Artshu is a Greco-Egyptian syncretic combination of two other figures, those of Khonshu and Artemis. Similarities include both being personifications of the moon, with Artemis occasionally being identified with the moon goddess Selene. Both figures are additionally associated with healing and curing of disease. This is seen on the mural as Artshu causing the healing nature of sleep, by extracting a form of evil spirit or nightmare, and banishing it to the moon. By sleeping in groups, the slaves of Gi’eif hoped to invoke Artshu to comfort their nights and refurbish their bodies for the exhausting work to come.
> 
> Close inspection of Artshu’s portrayal reveals major themes of the religion. The simple clothing symbolizes a decreased distance between the divine and the peasantry, resulting in an antagonistic perspective on the upper echelons of society, as the concept of good has been concentrated downwards in the social hierarchy. Stories from for example a canopic jar, detail the significance of the chains. Myth pertains that they are stiff during the day, but become elastic at night, much like the situation of the Gi’eif slaves. There is also a more practical purpose, as the elasticity of the chains would allow Artshu to utilize them as a form of sling-shot or bow, to propel the nightmares towards the cosmos.
> 
> Additionally, the Gi’eif labor camp had a particularly unique perspective on the sun’s cosmological role. In place of a generous life giver, or an innocent observer, the sun was heavily correlated with domination, oppression, and machiavellianism. Certain sources explain how the sun is a sadistic force, one with the aim of subjugating denizens of the earth. This would be accomplished through two ways: Firstly, the obvious association of day with toil. Secondly, and more interestingly, through manipulation. By organizing the conditions of life on earth to depend on the energy of solar rays, the sun was able to toy with the planet without risk of rebellion.
>  
> Supporting this theory are commonly shared prophecies. For example: Stories speak of a destined moment, known as the “Birth of The Second Sun”. On this day, Artshu would lead a battle against the sun, in hopes of toppling its hold on earth and usurping the rule. Different endings describe different fates. Either Artshu would emerge victorious, and engulf the earth in glorious night, or the sun would further enforce its power, and eliminate the few hours humanity is safe from it by replicating itself.
> 
> It is unknown how much of the religion survived after the end of the Ptolemaic dynasty, as records from the time of Ptolemy X Alexander I suggest that after losing control of the southern kingdom due to Egyptian revolts, the camp was massacred in fear of the incident repeating, therefore causing the loss of much valuable historical evidence.

[[collapsible show="[+] [RPC-922 Xepimethology]" hide="[-] [RPC-922 Xepimethology]"]]
Xepimethologists focused on the best preserved evidence; the records of the Ptolemaic pharaohs. A letter from Ptolemy X Alexander I to what is assumed to be one of his wives showed the pharaoh’s frustration at the results of the Gi’eif raid. It detailed how the raid occurred late at day, in order for exhaustion to have weakened the slaves. The intended outcome was capture of a group of individuals to use in intimidation against the other labour camps. This group had however escaped after sunset, and soldier testimonies attest to the darkness of the night being a contributing factor.

Further study uncovered traces of habitation 3 miles northwest from the archeological site, buried underground. Among massive collections of woven linen — most likely used as sleeping mats — multiple human bones were found. Bioarchaeological analysis determined the presence of at least three distinct individuals; one adult male, one adult female, and one prepubescent female. Only the bone set of the young female was able to be completed, indicating that she was the only one buried. Dating placed the time of death to be around the fall of the Gi’eif camp. Additionally, there was heavy damage in the distal radius, femur, mandible and numerous fractures of the costae.

Most interestingly, a small box was found together with the young female’s skeleton. It contained a script written in demotic Egyptian. Translation determined it to be a form of prayer text. It is unknown whether the original owners were literate, as such ability would've been uncommon among the Gi’eif labourers. A digitalized version is attached.

> Formula for healing of the broken.

> They are weaved shut by Artshu,

> Ra comes back to open the cracks again.
> The cycle weighs the scale even.
> But Ra's wrath swelters,
> his son is brought to burn me double.
> Artshu will cover and extinguish his fire.
> I am a friend of Artshu,
> who will keep me safe from Ra.
> Until the day of liberation.
> Until the death of the Sun and its Son. 

South of Kom Gi’eif, xepimethologists uncovered a collection of large bronze discs, slightly curved to form flattened lenses. So far, 15 of such discs have been found sprawled in a complex pattern. It is noted how the discs are similar both in shape and composition to ancient Egyptian bronze mirrors, but the intention of the array remains uncertain.

The team approach ALKoG's northern entrance, a 3 meter high gate. There is an inscription on the sandstone around the opening. SEEK translates it to “PURE EYES ARE HELD BY THE HOUSE OF RADIANCE".

The end of the hallway widens into a large auditorium. Stone seats stretch along the width, oriented towards a stage to the south. In the middle of the room is a 5 meter diameter sphere. There is a repeated inscription on it, SEEK claims the images to to be the Egyptian “sun disc.” Additionally, a snake statue rests on top of the sphere, claimed to be the “Uraeus”, a symbol of royalty, power and dominion. BOOK asks if the others can hear a noise, described as a faint, rhythmic whispering. Neither ARID or SEEK do. 

Around the sphere the ground descends in a cone. On the bottom is a cuboid cage holding up the sphere. On the northern side, 4 of the thick stone bars are broken. ARID notes two things inside the cage; bird feathers, and scrambled inscriptions on the floor. SEEK takes a picture of the writings.

The stage in the southern part of the auditorium has an inscription on its front. “DARK EYES ARE BURNED BY THE HOUSE OF RADIANCE.” On top of the stage is a golden throne. SEEK claims these to be common seats of pharaohs, and that the specific build of this one belongs to the Ptolemaic dynasty. BOOK praises the architecture of the roof’s holes, as the light is guided to focus on the stage. 

ARID asks SEEK for an interpretation of the illustrations on the throne. During this time, BOOK ascends the stairs to the stage and centers himself on it. SEEK explains how the pharaohs were considered man's link with the divine, and the path of the gods’ wisdom. ARID suggests demolition of ALKoG before the contract expires. SEEK protests. BOOK is asked for his opinion, but does not answer.

ARID grows frustrated at BOOK’s silence, and demands BOOK to descend from the stage. BOOK begins vocalizing a low tone which echoes throughout the auditorium as it is amplified. The light from the holes begins shifting, and localizes on BOOK. BOOK begins chanting the following:

> [[=]]
> KEEPERS AND LOVERS OF LIGHT AND RADIANCE
> 
> LET YOUR PURE EYES SWEAT IN AWE
> 
> LET YOUR HOLY THROAT RESONATE IN PRAYER
> 
> LET YOUR TRUE BONES MARCH IN DEFENSE
> 
> AGAINST THE COWARDS AND PEASANTS OF SHADOW AND UMBRA
> 
> LET THEIR DARK EYES BOIL IN FEAR
> 
> LET THEIR VILE THROAT SHATTER IN SCREAMS
> 
> LET THEIR HOLLOW BONES BREAK IN LABOR
> 
> UNTIL DARKNESS DIES
> 
> UNTIL LIGHT GLARES AT ALL
> 
> UNTIL THE COMING OF THE SECOND SUN
> [[/=]]

ARID tackles BOOK off the stage, who regains awareness. The auditorium is no longer silent, for a chant can be heard from multiple disembodied voices resonating throughout, repeating the phrase “Until the coming of the Second Sun.”

SEEK begins retreating out of the ALKoG, but is stopped by BOOK. BOOK requests the diagram of the bronze discs that previously occupied the ALKoG space. Upon retrieving it, BOOK commands the other team members to shed their clothes and put them over the listed locations. SEEK and ARID initially protest, but BOOK continues regardless, after which they comply. 

Upon covering 7 of the locations, ALKoG vanishes and the team is back in the dessert and half naked. BOOK orders a better veil over the discs, “before the world brightens more.”
