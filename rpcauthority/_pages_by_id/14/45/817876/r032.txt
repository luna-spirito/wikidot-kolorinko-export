**Registered Phenomena Code:** RPC-354

**Object Class:** Omega-Orange

[[include component:hazards top-spacing=none|sapient=true]]

**Containment Protocols:** Due to the wildlife conservation policies of Botswana, containment of RPC-354 is limited to halting the spread of information regarding the species. An undisclosed annual monetary transfer is to be paid to Botswana's current administration in order to ensure cooperation with the Authority in the containment of RPC-354 in its natural habitat.

**Description:** RPC-354 is a species of primarily bipedal apes originating from the Ghanzi district of Botswana. While the properties of the species have only recently come to the knowledge of the Authority, it is known that locals of the area were well aware of the existence of RPC-354 since the late 1800s, referring to them as "trash-feeders."

RPC-354 is notable for its usage of human waste. It has been observed utilizing metal framings to construct small houses with an unexpected amount of intricacy in their design. 

No two houses built by RPC-354 will have been the same, and appear to have components built for artistic value rather than to serve an objective purpose. While in the past RPC-354 would use driftwood to construct their houses, a recent cultural advancement caused a transition to metal framing instead.

Works of art produced by RPC-354 indicate a level of awareness of their environment. They commonly produce renditions of African wildlife[[footnote]]Elephants, lions, and antelope most frequently. It should be noted that they generally seem to avoid creating renditions of chimpanzees.[[/footnote]] with a less frequent occurrence of abstract art comprised of geometric shapes, displaying a degree of abstract thinking. As of the Authority's initial contact with RPC-354, they have been observed creating artwork of human beings.

[[include component:image-block name=http://rpcauthority.wikidot.com/local--files/rpc-354/IMG_4199.jpg|caption= A human artwork produced by RPC-354, thought to be a recreation of Researcher Kleiner.]]

New technologies and materials were introduced to the locals and RPC-354 with the colonization of Botswana in 1885 as the materials were discarded. An expedition log recovered in 198█ documents an interaction between a duo of British explorers and RPC-354 wherein an instance of the latter was observed using a discarded flintlock musket, presumably to defend its territory from a poacher.

RPC-354 instances display low levels of sapience. One specimen extracted by the Authority for research purposes with permission from the Ghanzi wildlife conservation society displayed a notably advanced degree of pattern recognition during testing, which is thought to be equivalent to that of a preadolescent. The specimen spent the first 12 hours attempting to escape from the chamber before cooperating, and displayed obvious discomfort during testing thereafter.

How RPC-354 obtained such a high level of intelligence is not currently known. It is theorized that this could have been the natural result of evolution, suggesting that more animals may transition to a lifestyle of bottom-feeding off of human societies in the future. However, research has indicated that the genetic profile of RPC-354 is unique in its composition, with only a ≈53.4% similarity to the human genome. The rest of its genetic profile appears to be composed of a previously unknown chemical alternative to DNA. More research is required to discern the properties of this chemical.

> George Frederick Samuel Robinson, 1st Marquess of Ripon, Secretary of State for the Colonies
> Colonial Office
> 14 Downing Street
> Whitehall
> London
> United Kingdom
> 
> 27th September, 1894
> 
> It is of my deepest regrets that I have not written to you sooner. Nearly a decade has passed since my venture into Bechuanaland, and in the years since, I have struggled with a profound sense of dread, as well as having lost many of those close to me. As I fear I may be institutionalized within the coming weeks for my affiliation with the topic at hand, it has been made a matter I feel I must bring to your attention.
> 
> It was two years after the Bechuanaland Protectorate was established when I, along with Sir Thomas Weatherby, were personally requested by your predecessor Frederick Stanley, Earl of Derby to tour the newly colonized region. The first weeks of the expedition were a matter of controlling and interacting with the local populace, as well as to conduct what scientific research we could. The locals were admittedly clever. Their domiciles, while shoddy in construction, provided a cool hospice from the blazing hot rays of the sun. 
> 
> The locals who had acquainted themselves with our language entertained us with tales of gods, monsters, and spirits, as well as legends of crafty and intelligent beings who stole their waste. To the locals, all of these beings were real, and walked the land alongside them. Sir Thomas and I discarded the claims as mere tall tales brewed up in the minds of uncivilized peoples.
> 
> It would be days later, however, when a curious event occurred. As my partner and I slept, we were awoken by the sound of gunfire. Fearing that the locals had obtained weaponry, we gathered our rifles and ran to where the shot had rang out. When we reached a watering hole, we witnessed a most perplexing and disturbing sight.
> 
> Before us lay a man, lifeless, blood seeping from an entry wound in his chest. A large blade lay centimeters away from his outstretched hand. Across the body of water, a strange figure stood tall with a rifle in hand. The creature's appearance deeply unsettles me even merely upon recalling it. By all accounts, this was a person. It stood upright, unlike any animal I've ever known. The eyes of the beast were inverted from a human's with black sclera and white pupils, and yet they returned my gaze as a man's would. It was covered head to toe in thick, coarse fur, however, what most perplexed me was that it appeared to be in as much shock and confusion as we, clearly trembling before us. As my partner and I watched, the creature's hands shook most terribly as it stared at the lifeless man. It would then sling the rifle over it's shoulder, running off into the night. 
> 
> Sir Thomas and I, upon witnessing this, made the decision not to pursue the creature at risk of our own lives. We completed our duties in the territory, gathering as much information as we could about the beings, though the locals were less keen to cooperate with us after the event, having assumed the murder was of our doing. After we had returned from the colony, I made the choice to retire from my employment under the Colonial Office, and have since lost contact with Sir Thomas.
> 
> Through my further studies into reports of mysterious, perplexing, or otherwise anomalous species, I have been made aware of the existence of a new agency under the throne which deals in matters of the paranormal.[[footnote]]Thought to be referring to Monarch Security.[[/footnote]] As my requests for the classification of a new species have been denied and ridiculed by the larger scientific community, it is my wish that this phenomenon be brought to the attention of and investigated by this department. I have attached an illustrative depiction of the being, as I remember it from that most dreadful night.
> 
> With the utmost respect and humility,
> 
> Sir Robert Winchcombe[[footnote]]English explorer known for documenting various plant and animal species in colonial Botswana. 
Died of sepsis in 1897 at the Middlesex County Lunatic Asylum, now St. Bernard's Hospital.[[/footnote]]
