**Containment Protocols:**

[[tabview]]
[[tab Original]]

RPC-230 entities are to be contained in Terrestrial Containment Chamber #032 in the Beta Sector of Site-002. The interior of the containment chamber is to simulate a forested area with the climate control system set to maintain a dry and arid environment and to contain an in-ground water source.

RPC-230 instances are to be fed a steady omnivorous diet of raw meat and desert foliage. Standard pre-feed screening protocols for foodborne illnesses are in place.

Maintenance personnel are to inspect the containment chamber biweekly for any damages or abnormalities. Healthkeeping personnel are to check on the entities once a month and are authorized to adjust the diet of individual entities.

In the case of a containment breach, containment personnel are to attempt entity recontainment utilizing non-lethal means. Lethal force may only be imposed by the approval of the acting site director.

A five-year ban on experimentation with RPC-230 instances is in place, to be released on 21/02/2007, or until the confirmation of the entities' ample reproductive capabilities. Contact the current attending head researcher for more information.

[[/tab]]
[[tab Revised]]

RPC-230 entities are to be kept in a secure off-site containment facility 30km southwest of Site-002, codenamed 'Sugar.' The living habitat containing RPC-230 must be maintained to simulate the forested semi-arid environment of the surrounding mountain range and contain an ample source of water.

The containment chamber of RPC-230 and its surrounding areas are situated atop radioactive soil in order to assist the entities' autophagy mechanism. Visiting and attending personnel must undergo a mandatory HAZMAT safety course prior to entering the containment area.

Instances of RPC-230 are to be fed a steady omnivorous diet of the flower and de-spined flesh of barrel cacti, meat of whitetail deer, and/or several other flora and fauna native to the state of Nevada and the surrounding Mojave Desert. Contact the attending head researcher for information on dietary restrictions.

Essential personnel (containment, medical, and maintenance) are to be housed in a permanent operational camp 20km south of the containment site codenamed 'Mercury'. Daily personnel shifts are to be rotated every 6 hours, with the duration of personnel deployment being 90 days.

In the case of a containment breach, all non-essential personnel are to enter the secure panic chamber in a calm and orderly manner. Containing personnel are to attempt entity recontainment utilizing non-lethal means. Lethal force may be deployed per the approval of the attending head researcher.

**Description:**

RPC-230 is the collective designation for a group of bipedal humanoid entities, measuring approximately 175cm tall in adult form. Instances possess pale, leathery skin, and lack forelimbs. In addition, specimens have a unified body and head with no neck, and a pair of three-jointed digitigrade legs. Individual designations are displayed as RPC-230-1 through RPC-230-34 (refer to the inventory list for more details).

The pale coloring in the skins of RPC-230 entities is due to oculocutaneous albinism mutation present in all instances. --It is currently unknown whether RPC-230 instances are inherently albino, or if this was caused by a singular mutation that is made chronic due to uncontrolled inbreeding.--[[footnote]]See attachment 230/03.[[/footnote]] Due to their inherent lack of defense against ultraviolet light, RPC-230 instances have adopted a nocturnal circadian cycle.

The unified upper body of RPC-230 entities exhibits several extensive calcified protrusions emerging from either flank, in the front three-quarters of their bodies, nearing the mouth at the front. --The function of these protrusions is still debated among Authority biologists.--[[footnote]]See attachment 230/01 below.[[/footnote]]

**ID:** 230/01

**Log Date:** 21/08/2003
**Researching Personnel:** Dr. Robert Hanson, M.D

----

Identifying the purpose of the bony protrusions on RPC-230 instances has proved to be a challenging endeavor, to say the least. Although determining the physiognomy of these creatures is nigh impossible, the top brass still hasn't given me the go-ahead to take a biopsy sample.

Nevertheless, if we cross-reference the skeletal structure of most mammals out there—which I am positively sure that RPC-230 can be classified as one—we can start to see what seems to be a slight disconnect between the head and the rest of their bodies.

The connection between the heart and the brain is, without a doubt, the most critical connection between every vertebrate. The heart pumps blood to oxygenate the brain, and the nervous system extends down the spine and throughout the entire body to allow the body to move. These creatures do have necks and, with them, jugular arteries.

These bony protrusions seem to bridge the gap between the head and the rest of the body, essentially forming a "pseudo-neck," and the function of that protrusion is quite simple: It protects the jugular arteries from external trauma.

Might I say though, that these bony protrusions seem awfully similar to those found in human skeletons. Namely, the three primary protrusions extending from the snout of the creatures, appear to be the humerus, ulna, and radius bones found in a human arm.

I would like to propose a new theory on the origins of RPC-230 and to pursue said theory by inspecting the creatures hands-on. Alas, with the research limitations imposed by my supervisors, all of this remains a mere theory.

[[/div]]
[[/collapsible]]

RPC-230 instances exhibit an omnivorous diet and are documented to have consumed a plethora of flora in the lush environment of Mt. Charleston. Specimens are known to have hunted down desert lizards, small birds, and even large terrestrial mammals such as deer and sheep.

RPC-230 instances also exhibit docile behavior; they do not see humans or other predatory entities (presented as still images) as a threat unless provoked. It is unknown whether this behavior is an inherent trait or whether RPC-230 instances have had no natural predators in the arid environment of the Mojave Desert.

There is no evidence that RPC-230 instances can reproduce in large part due to the lack of sexual organs. Although a few entities seem smaller than the rest, there is no evidence that those smaller entities are the offspring. The lifespan of RPC-230 instances is also currently unknown.

RPC-230 has also shown excellent adaptability and survivability traits and has exhibited a migratory pattern, with a few instances being spotted as far as Fresno, CA, USA in response to a widespread Authority containment operation in their native habitat of Mt. Charleston in Nevada.

Today marks the fifth entity out of a total of thirty-four to have succumbed to this mysterious plague. I, myself, with the help of the maintenance personnel, have ensured optimal living conditions for these creatures. No change in diet, no change in temperature, no breach in containment.

And still, five of them just inexplicably dropped dead. Maybe they have reached the end of their expected lifespans, but if this is a plague, I must cure it. I've tested the water source and feed for foodborne illnesses to no avail. Maybe it's something simpler like a common cold, but in order to find out, I need to dissect one of them.

The biopsy limitation is still in place, and the previous four entities were all sent to be incinerated. Maybe this will be the time that they see I need to do what I do best. I'm saving the fifth corpse—number twenty-nine—in a holding chamber until they come around.

I just hope that they can see, that these creatures hold a sense of beauty they could never comprehend.

**Discovery:**

[[collapsible show="+ [230/03] The Zoology of RPC-230" hide="-hide [230/03] The Zoology of RPC-230"]]
[[div style="border: var(--blockquote-border); color: var(--text-color); background: var(--blockquote-background); border-radius: var(--blockquote-border-radius); padding: var(--blockquote-padding); box-shadow: var(--blockquote-box-shadow); margin: 0px 50px 25px 50px; padding: 3px 20px;"]]

**ID:** 230/03

**Log Date:** 19/05/2004
**Researching Personnel:** Dr. Remy Hadley, M.D.

----

The findings presented within this paper were the result of the dissection of RPC-230-29, the fifth entity to have suddenly died, and was preserved by Dr. Robert Hanson. No exterior ulcers or lacerations were seen on the corpse, and no patches of infected and necrotic tissue were seen on the skin.

I want to preface this report by stating that the theory suggested by the late Dr. Robert Hanson about the origins of RPC-230 was, in fact, true. Electron microscope imaging and extensive blood work of RPC-230-29 tissue found human chromosomes and human hemoglobin, respectively.

The pre-dissection MRI imaging of the entity found significant cellular degradation throughout the body, which was confirmed by the primary dissection of the internal cavity. Several lacerations were seen throughout the entity's internal organs supplanted by abnormal tissue growth.

[[=image http://rpcauthority.wdfiles.com/local--files/rpc-230/230_3.jpg width="450px"]]

Symptoms suggest a typical case of malignant tumor, which may or may not be caused by internal factors such as inherited DNA. However, with the scope of the growth, and as suggested by a theory provided by Dr. Graham Lodge, who assisted me with the dissection, the cause of the tumor growth may have been environmental exposure.

The vast scope of cellular degradation and lacerations found within the internal cavity suggests acute radiation poisoning. However, since the containment chamber was found to be perfectly sterile, even against Radon gasses, Dr. Lodge indicated that it might have been the entity's lack of autophagy that induced the tumorous growths, which ionizing radiation assisted.

Simply said, RPC-230 may have been lacking in the necessary genes to enable natural cellular degradation via autophagy, which ultimately prevented dead tissue from being expelled and thus, forming tumors. With the proximity of RPC-230's natural habitat to the radioactive soil of the Nevada Test Site, Dr. Lodge and I have concluded that RPC-230 entities need natural ionizing radiation to prevent tumors.

Additionally, with the findings presented by the Authority Intelligence Bureau, RPC-230 may have been emitting radiation on its own of an unknown nature that rewrites the DNA of various fauna into its own. This may or may not have been what caused the illness to befall the former attending head researcher, Dr. Robert Hanson.

I suggest inducing a radiation-positive environment in RPC-230's containment chamber and minimizing personnel exposure to the entities.

[[include component:image-block | align=right| width=250px | name=http://rpcauthority.wdfiles.com/local--files/rpc-230/230_2.jpeg| caption=A family of Cactus Springs inhabitants, 1955. Taken by a traveler.]]

In accordance with the findings presented by Dr. Hadley, The Authority now has ample evidence that RPC-230 instances were, in fact, the radiation-induced mutated remains of the former inhabitants of Cactus Springs, NV USA; a now-abandoned silver mining town just north of Mt. Charleston.

In 1957, personnel from the Authority's MST Sierra-8 "Sundowners" Anomaly Department were investigating a series of missing persons reports throughout the state of Nevada. This was later proved to have been the doing of an organized crime in 1959.

However, Sierra-8 personnel also discovered a case of an entire town's inhabitants having seemingly gone missing overnight. This event made its way onto the local newspaper in Las Vegas, written by Rick E. Blaine: a reporter, and a prominent ghost hunter at the time.

In the article, Mr. Blaine stated that the inhabitants of Cactus Springs appeared unnaturally sickly, pale, and unresponsive when he was stopping over at the town for supplies while on his way to Tonopah. The next day, he passed through Cactus Springs again only to find the town completely abandoned, with the people's belongings simply left behind, and no evidence of a struggle or a sudden escape.

This prompted Mr. Blaine to investigate the area extensively and subject the residents of Las Vegas to a fear frenzy. Unbeknownst to Mr. Blaine, the grounds surrounding Cactus Springs were heavily contaminated with radiation following the battery of above-ground nuclear tests in the Nevada Proving Grounds nearby.

Rick E. Blaine later published another article that recounts his encounter with a supposed ghost of a former Cactus Springs inhabitant. Eventually, Mr. Blaine contracted a severe case of acute radiation poisoning which gave him severe haematemesis and made his skin turn pale. These events tripled the ghost scare throughout Las Vegas, which prompted an Authority response to impose a quarantine perimeter around the city.

The Authority found no evidence of anomalous activity surrounding Cactus Springs and chose not to investigate the matters further, and to instead redact the story and amnesticize the residents of Las Vegas. However, Rick E. Blaine was adamant in his accounts to the point that he attempted to escape Authority quarantine to pursue the entities, which ultimately resulted in his termination on 04/06/1957.

In 1989, hikers and rangers in Mt. Charleston started to report sightings of ghosts deep within the forest. With more and more photographic evidence received in the following years, the Authority launched a full-scale containment operation surrounding Mt. Charleston in late 2002. This time, the Authority elected to pre-emptively amnesticize the witnesses to prevent another cryptid hunter incident.

The first RPC-230 instance was taken into Authority custody in early 2003, with the remaining 29 apprehended in the following months. However, 5 instances managed to slip through the containment operation and were spotted in the outskirts of Fresno, CA USA in 2010. They were subsequently apprehended 2 months after the first reported sighting.
