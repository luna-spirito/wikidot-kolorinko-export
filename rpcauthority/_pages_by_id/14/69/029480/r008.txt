[[=]]
+ Introduction
[[/=]]

----

“The Mindsmith of Marks” is the alias attributed to Bogdan Adamchuk, a pioneer of neurosurgery and a close associate of António Egaz Moniz, the father of leucotomy. Moniz attested in his personal diaries how Adamchuk inspired him to “critically question the idea of the sacrosanct.” However, the efficiency of his methods, together with an inability to replicate or empirically understand them, posthumously classed Adamchuk as a psychic. 

Adamchuk was orphaned at 12 years of age during the 1895 Lunchovy Most Madness, an epidemic of violent mass hysteria plaguing the inhabitants of Lunchovy Most village in modern day Belarus. He was discovered by Vladimir Sergeyev, a tax collector under the Russian Empire.

[[collapsible show="[+] Vladimir Sergeyev testimony" hide="[-] Vladimir Sergeyev testimony" hideLocation="both"]]
> 
> "Believe me when I say, Lunchovy Most had no wealth. When I arrived there was nothing, but the stench of perforated corpses dried upon the streets. The peasants have gone mad! They killed each other! I stand witness to this because of the scenes before me. They lay dead over one another, still gripping the pitchforks and sickles that rested inside their compatriots' bodies. I could see old women crushed by horses, with hoofprints over broken bones. Those with the least gruesome death were the children, as they could not resist, and therefore required little force.
> 
> A veil of death whispered to me to leave as soon as I arrived. And I was about to, before hearing someone cry. Life, I thought! The faint sorrow led me to the ashes of a house. Within it sat a boy, tugging at the red-stained cloth of his father, who had stabbed a knife into the heart of a woman I could only assume to be his wife, and received his due back. The boy is in the carriage with me now. He must have been deeply scarred, for he continuously covered the same spot on the top of his head. Suspecting an injury, I moved to inspect it, but he would adamantly refuse me. I sympathize with the poor child, believe me, but when I asked him what happened, he answered only with an empty glance. Whence I left the mad town, a fleeting thought terrorized me: Was the boy a survivor, or a battle’s victor? Then I think back to the young ones for whom a simple slice had sufficed, and I am able to chase the thought away."
> 
> (A report left by Sergeyev to his bureaucratic superior.)
> 
[[/collapsible]]

Adamchuk was admitted to a mental asylum, where he attracted the special attention of the head psychiatrist.[[footnote]]This psychiatrist had later released a paper on the effects of mass hysteria and adolescent trauma, specifically focused on cannibalism of children during the Russian Famine of 1921-1922.[[/footnote]] At this institution, Adamchuk received an impressive education, and university records describe completing his residency in neurology in 1925.

[[=]]
+ Later Pursuits
[[/=]]

----

In 1926, he travelled to the village of Marxstadt (now known as a town named Marks) within Saratov Oblast, on the east riverbank of the Volga river. The Marxstadt populace was one of the most distraught by the Russian Famine, and reduced to only 150~ in number. The depressed population was, with Adamchuk’s treatment, restored, and is now the Marksovsky District’s administrative center.

Details regarding this time are scarce, however an anonymous scholar once described an event involving “The Mindsmith of Marks”.

[[collapsible show="[+] Marxstadt record" hide="[-] Marxstadt record" hideLocation="both"]]
> 
> Radicals are seen in every society, sewing distrust and chaos in the hearts of men. A specimen of such intent was the woodworker Collovsky (who long since has had grievances with the Mindsmith that repaired us from sorrows) who today hastily entered the square, and demanded attention. Appearing to have come from the hill upon which the Mindsmith’s place rests,  he shared a tale I will now, due to my obligation as scholar, share forth: 
> 
> “Listen! Arina (the woodworker's wife) returned yesterday from the Mindsmith’s house (an old church, abandoned by the priest after the sins of the famine). Upon seeing our daughter hungry, a grief had stricken her, and she hurried to the hills to have it treated. Today, I told her these exact words: ‘Make me my favorite.’ And instead of her famous apple pie, she cooked me an egg!”
> 
> “This is not but a lapse in memory. Neither is it the first time. All of you, each time you return from his hole, you have left something behind. You know this! Confront it! The Mindsmith is a godless traitor, a devil who never takes off his hat, lest he bears his horns![[footnote]]Referencing how  Adamchuk was always described wearing a hat.[[/footnote]] I sympathize with you, he cured us of the price of our sins better than any priest, but at what cost?! I never visited him, so I suffer in remembering the taste of my late son’s flesh, but I still live! It is possible!”
> 
> At this moment, one of the audience demanded the woodworker to shut his mouth. But he continued.
> 
> “Today I went to investigate this foreigner, and arrived without an appointment. Listen to me  reveal his dirty secrets. I kicked in the locked door to the chapel, and was greeted by a horrifying sight. Metal sticks and icepicks laid about the benches, saws on the prayer table. The paintings on the wall had faded, and I felt an unholy doom reek in me. As if something was reaching into my head, pushing me to madness, stealing my thoughts, shaking my soul.”
> 
> “I heard a sound, that of a music box, echoing throughout. A solemn tune, but fractured and broken. It invaded me; as if the claws of death itself tapped the rhythm on my wet bones. I proceeded further, into the source of the sound, a room in the back.”
> 
> “As I rounded the corner, my heart beat itself louder, then stopped to imitate my shock. On the walls of that room laid jars on shelves, filled with gray, or pink, or maybe brown meat. Each jar contains but a small fragment. In the middle however, was Juli (owner of the bakery) sitting on a wooden chair. Travelling over and into her eyes were small metal sticks penetrating towards her head. Copper string dangled from the nostrils. Spikes entered a hole bored in the temple, and a rusty metal box was attached to the back of her neck. She had been surrounded, her mind captured on all fronts.”
> 
> “Beside her stood the Mindsmith partly covered by darkness. He was rapidly switching between each metal’s handle, slightly adjusting it. It reminded me of a frantic spider, weaving the  web around its prey. Worst of all, however, was the opening at the top of her skull. Leaking into it, making contact with her exposed brain was a pink, thick, viscous thread; his own tissue. I followed it, and saw how it emerged from a similar hole in the Mindsmith’s head. “
> 
> “Do you understand?! Do not let this sweet devil worm his way into your minds and souls! He will slowly take away all you have, until you become but an empty husk!“
> 
> The audience stood flabbergasted. The woman who broke the silence was Juli, who had just returned from her treatment.
> 
> “You’ve had it in for him ever since he arrived. This is a revelation to none - but you.”
> 
> The woodworker stalled, and turned towards the crowd to say: “Are you insane!?”
> 
> It answered him brutally.
> 
> //“His methods saved us!”//
> 
> //”We’d all be hanging from nooses, or bleeding from our wrists, hadn't it been for the Mindsmith!”//
> 
> //”You are the devil! You are insane!”//
> 
> //”How can you live with it?! How can you live with your own child’s blood on your hands, and their meat in your mouth?! You are a monster!”//
> 
> //”You should be thanking him!”//
> 
> //”Thank him!”//
> 
> He protested, but it was to no avail. The audience had seen the radical, and surrounded him. They feared that by allowing such mockery, the Mindsmith would leave them. The woodworker was pushed down, beaten, and kicked. The crowd chanted together: //”All thank the Mindsmith of Marks! All thank the Mindsmith of Marks!”// At the end of it, his head had become an unrecognizable puddle of pink, white and red.
> 
> This tale teaches us important lessons. When the grace of the greats blesses you, and saves you from misery and despair, do not bite its hand. Sin and guilt will eat you, so accept the brief respite granted to the reprehensible. Indeed, thank the Mindsmith of Marks.
> 
[[/collapsible]]

It is unknown when Adamchuk left Marxstadt, but he met with António Egaz Moniz sometime after.

[[=]]
+ Introduction
[[/=]]

----
Aquarius is the head singer-songwriter of a traveling musical troupe known as “Age of Aquarius with the Rainbow Tail”, commonly abbreviated as AART. He is known to use an eccentric type of thoughtography, inducing color shifts in physical materials when playing music.

He was born in poverty along the western coast, and began playing the blues when as young as 14. The niche periodical “Underground Tunes” portrayed Aquarius as an icon of the Californian ghetto musical scene. However, his career came to an abrupt stop after being rendered completely blind by Leber's Hereditary Optic Neuropathy[[footnote]]A neurological condition causing degredation of the optic nerve and blindness in primarily young men.[[/footnote]] at age 17.

Various paparazzi magazines followed Aquarius after his early retirement. While these sources are untrustworthy, many accused Aquarius of becoming involved in the psychedelic drug trade prevalent within the New Age movements of the time.

In 1964, at age 19, Aquarius made a surprise appearance at a counterculture festival held to raise awareness of sex-based discrimination. This is the earliest confirmed instance of his color-based thoughtography. An attendee later detailed the event in a letter sent to multiple New Age groups:

[[collapsible show="[+] Attendee letter" hide="[-] Attendee letter" hideLocation="both"]]
> 
> … the high point was irrefutably the rebut of Jim Lacks. 3 hours in, everyone’s energy had begun to dwindle. That changed when he appeared on stage, and the crowd cheered before he even uttered a word. He stood still, perfectly silent, and just waited for us to quiet. Then he said words I’ll never forget:
> 
> “You thought it was over?! It was never over. My eyes may be useless, but I can now feel, hear, taste and smell more color than ever! Before me is the red of love, the green of life, and the blue of peace, without the black that follows! (He now presented us with a small tablet, and ate it.) Join me! Drink soma with me! We’ll paint the world with love, life and peace!”
> 
> And after that, he blessed the audience with the most otherworldly and mind-shattering show metaphysically possible. Psychedelic rock doesn’t scratch the surface, it was a mixture of all genres, blended together but still larger than the sum of its parts; it was a painting in auditory. A rainbow was born with that music, which spewed its bright colors all over us, illuminating the entire concert with ##red|LOVE##, ##green|LIFE##, and ##00B4D8|PEACE!## 
> 
[[/collapsible]]

Later investigation found that the punch distributed during the event was laced with lysergic acid diethylamide.

[[=]]
+ Later Pursuits
[[/=]]

----

Aquarius founded AART, originally having 9 members, some time later. It immediately started an “endless” international tour focusing on low-income, developing, and countries with a poor humanitarian score, therefore evading news coverage for most of the time it operated.

However, AART’s international ventures were noted to coincide with the rise of various radical ideologies within these countries. Certain governments began publicly barring AART from performing officially, due to fear of inducing resistance or revolutionary movements. This did not stop Aquarius’ troupe, and they began performing illegally under a new mantle of vigilantism.

The group returned to the United States in 2019. Aquarius, however, was absent. Additionally, AART had grown to over five thousand members. Various interviews revealed AART to have been sustaining itself with supplies from visited locations, as well as recruiting new players along the way. 

One of the drummers detailed his recruitment into the troupe. It has been translated from Tagalog.

[[collapsible show="[+] AART drummer's account" hide="[-] AART drummer's account" hideLocation="both"]]

I felt so alone, I even wished for them to come back, despite my sure demise. They left to enjoy the music without me, I thought.

He said something, I think in English, I don’t remember. But I know what he meant; it was an invitation to drink Soma. And by doing so, I had become of the forever high, of the musicians that usher the Age of Aquarius; a member of the immortal dance. 
> 
> Before I knew it, my restraints were loose. A free man left his deathcell, and entered the town, which had been transformed into a festival. There were cars and other vehicles I seldom saw, spewed about. On top of them were artists, playing songs otherworldly. The entire town had amassed, and danced in between. The elderly danced with the young, the rich with the poor, the cats with the dogs. Yes, yes, everyone danced together.

We knew not the black of despair, only the red, the green, the blue of love, life and peace!

Not much is known of Aquarius after AART, as members of the troupe give contradicting accounts on why or how he disappeared. His work however, remains a staple of Psychedelic and alternative music. It has even influenced the likes of the outwardly anti-psychic [http://rpcauthority.wikidot.com/academy-hub Academy of True Art], as proven in the rise of New Age-related paranormal songs after Aquarius' retirement from the public.

[[=]]
+ Introduction
[[/=]]

----

Estelle Esther was a practitioner of astrological fortune telling from the French city of Baraize. She is credited with returning economic prosperity to the city, which had fallen into dismay during WW2.

Of the few Baraize newspaper issues that survived the collapse, almost all of them feature opinion pieces on Esther. She reportedly made numerous unsolicited predictions regarding the populace, such as: warning of poor financial investments, advising on the outcome of futile educational pursuits, and future domestic misfortunes of newly engaged couples. 

In 1940, a large amount of the city’s masculine youth was drafted to defend the western front. Esther had interrupted a ceremony held to those leaving to participate in the war. An editorial in issue #123 of the Baraize Periodical describes the event:

[[collapsible show="[+] Baraize Periodical #123 excerpt" hide="[-] Baraize Periodical #123 excerpt" hideLocation="both"]]
> 
> As the city reluctantly bade farewell to the brave young men departing to defend our country, a familiar she-devil came to infer chaos and spread fear. Estelle Esther had climbed upon one of the military trucks, and dragged through mud the valiant sacrifices about to be made. 
> 
> She mocked our diligence: “Do not lie to yourself! Your sons are not departing as heroes; but fodder for artillery!”
> 
> She disgraced our fighters: “Listen to me! Your place is high in the stars, not under sickly dirt!"
> 
> Then, she dared to attempt to shake their faith: "Francois, your bright mind will be lost under shocking shells! Raphaël, the last picture you’ll draw is with the blood of your amputation! Gabriel, your silver tongue will rust, and you will return to this city alone and pitiful!”
> 
> We all rightly cursed her, but Estelle refused to listen. Her arrogance is immeasurable, and she thinks of us simple sheep, suited only to worship a young girl’s predictions. Nothing is better proof than her last insult: “You are all idiots! Mindless fools! Your ignorance and stupidity will bring this city to ruin! Baraize will be left empty of life and spirit, its corpse rotting as those you sent to die in the trenches!”
> 
> I say, do not let the rascal plague us any longer. Throw out the seamstress of chaos, we have no need for she-devils.
> 
[[/collapsible]]

A short time after this event, Esther was banished from Baraize. She made her way to Paris, funding the journey by carrying out fortune-readings, and became on of the last to join the French Army’s 25th Medical Unit the 21st of March, 1940.

----

[[=]]
+ Later Pursuits
[[/=]]

----

Reports from her time in the army praise her as a nurse, extremely concerned with the well-being of her patients. Certain sources depict Esther sharing with wounded soldiers the meaning of the night’s horoscope; the constellations and orientations within. It was at this time her peers began referring to her as “Starmother”.

However, her time within the unit was cut short, as she began giving fortunes. She coerced some soldiers to desert with fear, and others she tempted with promises of fulfilling lives despite persecution by the army. When the higher ups of the regiment caught wind of Esther’s treason, she had already fled. Her encampment was soon overtaken by the Axis forces; allegedly due to a shortage of fighters to hold the front.

Little is known of Esther immediately after fleeing the army. Scattered accounts support her adopting some kind of traveling divinator role, however these are inconsistent in the matters relating to Esther herself. There are, however, two unifying factors: Esther’s prices were high, yet her customers numerous, along with her branding under the Starmother moniker. 

Working as a traveling divinator graced Esther with wealth, that she brought with her on a return to Baraize, which at this time had been abandoned by around 90% of its inhabitants.[[footnote]]Analysts suggest Baraize collapse was due to a combination of factors, however most notably sudden emigration of the capable workforce.[[/footnote]] There she commissioned craftsmen from nearby towns to build a nursing home called “the Observatory.”

In the modern day, sources on the late Esther’s operations within the Observatory remain scarce; as documentation regarding it was lost when Baraize’s town hall reorganized its archives.[[footnote]]This happened due to an economic and urbanist boom that revitalized the fading city shortly after Esther’s return, and the rapid surge of bureaucratic necessity could not be satisfied.[[/footnote]] Therefore, our understanding stems from testimony of the Observatory's current keepers. One such account is given by Bastille Castel, an astrologist and nurse, recorded by a journalist who visited Baraize to learn of its development:

>

>

> 
> But no, the woman knew no tears, she instead smiled at the sight of Gabriel. She told him to look up at the pearly night sky, //Do you not remember the Baraize heaven, young man?// she said, //Do you not notice, how there are more stars than ever?// Gabriel could not understand, but the woman went on. //You left to find your place, but it was here, among the constellations, all along.// She pointed in the direction of the city’s town square, //Go to the Observatory, Starmother ought to put you where you belong//. 
> 
> And so Gabriel drifted between forgotten houses in unseen alleys. An onlooker might have compared him to a specter, the way his solemn steps weaved throughout the once vibrant and lively city. His eyes darted from nostalgic playground to the corpses of memorable cornershops, attempting to save himself from the despair of regret. 
> 
> The Observatory stood in place of the old town square. On the entrance arch it said: //For Failed Adventurers//. Gabriel thought it clever; by setting foot in this place you admit and lay bare your failures, you embrace the shame and embarrassment of having left something beautiful and returned with less. Gabriel gnawed his teeth, swallowed his pride, and entered.
> 
> It was a pitch black lobby, with only a few shy candles making apparent the room's enclosures. They were oriented in similar patterns, recognizable by some as the constellation of Hunter Orion. Despite the darkness, Gabriel could not feel more seen, as from it a feminine figure could be distinguished; and her blue eyes stared right down his exposed soul. //Gabriel?//, she asked. //Welcome back.//
>

>

>

>

> 
> Gabriel had hoped inference would continue to guide him, so the question was startling. It was not however, a hard one, the difficulty simply lied in the utterance of an answer. He could not bring himself to form the words.
> 
> Starmother continued to look through the telescope, then said: //I understand. You are of the many who left the Bosom of Baraize, looking for your place in false stars. Now you understand that such a place is not earned, it is bestowed. Now you’ve come to salvage what little of you the war left unravaged.// Gabriel, frightened by her brazenness, could only shake his head in dismissed protest. //Yes, oh yes… now you have returned, only to find even this innocence corrupted, only to have destroyed the sanctity of your adolescent memories. Now you truly are nothing.// He said nothing. //Admit it.// He nods.
> 
> //You poor thing. Only very, very few can truly chart their own way. Do not worry, I will grant you guidance, I will see your talents, your uniqueness, and I will give you your place in the stars which you have so desired.// Gabriel let her words flow through him, as he looked longingly at the sky. //But if I’m to consult the heavens about you, I must ascertain some things.//
> 
> She began with pure inquiries: //What night were you born? The craft of your parents? Childhood frivolities?// Such questions any astrologer might wonder about. Then came secrets Gabriel wanted not to part with. //Romantic interests? Disagreeable politics? Degenerate hobbies?// He tried to circle around the truth instead of giving it outright, but Starmother was relentless.
> 
> //You must tell me.// Gabriel began looking around and anxiously stepping in place. //Gabriel, look at me. You want salvation, indeed?// He nods. //But you act like you’d rather stay down in the mud of the trenches. This may not be an applicable treatment for you, after all.// At this, Gabriel shook. //Then answer.//
> 
> She asked question after question, leading Gabriel deeper and deeper into shamelessness. The space between the answer and the next inquiry decreased. Eventually, it disappeared. Soon enough, they began talking over one another. Gabriel’s floodgates had been opened. He laid laid bare all embarrassment, he advertised his guilt, he hanged to dry the despicable thoughts, all evil imaginations, sold every personal detail; every moment lust, of wrath, of gluttony, of pride (which he now had none of), of greed, of sloth and of envy. Starmother no longer prompted him with questions. //Yes, show the stars what you truly are!// Every person he had ever wished harm, every desire that had come unfulfilled, every gamble that left him craving more, every crime that escaped punishment, every morsel eaten to survive when the squadron's rations ran out, every defenseless enemy killed, everything admitted and robbed.
> 
> In the end, Gabriel was brought to his knees. He laid in a puddle of his soul, which was no longer simply exposed, but brutalized into an unrecognized porridge on the floor, spread out for the stars to witness and mock. 
> 
> //Good, very good. They now know exactly where you will do most good.// Starmother knelt beside him, and caressed the crying thing. Gabriel, who had been cast into a cold hell, grasped her hand and felt the warmth of heaven. //Follow me.// Once again, she guided him through the dark corridors of the Observatory. 
> 
> They emerged to a wide enclosure with numerous occupied beds organized in parallel. Starmother took him to an empty one, and tapped on it. Gabriel, without even discarding his boots, crawled on. Starmother tucked him in. 
> 
> She closed her eyes, and began to sing a beautiful, quiet rhyme. Despite being in a hall full of people, Gabriel felt the lullaby could only be heard by him. He let it and the mattress surround him, complete consumption. Soon it felt as if he had become one with the bed, and that removing the blanket would be akin to peeling off skin. He began to fear that if he shut his eyes, he may never wake again. But it was too late, all power had been taken, and he could no longer have that terror and uncertainty move him; he was captured by the bed’s safety.
> 
> Starmother put her hand on his face, and closed the lids. The last thing Gabriel’s ears could ever hear was her sweet voice proclaiming: //My child, be projected to the astral grounds. Be accepting of the grace, of your given place in the stars.//”
> 
> Mr. Castel finished his tale, and asked if there was more I wanted to know. I only had one question: How do you know this? 
> 
> At that moment, the attendant returned his gaze upwards and told me: “Well, while not busy blessing the sky of Baraize, he spares some time to talk with me.”
> 
[[/collapsible]]

The Observatory is still operating to this day, despite Esther's death in 1984. The city of Baraize holds an annual festival every year on the day of its founding, dubbed the "Festival of our Blessed Sky".

[[=]]
+ Introduction
[[/=]]

----

Oberst Albrecht Engelbert, known as Feruerpest on the battlefield, was a high-ranking officer of the German Imperial Army during World War 1, and an avid user of pyrokinesis in military strategy. A key part of Engelbert’s offensive was the use of the //Flammenwerfer Infanterie//; a regiment utilizing primarily flamethrowers and specifically trained to induce fear in the enemy, meant to synergize with the Oberst’s abilities.

Engelbert was initially acquired by the [http://rpcauthority.wikidot.com/foa-hub Reichsokkultabteilung] (Imperial Occult Agency, IOA) before serving the army. IOA found Englebert through rumors from the city of Bremerhaven during the sixth cholera pandemic. More specifically, the church accused Engelbert of using "demonic arts” to treat those sick with the disease. In reality, Engelbert was searing wounds and fresh amputees with cauterization.[[footnote]]Burning the body with the intent to stop bleeding or prevent infection.[[/footnote]] Nevertheless, Engelbert was captured as an item of paranormality, and became subject to experimentation. The following is an IOA document retrieved after the Treaty of Versailles, dated to 1913 and concerning Engelbert.


[[collapsible show="[+] IOA document" hide="[-] IOA document" hideLocation="both"]]
> 
> Proposal for utility: Item can promote and direct the force of heat and flame. Testing has demonstrated the capability of extending flamethrower usage duration by some 20 seconds. 
> 
> Additionally, the subject can amplify heat to cause a combustion in objects with a lower energy density. On one occasion, the item produced an inferno of epic proportions. The attending researcher, upon witnessing the blaze, was anomalously set alight. 
> 
> We hypothesize that fearful experience can produce organic processes with a higher energy-expenditure and heat generation, processes which can serve as a point of ignition. Together with the tension of warfare, the item can prove useful in elimination by means of spontaneous combustion.
> 
> This weapon will be named //Feuerpest//. May it irresistibly spread fear and fire in the hearts of our enemies.
>

[[=]]
+ Later Pursuits
[[/=]]

----

Engelbert’s Flammenwerfer Infanterie were deployed to numerous missions during WW1. Their method of attack was deliberately slow, sparing survivors as a vector for demoralization. This became characteristic of Engelbert’s fighting, and explicitly efficient against the French tactic of defence in depth during the Battle of Verdun. Rumors, farces and intimidation were key vectors of demoralization and common on Englebert’s fronts. 

The following is a translated testimony recorded by the French military authorities. It is given by a soldier whom had deserted from the Battle of Verdun defensive line.

[[collapsible show="[+] French soldier interrogation" hide="[-] French soldier interrogation" hideLocation="both"]]
> 
> **A. Marceau**: "Lieutenant Lupin of the second army, you are hereby accused of deserting from Verdun in the midst of an enemy attack. How do you plead?”

Pillars of flame poked out from the enemy, before disappearing then being followed by more. Rations were delivered between the colonies daily by the Captain himself, and reports collected. The battle during that interlude became inexplicably still, and deathly quiet. I regret not making use of that peace. While we stirred anxiously, huddled in our hollows, who knew what preparations occupied the Pest. I’d come to know. Following him were two other compatriots, all running wildly across the battlefield. The Germans did not shoot them. Instead, they stained the blue sky with orange, red and black.

Engelbert has left a lasting impact on the people of France. He is credited to be one of the main contributing factors to anti-psychic sentiment, and the eventual, organized formation of The Academy of True Art.

Despite this, almost nothing is known of him outside of notable military operations. He eluded arrest after the fall of the Central Forces, along with his Flammenwerfer Infanterie. However, certain legends and myths still speak of the Feuerpest, and his army of hell-spawn terrorizing isolated villages and towns across the French countryside, commonly being used to make sense of unexplained arson.
