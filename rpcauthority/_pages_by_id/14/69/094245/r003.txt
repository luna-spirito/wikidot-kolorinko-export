**Registered Phenomena Code:** 706

**Object Class:** --Gamma-Red-- Gamma-Purple

**Hazard Types:** Incorporeal, Visual, Sentient (?), Tychokinetic.

**Containment Protocols:** As of 26/06/2025, Site-113 has been destroyed and decommissioned. RPC-706 is currently uncontained; containment efforts are to be focused on locating and monitoring its last known observer(s). If possible, they are to be transfered to a secluded location, where RPC-706 can remanifest in isolation. Long-term containment protocols are still pending approval; more information has been made available on Addendum 706-03.

[[collapsible show="[+] Archived Containment Protocols " hide="[-] Archived Containment Protocols" hideLocation="top"]]

**Containment Protocols:** RPC-706 is to be contained in a Type-03[[footnote]]Animal Containment.[[/footnote]] Isolated Unit within Sector-γ, Site-113. In the event that RPC-706 breaches containment, immediate efforts must be made to locate its last known observer(s) and transfer them to Sector-γ. Only after a 168 hour period and confirmation of RPC-706's reappearance may on-site containment teams initiate search operations to locate and relocate it back to its containment unit.

**Additional Containment Protocols:** Sector-γ is accessible via Site-113's Administrative Elevator; it is to remain permanently sealed and physically isolated from all other on-site facilities. Sector-γ must be staffed exclusively by Category-5 Visually-Impaired (VIP) Personnel. [[footnote]]Staff members with complete, irreversible blindness.[[/footnote]] If not visually impaired, staff members must undergo two orbital exenteration surgeries prior to entry. All environmental navigation within Sector-γ is to be conducted using tactile markers, guiding rails, sound cues, and signals on braile.

Maintenance and care procedures for RPC-706 must be performed manually, with operational oversight provided by a communication team stationed off-sector. VIP-staff must remain on-site continuously, and are expected to work following a 7-day rotational schedule.[[footnote]]Essential day-to-day items and needs are provided weekly, found within the Staff Block.[[/footnote]] Decontamination is mandatory at the end of each week.

Off-sector duty reintegration, must be requested and aproved by Site Director James H. Fields. The entry and exit to Sector-γ is permitted only under the following conditions:
* Complete decontamination of all personnel and equipment, including airborne particle filtration and the incineration of potentially hazardous material.
* Site Director approval.
* Verification that RPC-706 is still contained.

Failure to implement these protocols will result in a γ-Class Atropos-Disaster.[[footnote]]Regional-wide containment catasthrophe.[[/footnote]]

[[/collapsible]]

------

**Description:** RPC-706 is a cat of the Bombay breed, weighing ~4.2 kg and measuring ~65 cm.[[footnote]]Measurements gathered from various testimonies.[[/footnote]] Its physical attributes are those commonly shared with a healthy member of its species, and it displays typical behaviors and instincts of a domestic cat.

The anomalous effect of RPC-706 triggers once direct visual contact is established between the anomaly and one/many observer(s). Upon breaking line of sight, RPC-706 will disappear instantly and remain absent for 168 hours. [[footnote]]Relative to the observer(s).[[/footnote]] When this period concludes the observer(s) will suffer a fatal incident, typically caused by sudden, accidental and/or unusual circumstances. Following this event, RPC-706 will reappear within a 7 meter radius of the fatality's location.

No known method exists to prevent RPC-706 from disappearing, nor to interrupt the ensuing fatality's timeline. All individuals who observe RPC-706 will, without exception, die.

**Addendum 706-1:** While not exhaustive, the following incidents have been compiled to demonstrate and document the nature of RPC-706. As of 19/01/2025, the full list is incomplete; you may request a full briefing by contacting VIP Lead Researcher and Containment Specialist Dr. Helen E. Hastings.

[[collapsible show="+ [706/01] Notable Incidents " hide="- [706/01] Notable Incidents" hideLocation="top"]]

> **Date:** October 25, 1859.
> ------
> **Victim(s):** 45 year old Victor Saxton, fisherman and sailboat captain.
>
> **Incident details:** During the Great Storm of 1859 off the coast of Anglesey, Wales,[[footnote]]Also known as the Royal Charter storm.[[/footnote]] wind velocities exceeding ~160 km/h (~100 mph) forced multiple vessels ashore at Port Wen. Whilst trying to secure his sailship at the port, Victor was impacted by the bow of one such vessel, fatally crushing him in the spot.
>
> **Notes:** 7 days before the incident, Victor wrote the following entry on his journal: ''About 300 miles off shore, at sunset, I found a cat swimming by itself in the middle of nowhere. And the moment I blinked it vanished. I didn't know cats could make it this far out in the water''. As of now, this is the oldest mention of RPC-706 found so far.

> **Date:** Date: July 6, 1916.
> ------
> **Victim(s):** 27 year old Nikolas Svenz, airplane technician.
>
> **Incident details:** While swimming at a beach north of Spring Lake, New Jersey, Svenz went missing at 03:02 P.M. Three minutes later, his body was found washed up ashore. A subsequent forensic study concluded that he had been decapitated by a marine animal; the bite-marks and teeth imprinted on the body matched that of a //Carcharodon carcharias//.[[footnote]]Great White shark[[/footnote]]
>
> **Notes:** During an interview, Svenz' mother revealed that he had reportedly seen ''a vanishing black cat'' on Switzerland, just a week prior to traveling. As a result, Svenz inadvertenly brought RPC-706 into the American continent, where it has remained ever since.

> **Date:** February 17, 1949
> ------
> **Victim(s):** 48 year old Lieutentant Jesse Simmons, police officer.
>
> **Incident details:** Lt. Simmons had been bedridden since the 10th of February, at the ██ ██████ Hospital, in St. Louis, Missouri. At 06:08 A.M, two nurses were transporting him on a hospital stretcher. Upon entering the hallway elevator, the brakes malfunctioned, and the elevator plunged from the 15th floor into the ground. The impact severely injured both nurses, and killed Lt. Simmons via atlanto-occipital dislocation.[[footnote]]Internal decapitation via separation of the spinal column from the skull base.[[/footnote]]
>
> **Notes:** On February 10, while having breakfast with his wife, Lt. Simmons sighted RPC-706 on his backyard. Later at 12:01 P.M, during an active shooting, he recieved two gunshots on the torso and head respectively. Notably, the bullets pierced his left lung, and significant brain tissue on the left hemisphere. In spite of this, Lt. Simmons remained alive (albeit comatose) up until his expiration date.

> **Date:** March 23, 1959.
> ------
> **Victim(s):** 12 children, ages 9-11; all elementary school students.
>
> **Incident details:** During a tornado outbreak in Marshall County, Oklahoma, an F4 tornado deviated from its projected trajectory and impacted ███ █████████'s Elementary School. Debris generated by the storm obstructed egress points, preventing twelve children from evacuating their classroom. The structure was subsequently compromised when the tornado intersected the building, exposing the occupants to wind velocities exceeding 320 km/h. The children were vertically discplaced by ~400 meters, and their bodies were scattered within a 25km^^2^^ area around the area of impact.
>
> **Notes:** A week prior, the students informed their teacher of an RPC-706 sighting. Although the tornado outbreak claimed the lives of up __>__86 people, it is worth nothing that these were the only casualties confirmed within the school.

> **Date:** September 10, 2010.
> ------
> **Victim(s):** Lieutenant Sarah Wilson, 29 years old; and 13 officers and officials of ACI Unit Delta-02, ages 20-55.
>
> **Incident details:** During a mission briefing, Lieutenant Sarah was giving a speech when RPC-706 unexpectedly entered her tent. The entirety of Unit-02 was exposed to RPC-706 simultaneously via visual contact with the anomaly and the recorded live feed. 7 days later at 20:15 P.M, during a mission on the border region of Kalapata, Tanzania, the unit encountered an innert Mi-24 helicopter gunship stationed in the middle of the dessert. Upon contact, communication was suddenly lost. Two days later, 4 partially-torn appart bodies were identified hanging from [/rpc-666 RPC-666]. The rest of the team has yet to be found.
>
> **Notes:**  This is the earliest verified encounter with RPC-706 by Authority operatives. Notably, when the footage was reviewed, RPC-706 was absent from the recording.

> **Date:** September 10, 2010.
> ------
> **Victim(s):** 3 members of ACI Unit Delta-02, 21, 22, 19 years old respectively.
>
> **Incident details:** All three officers had been sent to ███████, Arizona, 3 days prior to the recovery mission to investigate the previous encounter with RPC-706. At 10:15 A.M during a refuel stop, the squad inadvertenly drove into a gas station that had become an instance of [/rpc-111 RPC-111]. While refueling, Officer Simon G. recieved a cognito-harzardous SMS text-message. Upon replying, the gas station exploded with a force of 3.4 kilotons, vaporizing the remaining members of Unit-02 instantly.
>
> **Notes:** While there were no subsequent sightings in the african regions, RPC-706 was reportedly sighted by the local firemen and police department following the explosion. It is currently up to debate whether RPC-706 can choose its future location or not, in the case that it's sighted by multiple people.

**Addendum 706-2:** Sector-γ operation began on January 1, 2025, to aid in RPC-706 containment. Dr. Helen E. Hastings personally volunteered to partake in recontainment and relocation protocols; after going through a specialized blind-work training, she became the first Sector-γ VIP Staff Member. Once contact was established with RPC-706, immediate containment efforts were made.

**[ CONTAINMENT ATTEMPT - 01 ]**
[[/=]]
On June 12, 2012, MST Foxtrot-4 (''Prey'') succesfully located and isolated RPC-706 into a secluded forest region near St. ███ ██████, 15 km south of the Rocky Mountains. CSD-1822 was sent on a solitary search party, and came into direct contact with RPC-706 shortly after. He was then transferred to Sector-γ, where Dr. Hastings personally interacted with him on a day-to-day basis.  
 
7 days after exposure, an alert originated from Containment Unit 01. A malfunction in the containment chamber had raised the chamber pressure to 13 atm over the following days. At 01:02 P.M, during the scheduled feeding hour, the food compartment was opened by Dr. Hastings. It was then when the chamber instantly decompressed from 13 atm down to the ambient external pressure of 1. CSD-1822 was forced through the 20 cm x 7cm opening, projecting his internal organs into the hallway within ~0.1000 seconds. This explosive decompression also forced Dr. Hastings against the hallway wall, forcefully dislocating her left foot, and breaking two ribs on impact.
 
Following this event, RPC-706's prescence was confirmed in the form of a loud meow, originating from within the chamber. Dr. Hastings, although injured, succesfully relocated RPC-706 to the adjacent containment chamber. Once secured, Dr. Hastings moved to the Medical Wing for emergency treatment and disinfection.
 
When Dr. Hastings returned to the Containment Block, RPC-706 was found absent from its containment chamber.

**Foreword:** Sergeant Paul H. Werner was deployed to Sector-γ after the on-site audio feed captured the distressed scream of Dr. Hastings. They had previously met eachother during briefing on RPC-706's Containment Attempt. Sector-γ's emergency red lights had been activated once RPC-706's absence was confirmed.  The following footage was recorded by Sgt. Werner.
------
**< [19/06/2025 - Begin Log >**

[Dr. Hastings is seen at the entrance of Chamber-01, sitting near the remaining viscera of CSD-1822. Sgt. Werner slowly aproaches Dr. Hastings]

**Sgt. Werner:** Hello Helen.

[Dr. Hastings turns her head to Sgt. Werner's direction. She stops sobbing and takes a deep breath, before releasing a loud, profuse sigh]

**Dr. Hastings:** Paul?

**Sgt. Werner:** Listen, we need to figure out what went wrong. We can't cont-

**Dr. Hastings:** EVERYTHING! - went wrong. It's gone. I had it-

[Dr. Hastings raises both of her hands toward Sgt. Werner]

**Dr. Hastings:** It was right here. Do you know-

[Dr. Hastings stands up, by supporting her weight onto her right foot. She grunts in the process, before resuming.]

**Dr. Hastings:** -you know the fucking sacrifice I put into this?! I gouged my eyes out, killed a man, and for what?!

**Sgt. Werner:** Its not your fault-

**Dr. Hastings:** Do you know- [scoff] no, you know what? You're right, its not my fucking fault!

**Sgt. Werner:** Calm down!

**Dr. Hastings:** And you wanna know how I know? Cats tend to shed a lot of hair. Whoever -

[Dr. Hastings takes a deep breath.]

**Dr. Hastings:** -whoever cleaned me up, whoever's in charge didn't know that.

[Sgt. Werner takes one peek inside both the chamber in which RPC-706 manifested in, and the chamber it was transported to. He then, reaches for his radio.]

**Sgt. Werner:** HQ, this is Handsome. The anomaly is gone, maintenance is clear to proceed. I repeat, maintenance is clear to proceed, over.

**HQ:** Copy that Handsome; sending the maintenance team.

[Dr. Hastings sobs, then sighs briefly before continuing.]

**Dr. Hastings:** I'm willing to bet there isn't a singular hair on the chamber.

**Sgt. Werner:** And it didn't disappear when you put it down, correct?

**Dr. Hastings:** Yeah, it wouldn't let me close the door. I could still hear it purring on my way to Medical.

[Sgt. Werner exhales, before approaching Dr. Hastings.]

**Sgt. Werner:** Okay, here's what were gonna do. We'll review this whole - aftermath. Inch by inch. Are the people in the Medical Wing blind?

[Dr. Hastings nods her head, while humming briefly]

**Dr. Hastings:** Some of them, yeah. I know atleast two of them got them helmets on. They used pressurized water to clean me up.

**Sgt. Werner:** Okay, so we'll start with the water draining system. If anything clung onto their helmets, or the sewage, we'll find out. Then, we'll find whoever triggered the anomaly, and after that we can repeat this operation agai-

**Dr. Hastings:** No, no.

**Sgt. Werner:** We have to try again Helen. We could set up the chamber differently, we could force an accident to ocurr, we could -

**Dr. Hastings:** No, you don't understand. 706 hardly repeats itself. Who's to say it won't cause a snow storm next time? Or an avalanche - fucking - what about an earthquake? God, not even that. It could kill someone with so much as a pencil and a chair. And there's no way to know what it is until it's happened.

**Sgt. Werner:** We don't have many options. Helen - we have to try again.

**Dr. Hastings:** It had the option to go all the way to the Congo, and it chose to stay within the Americas. It made a choice, Paul. Otherwise, it would've - duplicated itself. So who's to say it doesn't have the option to stay free?

**Sgt. Werner:** We don't know that.

**Dr. Hastings:** No. But I'm sure it knows.

**< End Log >**

Researcher Rose L. Smith was briefed and informed about both the anomaly and the recontainment operation. After reluctanctly agreeing on being reassigned as CSD-1923, she was then transferred to Containment Unit 03. Per Dr. Hastings instructions, the entirety of Site-113 (including Sector-γ) was evacuated 1 day prior to the scheduled incident, with the sole exception of CSD-1923.  

On June 26, a daytime fireball was sighted flying over ██████, Georgia; its speed was measured at ~30 km/s. Moments later, Site-113 lost communication. The cause was later determined to be the result of a meteorite impact; the explosion flattened the entirety of Site-113 and incinerated 2.5Wkm^^2^^ of forest. Due to the isolated location of Site-113, cover up efforts were reduced to minimizing the severity of the impact.

Following the event, a firemen squad was notified by the local authorities, in order to contain the forest fire. Contact with RPC-706 was then re-established by Sgt. Oliver B. Díaz, one of the firemen deployed. The remains of CSD-1923 have yet to be recovered.
