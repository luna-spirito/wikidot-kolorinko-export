**Containment Protocols:** OL-Site-651 is to maintain the cover of an oceanic research facility owned by an Authority front. Authority naval vessels equipped with communication jammers are to patrol the perimeter of RPC-651's effective area and intercept any broadcasts originating from within. All non-Authority vessels detected entering the perimeter are to be turned away and escorted from the area. MST Kappa-12-OCEA "Rahab" is to operate specialized electric ROUVs[[footnote]] Remotely Operated Underwater Vehicles. [[/footnote]] within RPC-651's effective area for the purpose of studying RPC-651's behavior, collecting biological samples from RPC-651, and rescuing any survivors in the event of an attack.

All rescue operations within RPC-651's effective area are to be conducted by MST Kappa-12-OCEA using the aforementioned ROUVs. All manned rescue vehicles, Authority or otherwise, are to be turned away. Survivors of RPC-651 attacks are to be immediately issued class A-3 amnestics, and a disinformation campaign is to be established to blame incidents on non-anomalous conditions.

**Description:** RPC-651 is a large organism located beneath the seafloor of the Mexico Basin[[footnote]] The Mexico Basin is the deepest part of the Gulf of Mexico, with an estimated maximum depth between 3,750 and 4,384 m. [[/footnote]] in the Gulf of Mexico. RPC-651 is active in a 100 km radius and is believed to be between 50 and 200 km wide. It is difficult to attain a complete physical description of RPC-651 since its only visible appendages are a set of organs hereafter referred to as its "hooks."

RPC-651's hooks are red, crescent-shaped structures tipped with enamel. Each hook contains a swim bladder, as well as muscle structures likely used for locomotion via the intake and expulsion of water. The hooks are attached to the central mass of RPC-651 via thin muscle tissues, which extend into the seafloor. The exact number of hooks RPC-651 possesses is unknown. However, during attacks on larger vessels, RPC-651 has displayed the capability to extend over 200 hooks simultaneously.

When a ship crosses the perimeter of RPC-651's effective area, it will extend a large hook from beneath the seafloor to the surface to attach itself to the vessel by penetrating its hull. It will then drag the vessel to the seafloor at speeds exceeding 25 kilometers per hour. Smaller hooks may also extend to secure the targeted vessel. When its target reaches the seafloor, more hooks will emerge and drag it under.

RPC-651 will attempt to keep its targets mostly intact when pulling them beneath the seafloor, as it often resurfaces them later[[footnote]] RPC-651 uses its hooks' swim bladders to resurface sunken ships. [[/footnote]] to lure in more potential targets. RPC-651 also appears to display sapience, as it is capable of communicating in English and Spanish while exhibiting its luring behavior.

**Discovery:** In 1860, American sailor and folklorist Barnabas Blake wrote //A Record of the Folklore of American Sailors in the Gulf of Mexico//. One of the book's chapters is dedicated to "Hell Hooks," wherein Blake mentions that several whalers reported whales being dragged away from their pods by "crooked, demonic spears from the deep." In the 1870's, the //San Antonio Express// reported a sharp increase in the frequency at which ships were lost at sea in the Gulf of Mexico. The //Express// also reported accounts from two witnesses who corroborated the "Hell Hook" legend.

RPC-651 was discovered on 11/22/1950 after the sinking of SS //Llewellyn//. //Llewellyn// continued to broadcast unusual Morse code signals for several weeks after she sank, prompting an Authority investigation. This resulted in the sinking of ANV //Mandeville//. //Mandeville//'s last broadcast confirmed the existence of "Hell Hooks" as an anomaly. After the discovery of RPC-651, all copies of //A Record of the Folklore of American Sailors in the Gulf of Mexico// and the relevant //San Antonio Express// issues were confiscated and archived. OL-Site-651 was established on the coast of Yucatan, Mexico to monitor and contain RPC-651.

**Addendum 651-I: Transcript of Notable Distress Call**

[[div class="blockquote"]]

**Interviewed:** NOAAS //Tuscany//

**Interviewer:** ANV //Charybdis//

**Foreword:** On 07/03/1999, NOAAS //Tuscany// bypassed the Authority's blockade of RPC-651's effective area. Within 3 hours, the //Tuscany// was targeted by RPC-651. The following communication was recorded several days after the sinking of //Tuscany//.

**<Begin Log>**

**//Tuscany//:** Mayday! Mayday! This is the NOAAS //Tuscany//! We need a rescue immediately!

**//Charybdis//:** This is Coast Guard Sector, Corpus Christi. What is your position and nature of distress? Over.

**//Tuscany//:** We're around 375 nautical miles southeast of Corpus Christi! We are sinking!

**//Charybdis//:** Alright, requesting the number of persons on board. Over.

**//Tuscany//:** We have...20 persons onboard. We were just looking for oil, and we got a breach in our hull and...and now we're going down!

**//Charybdis//:** 20 persons onboard and-...Wait, you said NOAAS //Tuscany//, right? Over.

**//Tuscany//:** Yes! The NOAA. When do you think our rescue will arrive? We're really taking on water.

**//Charybdis//:** Okay, NOAAS //Tuscany//, 20 people on board. And you said you were doing oil explora- Wait.

**//Tuscany//:** Is there something wrong?

**//Charybdis//:** NOAA is oceanic research. Since when did the NOAA start doing oil exploration?

//Tuscany does not respond for over one minute.//

**//Charybdis//:** //Tuscany//, come in.

**//Tuscany//:** We were doing oceanic research on places in the ocean we could find oil, and we just suddenly got a breach in our-

**//Charybdis//:** Oil exploration is not in the NOAA's purview.

//Tuscany does not respond.//

**//Charybdis//:** I am beginning to suspect you are not telling the truth, //Tuscany//.

//At this point, the distress signal is triangulated to the Tuscany's approximate location. ROUVs detect NOAAS Tuscany at the bottom of the Mexico basin. The Charybdis reports the possible anomaly to the Authority.//

**//Tuscany//:** Please, you need to send a rescue!

**//Charybdis//:** Alright, I just got your location.

**//Tuscany//:** Wonderful! Now send in-

**//Charybdis//:** You're way too deep under the sea.

**//Tuscany//:** I don't care! I am telling the truth! You need to send someone! Quickly!

//At this point, OL-Site-651 gets in contact with the Charybdis and commands her to maintain communication with the Tuscany.//

**//Charybdis//:** Okay, but...why were you looking for //oil//?

**//Tuscany//:** We were looking for whale oil, but there aren't any whales around. There hasn't been for a long time.

**//Charybdis//:** Wait, what are you talking about? Whaling is illegal.

**//Tuscany//:** For the love of God, what does it matter? We're sinking! We're dying, and all you care about is why we're here? Send a rescue, please!

//Screams can be heard in the background of the Tuscany's audio.//

**//Charybdis//:** There's just a lot of things that don't add up here, //Tuscany//. You're from the NOAA, but you're trying to look for oil? And you're whaling, too? Not to mention you're at the bottom of the-

//The screaming on the Tuscany's end grows louder.//

**//Tuscany//:** Please, send it now! Can't you hear we are starving? Sinking! We are sinking!

**//Charybdis//:** The rescue is coming your way, over.

//The screaming abruptly stops.//

**//Tuscany//:** Thank you. That's all we needed.

**//Charybdis//:** Alright, now I need you to stay with me.

//The Tuscany does not respond.//

**//Charybdis//:** //Tuscany//? Come in?

**<End Log>**

**Closing Statement:** After communication terminated, RPC-651 continued to transmit distress signals from NOAAS //Tuscany// for several weeks after interviewing ANV //Charybdis//. On 09/09/1999, all signals from the //Tuscany// ceased.

**Addendum 651-II: Examination of RPC-651's Internal Anatomy**

On 03/11/2027, the Authority submarine ANV //Tethys// was scheduled for decommissioning. Researchers requested GD-OCEA to use the decommissioning of the //Tethys// as an opportunity to research RPC-651. GD-OCEA approved the request.

[[div class="blockquote"]]

**Foreword:** ANV //Tethys// was fitted with an unmanned control system and several pieces of recording and detection equipment in preparation to be sent into RPC-651's effective area. The //Tethys// began collecting data on 03/24/2027, when she reached RPC-651's effective area.

**<Begin Log, -400m>**

**-450 m:** Camera systems on the //Tethys// detect multiple hooks rapidly approaching.

[[include component:image-block | name=hooksalt1.png |align=center | caption=-470 m]]

**-500 m:** The largest of RPC-651's hooks penetrates the hull of the //Tethys// and begins dragging her down.

**-850 m:** The //Tethys// approaches crushing depth and begins to implode. More hooks latch onto the //Tethys//, accelerating her descent.

**-1050 m:** RPC-651 begins using the //Tethys// to broadcast distress signals to the Authority.
> Crushing depth reached. Hull breach detected. Send a rescue immediately.

**-3000 m:** RPC-651 continues broadcasting distress signals.
> Hull breach detected. Send a recovery crew immediately.

**-4300 m:** The //Tethys// reaches the seafloor. Sediment on the seafloor is displaced beneath the //Tethys//, pulling her underground.

**-4500 m:** The //Tethys// descends through several large spaces which appear to contain elaborate stone constructs.

[[include component:image-block | name=hooksalt2alt.png |align=center | caption=-5400 m]]

**-7050 m:** The //Tethys// descends into an open space. Her cameras do not detect anything for the next several hundred meters.

**-8100 m:** The //Tethys// is pulled through a fleshy surface by RPC-651's hooks and enters what appears to be the interior of RPC-651. The hooks dislodge from RPC-651's hull and allow her to sink freely. The //Tethys//'s attached hydrophone picks up vocalizations similar to whale calls.

**-8500 m:** The //Tethys// sinks into a mass of carcasses belonging to various marine mammals. Several of the carcasses belong to long-extinct and/or unknown species. Regardless, most of the carcasses appear to have expired recently, and some appear to move. All of them appear to have been stripped of skin and blubber. Among the carcasses are several ships from varying time periods. All ships appear to have sustained severe damage to the bottoms of their hulls.[[footnote]] Especially where fuel tanks are located. [[/footnote]]

[[include component:image-block | name=hooksalt3alt.png |align=center | caption=-8900 m]]

**-9000 m:** RPC-651 uses the //Tethys// to transmit a message.
> OL-Site-651. This is the ANS //Tethys//. Requesting a rescue. Over.

**-9100 m:** The last camera aboard the //Tethys// ceases functioning.

**<End Log>**

**Incident Log 651-I:** RPC-651 began attacking ships outside of its effective area in 2027. These incidents were initially assumed to be exceptions, as RPC-651's hooks are capable of independent locomotion. However, the frequency of these incidents has increased in the past 3 years. As of 05/12/2030, the Authority has detected 21 RPC-651 attacks outside of what was previously thought to be the boundaries of its effective area, 3 of which involved Authority naval vessels. At the time of writing, RPC-651's effective area is estimated to have increased by █%.

[[collapsible show="[+] Document 651-A" hide="[-] Document 651-A"]]

The following document was discovered by Authority agents embedded in one of the oldest known Cetace's Kin pods, which is currently operating under the cover of an environmentalist non-profit with special focus on the conservation of sea life. It is a letter dated 05/01/1888 and is believed to discuss RPC-651.

[[div class="blockquote"]]

Dear Jacobus,

I hope this message finds you well. I write to inform you of a most shocking, yet joyous turn of events. I had gathered this pod in Galveston hoping to combat the grievous efforts of the Continentals. For the past thirty years, I have witnessed their relentless slaughter of those made in Cetace's sacred image. It appears now, however, that we have found a most unlikely ally.

I am sure you are familiar with the Primordial Angler, who destroyed in his eternal gluttony the holy sanctuaries built by Cetace himself. For thirty years, I have monitored and rebuked him. However, when I last sailed out to perform the Rites of Cetace over the Angler's wicked waters, something strange occurred. From the depths emerged one of the Angler's grotesque tendrils, and impaled on its tip was Cetace's image, stripped of all skin. I had expected to meet my end right there and then, but the Angler did not pull me down. Rather, he spoke to me through the image of Cetace.

I remember his words as clear as day. He begged me to restore Cetace's grace to his domain so that he may once again feast upon the oils of the blubber. I told him of the wickedness of the Continentals and their whaling ships, which are full of whales and oil. He thanked me and told me to return in peace to my pod. Later on, I had read in the local paper that he has begun attacking Continental vessels.

Therefore, I am writing to ask you to reconsider our stance on the Primordial Angler. He does not seem to be a threat to Cetace any longer, and his actions are currently benefiting our interests. I understand how this may read as heresy, but I pray you consider my petition seriously.

[[>]]

Your friend and loyal kinsman,
Barnabas
