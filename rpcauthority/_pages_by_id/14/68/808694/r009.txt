**Censorship under Veil expansionism**
[[/div]]


[[span class="genre"]] Opinion [[/span]]
+ **Will more things be censored after the push for Veil expansionism?**
__From Jacob Cardigan__

[[include component:image-block name=http://rpcsandbox.wdfiles.com/local--files/wwwj-articler/moreveil.png
|width= 100%
|align= center
|caption= The Expansion of the Veil
]]


Since the [http://rpcauthority.wikidot.com/fail-safe 2019 Nevada Incident] and the Coronavirus pandemic, there has been a unanimous push from the world authorities in the forum to expand the definition of the veil. The UNAAC Digital Protection Act of 2024 will give world authorities expanded powers to suppress and erase information within unveiled networks.

The legislation is being justified as a safeguard against the spread of information about the unveiled world; however, it would also authorize unprecedented surveillance across global populations. Critics warn that its broad language allows authorities to silence dissents and permanently delete records under the guise of public protection.

This legislation will especially affect gray zones such as indigenous societies in Asia and Sufi orders in the Middle East, as religious immunity will gradually lose its protective function. These communities will be forced to either fully immerse themselves in the unveiled world or abandon it entirely in favor of structured ignorance.

Meanwhile, scientists in universities whose work inadvertently reopens avenues into restricted or suppressed fields may find their publications quietly altered, reclassified, or removed by authorities. This will likely lead to fatal consequences as scientific disinformation is being encouraged.

What does this say about the future? The arbitrary line we have placed between what should be in the light and what should be kept in the darkness was, at one point in history, a mere thought experiment. The result of this split of worlds has led to atrocities against humankind. The protection of monsters that are better left dead, and rumors of human experimentation from the very government meant to uphold human rights and dignity. 

There are numerous cases in which the discovery of new animal species, breakthroughs in medicine, and life-changing technological advances are gatekept from the public due to their suspected association with anomalous sciences. Without a substantial number of the population to protest against them, this status quo will continue. The consequences of mentioning or stumbling across the hidden dark world are now becoming heavier. And what we have once considered to be a mundane part of everyday life may be censored the next day, never to be seen by the light ever again.

+ **Young Russian sorceress wins the Fulwell Awards at the Cornwall Institute**
__From Francis Doyle__

The Cornwall Institute is an ancient educational institution that has fostered strange and extraordinary individuals since 1786. Students and lecturers from across the world gather there to study the thaumaturgic arts, disciplines long forgotten in the modern age. Britain and its Royal Congregation of Sorcerers have made great efforts to preserve these fading arcane traditions, leading to the establishment of what many would call “magic schools,” such as Eccleston University and the Cornwall Institute.

The Fulwell Awards is a traditional annual ceremony hosted by the Cornwall Institute, held continuously since the 17th century. Named after the thaumaturgist Hanserd Fulwell, the award is granted to students who demonstrate exceptional skill in the thaumacrafts, typically expressed through the arts or inventive work.

[[div class="blockquote" style="display: inline-block; width:40%; float: right; margin: 0px 15px 0px; 10px"]]
++++ **On the Silver Ghost**
------
[[f>image http://rpcsandbox.wdfiles.com/local--files/wwwj-articler/SophiaB.png style="width: 120px;"]]
//[http://rpcauthority.wikidot.com/no-0288 Ghostly aristocrats] are not uncommon in the United Kingdom// - in fact, the UK government tends to employ the ghosts of deceased nobles. Magicians, it seems, inevitably encounter remnants of the supernatural from ages past, echoes of an era of magic that our modern age looks back on as nothing but a fairytale.

After her untimely death in the 18th century, Sophia's spirit and likeness were somehow imprinted on a silver mirror. She works for the government and lives with Yushatyr, preferring quiet and private life alongside her ward as opposed to the squalor common in her line of work.
[[/div]]

The winner of this year's Fulwell Awards is '[http://rpcauthority.wikidot.com/monarch-security-personnel#yushatyr Yushatyr]' from Russia. She was once a part of the Russian Ministry of Paranormality's federal thaumaturge sphere, a job she inherited from her parents by age thirteen. 

Her and her family's particular brand of thaumacraft revolves around Sakha silversmithing, an art that is said to engrave magical memory into silver surfaces. This has made her family a particularly important asset within the Russian Federation.

Her time under the Federation came to an abrupt end when her father’s involvement in the ongoing Russo-Ukrainian conflict prompted the Russian government to send her away, resulting in her immigration to the United Kingdom in 2018. There, she immediately caught the interest of Monarch Security's Royal Congregation of Sorcerers, where she was given tuition to further her education in the thaumaturgic arts. 

When she came of age, she was enlisted by the Royal Congregation of Sorcerers, where she was given tuition to further her education in the thaumaturgic arts. With her parents away, she was under the wardship of an aristocratic ghost from the 17th century, a spectral being of pure silver, if you would believe it. 

With the help of her guardian, she built a silver artifact called the "Watcher’s Lantern", which has a candlestick with a special flame that flares, leans, or dims to indicate the direction and proximity of monsters. Such an invention earned her the Fulwell Award from the Cornwall Institute. 

The achievement was attributed not only to her own skill and ingenuity but also to the mentorship of her guardian and the enduring influence of her heritage.

+ **A new era of occult detectives? An interview with Detective Hermes Magnus**
__From Jeremy Kovack__

The role of the detective has changed drastically in the modern, unveiled world. Once romanticized as trench-coated investigators stalking dim alleyways and candlelit ruins in search of monsters and curses, many occult detectives now spend their days tracing encrypted chatrooms, investigating digital hauntings, and navigating increasingly restrictive government oversight.

Few know this better than Hermes Magnus, a veteran private investigator with over three decades of experience dealing with anomalous disappearances, rogue thaumaturges, and hauntings. Hermes remains one of the few independent detectives still operating openly within unveiled circles.

We met in a dim riverside café in London under heavy rain. Hermes arrived late, carrying a cigarette case made of black iron and a leather folder filled with handwritten notes.

[[div class="blockquote" style="font-size: 13px"]]
**Jeremy Kovack**
What troubles occult detectives today?


**Hermes Magnus** 
The nature of anomalies today is different. More and more surveillance is being controlled by those [world authorities] up top, and it is becoming harder for private investigators in this [unveiled] spheres to do their jobs without the immediate intervention of the authorities.

[...] This doesn’t just go for the UK. The US, China, India, and even Afghanistan suffer from this. We are living in a digital age, as you would know. Information isn't stored in stacks of paper anymore; you have to condense everything into computer networks. [...] Just as monsters once roamed our forest at night, so too do they now stalk through these new dark woods. This is a new playing ground for these beings. And this is the role many occult detectives appear to have taken now: online investigators.

**Jeremy Kovack** 
Do on-the-field private investigators still exist then?

**Hermes Magnus** 
Of course, they do. The reason people like us exist is that if you call the government for help, they’ll seize any assets they deem useful or dangerous. Artifacts, documents, properties, sometimes even people. [...] At the end of the day, their job is to protect and control. For-hire investigators like me only do what we’re told to do. For a price, of course.

**Jeremy Kovack** 
How do you deal with the authorities then?

**Hermes Magnus** 
By being careful. Do things by the book, never step over the law. At least, don’t do it in a way that can lead to disastrous consequences. When in Rome, do as the Romans do. 

I myself come from a long line of thaumaturge detectives. It’s the reason why I have a name like ‘Hermes Magnus’: it’s a stage name that ensures not even spirits could track me. You always have to be careful.

**Jeremy Kovack** 
Do you have any detective stories you could share with us?

**Hermes Magnus** 
Not much I can legally divulge. You know the nature of my job.

**Jeremy Kovack** 
Of course.

**Hermes Magnus** 
Hm... There is one story.

**Jeremy Kovack** 
Do tell.

**Hermes Magnus** 
[...] Back in 1994, on the western Irish coast. There used to be rumours of a monster called the Bánánach that came for children who had lost their names, and devoured them whole. The rumour had persisted for around two years by that point, and nobody could prove such a thing existed.

I took on the investigation after MI13 and the New Dawn Initiative had already given up. There wasn’t any evidence of children going missing. But of course there wasn’t. Those children who vanished never had names to begin with. They weren’t written in any records, as if they had never existed at all. //But they did exist.//

**Jeremy Kovack** 
How could you tell?

**Hermes Magnus** 
I found evidence of them. Clothes left on the ground. School bags filled with freshly bought supplies. School classes with empty seats nobody seemed capable of acknowledging for too long. Finding the cause of these disappearances was... an adventure, to say the least. After months of trailing, I finally found it: //the Bánánach.//

[...] Sorry. I need a [break].

**Jeremy Kovack** 
Of course. 

//Hermes Magnus takes a 2-minute break to smoke a cigarette.//

**Hermes Magnus** 
The Bánánach was... not what I expected. It was a woman. Or rather, she used to be one. Before she became drunk off her own magic like a bacchanal, drinking herself to death over the loss of her two children. Their names were forgotten, too, unfortunately. It was as if they had never existed; they became part of an //antimeme//.

She wandered the country in search of them, and those she took away became infected by her spell. Their records would unravel, turning memories into lies. It was a kind of nontropic effect that made people unconsciously ignore it, like how the brain filters out the sight of your own nose. Except instead of a nose it was [...] people. That was the frightening thing, that reality itself was so eager to help her.

**Jeremy Kovack**
What happened in the end?

**Hermes Magnus** 
My associates and I hunted it down. And we killed her. It was the only way we could verify she stayed dead. If we kept her alive, eventually we wouldn’t know she was even there anymore. That’s the nature of antimemes. They try their best to censor themselves from humans and the memories we hold dear. Our brains refuse to acknowledge their existence; it's total and complete invisibility. 
[[/div]]

Hermes remained silent after that. Rain tapped softly against the café windows while somewhere beyond the fogged glass, London traffic moved like blurred lanterns through the night.

When asked whether occult detectives still had a place in the modern unveiled world, Hermes only laughed quietly before lighting another cigarette.
