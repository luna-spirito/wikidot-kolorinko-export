[[collapsible show="Archived Containment Protocols (1984-2007)" hide="-----"]]
>
> Under the 1984 Free Fellow Act, the Authority, the UNAAC, USOC, and unveiled actors in the Polynesian regions have contended that containment and informational suppression of RPC-796 are unnecessary for its most immediate victims. While it sacrifices veil integrity, the decision is deemed to be the best currently available method of minimizing RPC-796 related fatalities. This is as RPC-796 activity stimulates urban legends and cautionary tales regarding itself, ultimately diverting sea traffic from its zone of manifestation. This both averts Polynesian cultures from placing themselves at risk, and globally cements RPC-796 as a mythological artifact of old.
>
[[/collapsible]]

**Containment Protocols:** Once a year, GD-OCEA "Silent Surfer" must be present on the open oceans of the Polynesian Triangle, board RPC-796, and prevent it from sinking. GD-OCEA sails with a consort of MST Alpha-1-OCEA “Davy Jones” every 22nd of November. This will cause RPC-796 to appear in the vicinity of MST Alpha-1-OCEA’s vessel, allowing GD-OCEA to enact a specialized protocol which has proven to deactivate the anomaly until the 22/11 of the following year. This has rendered RPC-796 virtually harmless. A research team is currently attempting to produce an individual capable of inheriting the containment role.

[[include component:image-block name=http://rpcsandbox.wikidot.com/local--files/floating-with-myself/LNG%20Free%20Fellow.jpg|caption=The LNG Free Fellow 1998, the southern pacific ocean (photo Louis Micha).]]

**Description:**  RPC-796 is a seafaring vessel occasionally manifesting in Polynesian waters. It is morphologically dynamic; adopting its appearance from the available ships at its time of manifestation.

Historical documentation has revealed it to have appeared as (among other types): A cog in the 11th century, a carrack in the 14th, a galleon in the 16th, and a steamboat in the 19th. The earliest reference is an illustration on a clay shard found in modern day Tonga and dated to 1200 BC. It shows a prominent, broken waka canoe piloted by the mythological figure Maui, surrounded by smaller, sinking vessels.

Since painting the hull with the ship’s name became a popular practice during the 19th century, RPC-796 has appeared with the moniker of //"Free Fellow"//. RPC-796 has, since 1993, manifested as a liquefied natural gas-powered cruise ship, the //LNG Free Fellow//.

Before GD-OCEA began suppressing RPC-796, it would sporadically appear in the vicinity of another vessel. RPC-796 would remain stationary while releasing a distress signal. The //LNG Free Fellow// would fire a flare and send radio communication to the target vessel consisting of the following repeating phrase:

>
> IF A FELLOW SINKS - MAY THE TRAITORS DROWN
>

Surviving witnesses describe how RPC-796’s hull would begin to continuously degenerate, its funnel collapse, and other destructive patterns. This is despite the lack of obvious threats or dangers.

Simultaneously, a tychokinetic effect would influence the target vessel. This effect consists of a series of structural, navigational and/or mechanical failures invariably resulting in the ship sinking.

[[include component:image-block name=http://rpcsandbox.wikidot.com/local--files/floating-with-myself/Screenshot%202026-01-21%20141752.jpg|caption=Confirmed locations of RPC-796 manifestation (1999).]]

Early operations attempted to heed RPC-796’s distress call, however these proved futile when a spatial anomaly prohibiting access to the vessel was discovered. Upon entering a 500 meter radius around RPC-796, it will become impossible to progress further, as any motion towards the anomaly will be ineffective in closing the distance.[[footnote]]MST Alpha-1-OCEA members tasked with escorting GD-OCEA describe this effect as “Running on ice, but with a boat.”[[/footnote]]

GD-OCEA, Silent Surfer, is discriminated against by RPC-796. Since 22/11/2000,[[footnote]]Silent Surfer was recruited in 2007.[[/footnote]] GD-OCEA has yearly accessed and stopped RPC-796 from sinking, saving a great number of lives and allowing the anomaly to be taken outside the veiled world. The details of GD-OCEA’s protocol are limited to himself, MST Alpha-1-OCEA, and relevant researchers.

> **Cultural Significance of RPC-796; a Rationalization on the Effects of Veil Penetration.**
> - //Dr. Mila Phora, Department of Anthropology//
> __Chapter 1: Overview of historic views__
> 
> Indeed, RPC-796 presents a profound cultural influence on various Polynesian peoples, and is crucial in considering spiritual developments. The Free Fellow's unreachability, unpredictable manifestation, general mystery, and most importantly of all: sure doom; lend to an easy association with Eidolism. Therefore, common views tend to be folkloric, thanatological, and occasionally eschatological. Additionally, RPC-796 has often led to the manifestation of eccentric laws or regulations regarding seafaring claiming to deter (or in rare cases attract) RPC-796.
> 
> The Samoan people, and their Rotuman descendants, warned of //atua// (a spirit, soul or dead person) piloting RPC-796, who would target and drown individuals before converting them onto the vessel. A method of repelling evil was sailing in fleets, which would establish a domain over the ocean and strip the monsters of their control.
> 
> Maori cultures, however, believed RPC-796 to be a psychopomp guiding the dead towards //Rarohenga// (the Maori underworld-afterlife). In order not to be mistaken for a spirit, one had to wear the //Tā Moko// (skin tattoo representing reverence and divine wisdom). People who set sail without their tattoos would be taken down while still alive and doomed to be time-bound creatures in a time-free place: eternally aging until even the soul wilts to nothingness.
> 
> Tongans attributed little to RPC-796 itself, but mostly to its waters. Oceans where the stars are crooked and directions bend with them, where the sun both moves and stands still. The only person who could dare to sail these waters is //Maui//, the indomitable folk-hero. Even his boat, RPC-796, would sink, marking a certain demise for anyone of a lesser aptitude.
> 
> While these views are historical and have largely faded away, certain elements have survived to this day, such as superstitions and fears regarding seafaring. Nevertheless, contemporary RPC-796 mythological material usually stems from Nihil-adjacent[[footnote]]The Children of Nihil is a diverse term to describe various faiths primarily unified under similar Gnostic belief. Core groups are aware of the paranatural, while adjacent are veiled.[[/footnote]] groups. These groups interestingly bear a positive perspective on RPC-796. One cult found in the Northern Mariana Islands attempted to recruit members for an RPC-796 finding mission by distributing a pamphlet with the following text:

> **Take us to lands foreign, and lands anew, you free boat on that Mariana blue.**

> The recruitment campaign failed due to the pamphlet lacking information for meeting places or times. More on contemporary RPC-796 influences can be found in chapter 6.

I was spewed out of the interstice, just where I had entered, and could only hope the same for the rest of me.
